The Coordinate Plane · Problems 1–10 · 10-22Alex
1.(Inv. 3)
Evaluate Graph these three points: (2, 4), (0, 2), and (−3, −1). Then draw a line that passes through these points. Name a point in the second quadrant that is on the line.
Answer
\((-1,\ 1)\ \text{(answers may vary)}\)
Solution
The three points line up on the line \(y = x + 2\) (each point's \(y\)-coordinate is 2 more than its \(x\)-coordinate). Extending the line into the second quadrant (negative \(x\), positive \(y\)), it passes through \((-1, 1)\) — check: \(1 = -1 + 2\). Any point on the line with \(x < 0\) and \(y > 0\) is a correct answer.
2.(Inv. 3)
One vertex of a square is the origin. Two other vertices are located at (−2, 0) and (0, −2). What are the coordinates of the fourth vertex?
Answer
\((-2,\ -2)\)
Solution
The given vertices are 2 units left of the origin and 2 units below it, so the square fills the corner between them. The fourth vertex is 2 units left and 2 units down: \((-2, -2)\).
3.(Inv. 3)
Find the perimeter and area of a rectangle whose vertices are located at (3, −1), (−2, −1), (−2, −4), and (3, −4).
Answer
\(P = 16\text{ units};\qquad A = 15\text{ square units}\)
Solution
Length: from \(x = -2\) to \(x = 3\) is \(3 - (-2) = 5\) units. Width: from \(y = -4\) to \(y = -1\) is \(-1 - (-4) = 3\) units. Perimeter: \(2(5 + 3) = 16\) units. Area: \(5 \times 3 = 15\) square units.
4.(Inv. 3)
Points (4, 4), (4, 0), and (0, 0) are the vertices of a triangle. The triangle encloses whole squares and half squares on the grid. Determine the area of the triangle by counting the whole squares and the half squares. (Count two half squares as one square unit.)
Answer
\(8\text{ square units}\)
Solution
Counting on the grid: the triangle completely covers 6 whole squares, and the diagonal side cuts 4 squares in half, making 4 half squares \(= 2\) whole squares. Total: \(6 + 2 = 8\) square units. (Check with the formula: \(\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 4 = 8\).)
5.(Inv. 3)
Represent Draw a ray from the origin through the point (10, 10). Draw another ray from the origin through the point (10, 0). Then use a protractor to measure the angle.
Answer
\(45^\circ\)
Solution
The ray through (10, 0) runs along the positive \(x\)-axis. The ray through (10, 10) rises at a 45° slant (it climbs 1 unit for every unit it runs). A protractor at the origin measures the angle between them as \(45^\circ\).
6.(Inv. 3)
Name the quadrant that contains each of these points:
a. (−15, −20)   b. (12, 1)   c. (20, −20)   d. (−3, 5)
Answer
\(\text{a. III}\qquad\text{b. I}\qquad\text{c. IV}\qquad\text{d. II}\)
Solution
Use the signs of the coordinates: a. (−, −) → Quadrant III. b. (+, +) → Quadrant I. c. (+, −) → Quadrant IV. d. (−, +) → Quadrant II.
7.(Inv. 3)
Draw \(\triangle ABC\) with vertices at \(A\) (0, 0), \(B\) (8, −8), and \(C\) (−8, −8). Use a protractor to find the measure of each angle of the triangle.
Answer
\(m\angle A = 90^\circ;\qquad m\angle B = 45^\circ;\qquad m\angle C = 45^\circ\)
Solution
Sides \(AB\) and \(AC\) are equal (both run 8 right/left and 8 down), so the triangle is isosceles with the equal sides meeting at \(A\). The sides from \(A\) slant down at 45° on both sides, making a right angle at \(A\) (90°). The other two angles split the remaining 90° equally: \(45^\circ\) each. \(90 + 45 + 45 = 180^\circ\) ✓.
8.(Inv. 3)
Shae wrote these directions for a dot-to-dot drawing. To complete the drawing, draw segments from point to point in the order given.
1. (0, 4)   2. (−3, −4)   3. (5, 1)
4. (−5, 1)   5. (3, −4)   6. (0, 4)
Answer
\(\text{A five-pointed star (see figure)}\)
Solution
Plot each point and connect them in order: (0, 4) → (−3, −4) → (5, 1) → (−5, 1) → (3, −4) → back to (0, 4). The crossing segments form a five-pointed star.
