Lesson Practice

Problem a
Draw a square and an isosceles triangle. Draw all the lines of symmetry for each shape, if any exist.
Answer & Visual:
Explanation:

A square has exactly 4 lines of symmetry (one horizontal, one vertical, and two diagonal). An isosceles triangle has exactly 1 line of symmetry right down the middle, splitting the two equal sides.

Problem b
All but one of these letters can be drawn to have a line of symmetry. Which of these letters does not have a line of symmetry?

A   C   F   T   U   Y
Answer:

F

Explanation:

A, T, U, and Y have a vertical line of symmetry. C has a horizontal line of symmetry. The letter F cannot be folded over any line to match perfectly.

Problem c
Which four of these letters have rotational symmetry? (Hint: Rotating your paper upside down might help.)

I   N   P   S   W   Z
Answer:

I, N, S, and Z

Explanation:

If you rotate the letters I, N, S, and Z by 180 degrees (upside down), they look identical to their original orientation. The others (P, W) will not map onto themselves (W becomes an M, P becomes a lowercase d).

Written Practice

Problem 1
When the greatest three-digit number is divided by the greatest single-digit number, what is the quotient?
Answer:

111

Explanation:

The greatest three-digit number is 999. The greatest single-digit number is 9.
$$999 \div 9 = 111$$

Problem 2
The ratio of boys to girls in a band is 4 to 3. If there are 24 boys in the band, how many girls are there?
Answer:

18 girls

Explanation:

Set up a proportion: $$\frac{\text{Boys}}{\text{Girls}} = \frac{4}{3} = \frac{24}{G}$$ Find the scale factor: $4 \times 6 = 24$, so $3 \times 6 = 18$.

Problem 3
A rectangular prism has a length of 3 inches, a width of 6 inches, and a height of 8 inches. What is the volume of the prism?
Answer:

144 cubic inches

Explanation:

The volume of a rectangular prism is length times width times height.
$$V = 3 \times 6 \times 8 = 18 \times 8 = 144 \text{ in}^3$$

Problem 4
A full turn is 360 degrees. How many degrees is $\frac{1}{3}$ of a turn?
Answer:

120 degrees

Explanation:

Multiply the full turn by the fraction: $$360^\circ \times \frac{1}{3} = 120^\circ$$

Problem 5
Does a regular octagon have rotational and line symmetry? Use words to explain how you know.
Answer:

Yes, it has both.

Explanation:

Line Symmetry: A regular octagon has 8 lines of symmetry (four connecting opposite vertices, and four connecting the midpoints of opposite sides).
Rotational Symmetry: Because all 8 sides and angles are equal, rotating the octagon by 45 degrees ($360 \div 8$) around its center perfectly maps it onto its original position.

Problem 6
Sketch a rectangle similar to a 5 cm by 8 cm rectangle. Make the scale factor from the original rectangle to your sketch 2. What is the perimeter of the rectangle you sketched?
Answer:

52 cm

Explanation:

The original rectangle has dimensions 5 cm and 8 cm. With a scale factor of 2, the new side lengths are $5 \times 2 = 10 \text{ cm}$ and $8 \times 2 = 16 \text{ cm}$.
The perimeter of the new rectangle is $10 + 16 + 10 + 16 = 52 \text{ cm}$.

Problem 7
What is the area of a right triangle with legs of 5 cm and 12 cm, and a hypotenuse of 13 cm?
Answer:

30 cm²

Explanation:

The area of a right triangle is calculated using the two legs as the base and height (the hypotenuse is not needed for area).
$$Area = \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 12 = \frac{1}{2} \times 60 = 30 \text{ cm}^2$$

Problem 8
Draw a ratio box for this problem. Then solve the problem using a proportion.

A bookstore has 120 books on a display shelf. If 30% of the books are fiction and the rest are non-fiction, how many non-fiction books are on the shelf?
Answer:

84 non-fiction books

Explanation:

If 30% are fiction, then 70% are non-fiction (100% - 30%). Set up a proportion comparing the non-fiction percentage to the total: $$\frac{\text{Percent}}{\text{Total}} \Rightarrow \frac{70}{100} = \frac{x}{120}$$ Cross-multiplying gives: $100x = 8400$, so $x = 84$.
There are 84 non-fiction books.

Problem 9
Convert the fraction $2 \frac{1}{4}$ to a decimal (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. 2.25    b. 225%

Explanation:

$\frac{1}{4}$ is equivalent to 0.25, so $2 \frac{1}{4} = 2.25$. To find the percent, multiply the decimal by 100 to get 225%.

Problem 10
Convert the decimal 4.5 to a mixed fraction (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. $4 \frac{1}{2}$    b. 450%

Explanation:

4.5 is "four and five-tenths" ($4 \frac{5}{10}$), which simplifies to $4 \frac{1}{2}$ by dividing numerator and denominator by 5. Multiply 4.5 by 100 to find the percent: 450%.

Problem 11
Convert 65% to a fraction (a) and a decimal (b).
Answer:

a. $\frac{13}{20}$    b. 0.65

Explanation:

65% as a decimal is 0.65. As a fraction, it is $\frac{65}{100}$, which reduces by dividing numerator and denominator by 5 to get $\frac{13}{20}$.

Problem 12
$$3 \frac{1}{4} + 1 \frac{1}{2} + 2 \frac{3}{8}$$
Answer:

$7 \frac{1}{8}$

Explanation:

Find a common denominator for the fractions, which is 8: $$3 \frac{2}{8} + 1 \frac{4}{8} + 2 \frac{3}{8}$$ Add the whole numbers: $3 + 1 + 2 = 6$.
Add the fractions: $\frac{2}{8} + \frac{4}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{9}{8}$.
Since $\frac{9}{8} = 1 \frac{1}{8}$, add it to the whole number:
Total: $6 + 1 \frac{1}{8} = 7 \frac{1}{8}$.

Problem 13
$$\left(5 \frac{1}{3} \div 2\right) \div 1 \frac{1}{3}$$
Answer:

2

Explanation:

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions: $$\left(\frac{16}{3} \div \frac{2}{1}\right) \div \frac{4}{3}$$ Follow PEMDAS, evaluate parentheses by multiplying by the reciprocal: $$\left(\frac{16}{3} \times \frac{1}{2}\right) \div \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{3} \div \frac{4}{3}$$ Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction: $$\frac{8}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{24}{12} = 2$$

Problem 14
$$30 - (4.6 + 5.4)$$
Answer:

20

Explanation:

First, add the numbers in parentheses: $4.6 + 5.4 = 10.0$.
Subtract from 30: $30.0 - 10.0 = 20$.

Problem 17
Round the quotient to the nearest hundredth: $$0.236 \div 4$$
Answer:

0.06

Explanation:

Divide: $$0.236 \div 4 = 0.059$$ The hundredths place is 5. The next digit is 9, which is 5 or greater, so round the 5 up to 6. The rounded answer is 0.06.

Problem 18
Write the prime factorization of 360 using exponents.
Answer:

$2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$

Explanation:

Break down 360: $360 = 36 \times 10$.
$36 = 6 \times 6 = (2 \times 3) \times (2 \times 3) = 2^2 \times 3^2$.
$10 = 2 \times 5$.
Combine them in numerical order: $(2^2 \times 2) \times 3^2 \times 5 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$.

Problem 19
"All squares are similar." True or false?
Answer:

True

Explanation:

All squares have four equal right angles of 90 degrees. Because their corresponding angles are identical and all four sides are always proportional to each other, they are universally similar shapes.

Problem 20
$$5^2 - 3^2 \times 2 + 10 \div 2$$
Answer:

12

Explanation:

Follow the order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS):
Exponents first: $25 - 9 \times 2 + 10 \div 2$
Multiplication and Division (left to right): $25 - 18 + 5$
Addition and Subtraction (left to right): $7 + 5 = 12$.

Problem 21
What is the perimeter of this polygon? (An L-shaped polygon with top horizontal edge = 10 cm, left vertical edge = 8 cm, right vertical edge = 3 cm, and bottom-inner horizontal edge = 6 cm).
Answer:

36 cm

Explanation:

To find the perimeter, add all the outer boundaries.
Top edge = 10 cm
Left edge = 8 cm
Right edge = 3 cm
Bottom-inner horizontal edge = 6 cm
The missing bottom horizontal segment is $10 \text{ cm} - 6 \text{ cm} = 4 \text{ cm}$.
The missing inner vertical "step-down" segment is $8 \text{ cm} - 3 \text{ cm} = 5 \text{ cm}$.
$$Perimeter = 10 + 8 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 3 = 36 \text{ cm}$$

Problem 22
What is the area of the polygon from problem 21?
Answer:

50 cm²

Explanation:

Split the polygon into two rectangles.
Rectangle 1 (left side column): Width = 4 cm, Height = 8 cm. Area = $4 \times 8 = 32 \text{ cm}^2$.
Rectangle 2 (right side step): Width = 6 cm, Height = 3 cm. Area = $6 \times 3 = 18 \text{ cm}^2$.
Total Area = $32 + 18 = 50 \text{ cm}^2$.

Problem 23
Figures A and B are congruent triangles. Figure A is pointing straight up, while Figure B is pointing to the right and has been shifted lower down. Name the transformations that would position Figure A on Figure B.
Answer:

Rotation and Translation

Explanation:

Because the figure has had its directional orientation turned 90 degrees clockwise, a rotation is required. To then slide it perfectly into the new location of Figure B lower down, a translation is needed.

Problem 24
The perimeter of a rectangle is 40 cm. If the length of the rectangle is 12 cm, what is the width?
Answer:

8 cm

Explanation:

The perimeter formula for a rectangle is $P = 2l + 2w$.
$$40 = 2(12) + 2w$$ $$40 = 24 + 2w$$ $$16 = 2w$$ $$w = 8 \text{ cm}$$

Problem 25
A circular track has a diameter of 200 m. Find the circumference of the track to the nearest ten meters.
Answer:

630 m

Explanation:

Circumference formula is $C = \pi \times d$. Using 3.14 for $\pi$:
$$C \approx 3.14 \times 200 = 628 \text{ m}$$
The question asks to round to the nearest ten meters. 628 rounded to the nearest ten is 630 m.

Problem 26
Use a ruler to draw line segment $\overline{AB}$ $4 \frac{1}{4}$ inches long. Then draw a dot at the midpoint of $AB$, and label the point $M$. How long is $\overline{AM}$?
Answer:

$2 \frac{1}{8}$ inches

Explanation:

The midpoint divides the segment exactly in half. Divide $4 \frac{1}{4}$ by 2: $$4 \frac{1}{4} = \frac{17}{4}$$ $$\frac{17}{4} \div 2 = \frac{17}{4} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{17}{8} = 2 \frac{1}{8}$$

Problem 27
Use a compass to draw a circle on a coordinate plane. Make the center of the circle the origin, and make the radius 5 units. At which two points does the circle cross the x-axis?
Answer:

(5, 0) and (-5, 0)

Explanation:

Since the circle is centered at the origin (0,0) and has a radius of 5, it will extend 5 units right and 5 units left straight along the x-axis.

Problem 28
What is the area of the circle in problem 27? (Use 3.14 for $\pi$.)
Answer:

78.5 square units

Explanation:

The area formula for a circle is $A = \pi \times r^2$.
Radius $r = 5$, so $r^2 = 25$.
$$A = 3.14 \times 25 = 78.5$$

Problem 30
If Mark rolls a standard 6-sided die two times, what is the probability that he rolls a 6 both times?
Answer:

$\frac{1}{36}$

Explanation:

The probability of rolling a 6 on a single roll is $\frac{1}{6}$. Since the two rolls are independent events, multiply the probabilities together:
$$\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{36}$$
