Lesson Practice

b.

What is the perimeter of this regular pentagon? (Each side is 5 cm.)

Answer: \(25\) cm.

Solution: A regular pentagon has 5 equal sides. Perimeter \(= 5 \times 5\text{ cm} = 25\text{ cm}\).

c.

If each side of a regular octagon measures 12 inches, what is its perimeter?

Answer: \(96\) in.

Solution: A regular octagon has 8 equal sides. Perimeter \(= 8 \times 12\text{ in.} = 96\text{ in.}\)

d.

What is the perimeter of this hexagon? (Stepped hexagon: 10 in. across the bottom, 2 in. on the left, 5 in. on the right, 4 in. across the top.)

Answer: \(30\) in.

Solution: Trace the 6 sides: bottom \(10\), right side \(5\), top \(4\), inner step down \(5-2 = 3\), inner step left \(10-4 = 6\), left side \(2\). Adding: \(10 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 6 + 2 = 30\) in.

e.

MacGregor has 100 feet of fence that he plans to use to enclose a square garden. Each side of his garden will be how many feet long?

Answer: \(25\) ft.

Solution: A square has 4 equal sides, so each side \(= \frac{100\text{ ft}}{4} = 25\text{ ft}\).

f.

Draw a quadrilateral with each side \(\frac{3}{4}\) inch long. What is the perimeter of the quadrilateral?

Answer: \(3\) in.

Solution: Draw a 4-sided closed figure with every side \(\frac{3}{4}\) in. long. Perimeter \(= 4 \times \frac{3}{4}\text{ in.} = \frac{12}{4}\text{ in.} = 3\text{ in.}\)

g. REPRESENT

The lengths of the sides of this polygon are \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), and \(d\). What is the perimeter of the polygon? Begin your answer this way: \(P =\)

Answer: \(P = a + b + c + d\).

Solution: The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of the lengths of all its sides, so add the four side lengths: \(P = a + b + c + d\).

Written Practice — Problems 1–6

1. (14)

One eighth of the students in the class are in the school band. What fraction of the total number of students are not in the band?

Answer: \(\frac{7}{8}\).

Solution: The whole class is \(1 = \frac{8}{8}\). Subtract the band members: \(\frac{8}{8} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{7}{8}\).

2. (11)

The theater was full when the movie began. Seventy-six people left before the movie ended. One hundred twenty-four people remained. How many people were in the theater when it was full?

Answer: \(200\) people.

Solution: Add the people who left to those who remained: \(76 + 124 = 200\).

3. (13)

All ants have 6 legs. A scientist studying ants observes each leg on every ant in her sample. If her sample contains 84 ants, how many legs does she observe?

Answer: \(504\) legs.

Solution: \(84 \text{ ants} \times 6 \text{ legs each} = 504\) legs.

Written Practice — Problems 7–14

7. (16)

At what temperatures on the Fahrenheit scale does water freeze? At what temperature does it boil?

Answer: Water freezes at \(32^\circ\text{F}\) and boils at \(212^\circ\text{F}\).

Solution: On the Fahrenheit scale, the freezing point of water is \(32^\circ\text{F}\) and the boiling point is \(212^\circ\text{F}\).

*8. (19)

Write a formula for the perimeter of a rectangle. Then find the perimeter of this rectangle: (8 cm by 6 cm)

Answer: Formula: \(P = 2l + 2w\) (or \(P = 2(l + w)\)). Perimeter: \(28\) cm.

Solution: A rectangle has two lengths and two widths, so \(P = 2l + 2w\). For \(l = 8\) cm and \(w = 6\) cm: \(P = 2(8) + 2(6) = 16 + 12 = 28\) cm.

*9. (15) GENERALIZE

Write each number as a reduced fraction or mixed number:

a. \(3\frac{16}{24}\)    b. \(\frac{15}{24}\)    c. \(4\%\)

Answer: a. \(3\frac{2}{3}\)    b. \(\frac{5}{8}\)    c. \(\frac{1}{25}\).

Solution: a. \(\frac{16}{24} = \frac{16 \div 8}{24 \div 8} = \frac{2}{3}\), so \(3\frac{16}{24} = 3\frac{2}{3}\). b. \(\frac{15}{24} = \frac{15 \div 3}{24 \div 3} = \frac{5}{8}\). c. \(4\% = \frac{4}{100} = \frac{1}{25}\).

*10. (15) ANALYZE

Find \(a\) and \(b\) to complete each equivalent fraction:

a. \(\frac{3}{4} = \frac{a}{36}\)    b. \(\frac{4}{9} = \frac{b}{36}\)

Answer: \(a = 27\), \(b = 16\).

Solution: a. \(4 \times 9 = 36\), so \(a = 3 \times 9 = 27\). b. \(9 \times 4 = 36\), so \(b = 4 \times 4 = 16\).

*11. (18) CLASSIFY

Which of these figures is not a polygon? Write the reason for your choice. (A: 3-D box, B: rectangle, C: triangle)

Answer: Figure A.

Solution: A polygon is a flat (two-dimensional) closed figure made of line segments. Figure A is a three-dimensional solid (a rectangular prism), not a flat figure, so it is not a polygon.

*12. (18)

What is the name of a polygon that has twice as many sides as a quadrilateral?

Answer: An octagon.

Solution: A quadrilateral has 4 sides; twice as many is \(2 \times 4 = 8\) sides. An 8-sided polygon is an octagon.

*13. (17)

a. Each angle of a rectangle measures how many degrees?

b. The four angles of a rectangle total how many degrees?

Answer: a. \(90^\circ\)    b. \(360^\circ\).

Solution: a. Every angle of a rectangle is a right angle, \(90^\circ\). b. \(4 \times 90^\circ = 360^\circ\).

14. (4, 9)

The rule of this sequence is \(k = \frac{1}{8}n\). Find the eighth term of the sequence. \(\left(\frac{1}{8}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{8}, \frac{1}{2}, \ldots\right)\)

Answer: \(1\).

Solution: Substitute \(n = 8\) into the rule: \(k = \frac{1}{8} \times 8 = \frac{8}{8} = 1\).

Written Practice — Problems 19–24: Simplify

19. (10)

\(\frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\)

Answer: \(2\).

Solution: Add the numerators: \(\frac{2+2+2}{3} = \frac{6}{3} = 2\).

20. (15)

\(3\frac{7}{8} - \frac{5}{8}\)

Answer: \(3\frac{1}{4}\).

Solution: Subtract the fractions: \(\frac{7}{8} - \frac{5}{8} = \frac{2}{8}\). So \(3\frac{7}{8} - \frac{5}{8} = 3\frac{2}{8} = 3\frac{1}{4}\) (reduced).

21. (15)

\(\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{7}\)

Answer: \(\frac{2}{7}\).

Solution: Multiply: \(\frac{2 \cdot 3}{3 \cdot 7} = \frac{6}{21} = \frac{2}{7}\). (The 3's cancel: \(\frac{2}{\cancel{3}} \cdot \frac{\cancel{3}}{7} = \frac{2}{7}\).)

22. (15)

\(3\frac{7}{8} + \frac{5}{8}\)

Answer: \(4\frac{1}{2}\).

Solution: Add the fractions: \(\frac{7}{8} + \frac{5}{8} = \frac{12}{8} = 1\frac{4}{8}\). So \(3 + 1\frac{4}{8} = 4\frac{4}{8} = 4\frac{1}{2}\) (reduced).

Written Practice — Problems 25–26

25. (9)

a. How many \(\frac{1}{2}\)s are in 1?

b. Use the answer to a to find the number of \(\frac{1}{2}\)s in 5.

Answer: a. \(2\)    b. \(10\).

Solution: a. \(1 \div \frac{1}{2} = 2\). b. Each whole contains two halves, so \(5 \times 2 = 10\) halves in 5.

26. (7, 6)

Use your ruler to draw \(\overline{AB}\) \(1\frac{1}{2}\) in. long. Then draw \(\overline{BC}\) perpendicular to \(\overline{AB}\) 2 in. long. Draw a segment from point \(A\) to point \(C\) to complete \(\triangle ABC\). What is the length of \(\overline{AC}\)?

Answer: \(2\frac{1}{2}\) in.

Solution: \(\triangle ABC\) is a right triangle with legs \(1\frac{1}{2}\) in. and 2 in. By the Pythagorean theorem, \(AC = \sqrt{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{\frac{9}{4} + 4} = \sqrt{\frac{25}{4}} = \frac{5}{2} = 2\frac{1}{2}\) in.

Written Practice — Problems 27–30

27. (9, 10)

Write \(3\frac{1}{3}\) as an improper fraction, and multiply it by the reciprocal of \(\frac{2}{3}\).

Answer: \(5\).

Solution: \(3\frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}\). The reciprocal of \(\frac{2}{3}\) is \(\frac{3}{2}\). Multiply: \(\frac{10}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{30}{6} = 5\).

*28. (15) EVALUATE

Find a fraction equal to \(\frac{1}{2}\) that has a denominator of 10. Subtract this fraction from \(\frac{9}{10}\). Write the difference as a reduced fraction.

Answer: \(\frac{2}{5}\).

Solution: \(\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 5}{2 \times 5} = \frac{5}{10}\). Subtract: \(\frac{9}{10} - \frac{5}{10} = \frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}\) (reduced).

Answer: \(33\frac{1}{3}\%\).

Solution: There are 3 feet in a yard, so a foot is \(\frac{1}{3}\) of a yard, and \(\frac{1}{3} = 33\frac{1}{3}\%\).

Real-World Connection

Magali needs to fill a five-gallon cooler with sports drink for the soccer game. The label on the drink mix says that each packet will make 10 quarts.

a.

Will one packet be enough to make a full cooler? If not, how many packets will Magali need? Show your work.

Answer: No — one packet is not enough. Magali needs 2 packets.

Solution: There are 4 quarts in a gallon, so a five-gallon cooler holds \(5 \times 4 = 20\) quarts. One packet makes only 10 quarts, which is not enough. Number of packets needed: \(\frac{20}{10} = 2\) packets.

b.

A package of 8 packets cost \(\$11.95\). Estimate the cost of 2 packets. Show your work.

Answer: About \(\$3.00\).

Solution: Estimate \(\$11.95 \approx \$12\). Cost per packet \(\approx \frac{\$12}{8} = \$1.50\). Cost of 2 packets \(\approx 2 \times \$1.50 = \$3.00\).
