Problem k ANALYZE
Perry's protractor is marked at each degree. Assuming the protractor is used correctly, what is the greatest possible error that can be made with Perry's protractor? Express your answer as a fraction of a degree.
Answer: $ rac{1}{2}^\circ$ (or $ rac{1}{2}$ degree)

Explanation: The precision of a measuring instrument with unit increments is half of its smallest division. Since the scale is marked at every $1^\circ$, any angle measurement rounded to the nearest degree will differ from the true value by at most half a degree:

$$ ext{Greatest possible error} = rac{1}{2} imes 1^\circ = rac{1}{2}^\circ$$

Two thousand, four hundred twenty people gathered before noon for the opening of a new park. An additional five thousand, ninety people arrived after noon. How many people were at the opening of the new park?
Answer: $7{,}510 ext{ people}$

Explanation: Write the numbers in digits and add the two quantities together:

$$ ext{After noon: } + 5{,}090$$

Problem 2 Lesson 14
A number cube is rolled once.
a. What is the probability of rolling a 5?
b. What is the probability of not rolling a 5?
Answer: a. $ rac{1}{6}$   |   b. $ rac{5}{6}$

Explanation: A standard number cube has $6$ equally likely outcomes $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.

a. Only 1 outcome is a 5:

$$P( ext{rolling a } 5) = rac{ ext{favorable outcomes}}{ ext{total outcomes}} = rac{1}{6}$$

b. The remaining 5 outcomes $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ are not 5:

$$P( ext{not rolling a } 5) = 1 - rac{1}{6} = rac{5}{6}$$

Problem 3 Lesson 13
There are 210 students in the first-year physical education class. If they are equally divided into 15 squads, how many students will be in each squad?
Answer: $14 ext{ students}$

Explanation: Divide total students by the number of squads:

$$ rac{210 ext{ students}}{15 ext{ squads}} = 14 ext{ students per squad}$$

Check: $14 imes 15 = 210$.

Problem 6 Lessons 7, 17 | ANALYZE
Refer to the figure to answer a–c:
a. Which line is parallel to $\overleftrightarrow{ST}$?
b. Which line is perpendicular to $\overleftrightarrow{ST}$?
c. Angle $QRT$ measures how many degrees?
Answer: a. Line $QR$ ($\overleftrightarrow{QR}$)   |   b. Line $RT$ ($\overleftrightarrow{RT}$)   |   c. $90^\circ$

Explanation:

a. Two lines in the same plane that are perpendicular to the same line ($\overleftrightarrow{RT}$) are parallel to each other. Thus, $\overleftrightarrow{QR}$ is parallel to $\overleftrightarrow{ST}$.

b. The line segment/line forming a right angle with $\overleftrightarrow{ST}$ is line $RT$ ($\overleftrightarrow{RT}$).

c. Since $\overleftrightarrow{QR}$ is perpendicular to $\overleftrightarrow{RT}$, $ngle QRT$ is a right angle, measuring $90^\circ$.

Problem 8 Lesson 16 | GENERALIZE
At the grocery store Len put five apples in a bag and weighed them. The scale showed the weight to be $2 ext{ lb } 8 ext{ oz}$. What is this weight in ounces?
Answer: $40 ext{ oz}$

Explanation: Know that $1 ext{ pound (lb)} = 16 ext{ ounces (oz)}$.

$$2 ext{ lb} = 2 imes 16 ext{ oz} = 32 ext{ oz}$$

$$ ext{Total weight} = 32 ext{ oz} + 8 ext{ oz} = 40 ext{ oz}$$

Problem 26 Lesson 1 | ANALYZE
The cost of the meal was $\$15.17$. Loretha gave the cashier a $\$20$ bill and a quarter. Name the fewest possible number of bills and coins she could have received in change.
Answer: 5 pieces of change (1 five-dollar bill, 1 nickel, and 3 pennies)

Explanation:

1. Total money given = $\$20.00 + \$0.25 = \$20.25$.

2. Change owed = $\$20.25 - \$15.17 = \$5.08$.

3. To get $\$5.08$ with the fewest bills and coins:

  • 1 $ imes$ $\$5$ bill = $\$5.00$
  • 1 $ imes$ nickel ($5 ext{¢}$) = $\$0.05$
  • 3 $ imes$ pennies ($1 ext{¢}$) = $\$0.03$

Total items: $1 + 1 + 3 = 5$ (One $\$5$ bill, one nickel, and three pennies).

Problem 27 Lessons 2, 9
a. Compare: $\left( rac{1}{2} \cdot rac{3}{4} ight) \cdot rac{2}{3} \quad igcirc \quad rac{1}{2}\left( rac{3}{4} \cdot rac{2}{3} ight)$
b. What property is illustrated by the comparison?
Answer: a. $=$   |   b. Associative Property of Multiplication

Explanation:

a. Evaluate left side: $$\left( rac{1}{2} \cdot rac{3}{4} ight) \cdot rac{2}{3} = rac{3}{8} \cdot rac{2}{3} = rac{6}{24} = rac{1}{4}$$

Evaluate right side: $$ rac{1}{2}\left( rac{3}{4} \cdot rac{2}{3} ight) = rac{1}{2} \cdot rac{6}{12} = rac{1}{2} \cdot rac{1}{2} = rac{1}{4}$$

Since both equal $ rac{1}{4}$, the relationship is equal ($=$).

b. Changing the grouping of factors in multiplication does not change the product. This is the Associative Property of Multiplication.
