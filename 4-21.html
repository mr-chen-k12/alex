Lesson Practice

Problem a
Draw a rectangle and a parallelogram that is not a rectangle or rhombus. Draw all the lines of symmetry for each shape, if any exist.
Answer & Visual:
Explanation:

A standard rectangle has exactly 2 lines of symmetry (one horizontal and one vertical straight through the midpoints). A standard parallelogram that leans to the side has 0 lines of symmetry because it cannot be folded along any line to perfectly overlap itself.

Problem b
All but one of these letters can be drawn to have a line of symmetry. Which of these letters does not have a line of symmetry?

D   E   H   J   M   X
Answer:

J

Explanation:

M and X have a vertical line of symmetry. D, E, H, and X have a horizontal line of symmetry. The letter J cannot be folded over any line to match perfectly.

Problem c
Which two of these letters have rotational symmetry? (Hint: Rotating your paper upside down might help.)

H   L   M   Q   V   X
Answer:

H and X

Explanation:

If you rotate the letters H and X by 180 degrees (upside down), they look identical to their original orientation. The others (L, M, Q, V) face the wrong direction or look inverted (like M becoming W) when rotated.

Written Practice

Problem 1
When the greatest three-digit number is divided by the greatest one-digit number, what is the quotient?
Answer:

111

Explanation:

The greatest three-digit number is 999. The greatest one-digit number is 9.
$$999 \div 9 = 111$$

Problem 2
The ratio of cats to dogs in a pet hotel is 4 to 3. If there are 15 dogs in the hotel, how many cats are there?
Answer:

20 cats

Explanation:

Set up a proportion: $$\frac{\text{Cats}}{\text{Dogs}} = \frac{4}{3} = \frac{C}{15}$$ Cross-multiply or find the scale factor: $3 \times 5 = 15$, so $4 \times 5 = 20$.

Problem 3
A glass cube has side lengths of 6 centimeters. How many cubic centimeters is the volume of the cube?
Answer:

216 cubic centimeters

Explanation:

The volume of a cube is calculated by cubing the side length ($s^3$).
$$V = 6 \times 6 \times 6 = 36 \times 6 = 216 \text{ cm}^3$$

Problem 4
A full turn is 360 degrees. How many degrees is $\frac{1}{8}$ of a turn?
Answer:

45 degrees

Explanation:

Multiply the full turn by the fraction: $$360^\circ \times \frac{1}{8} = 45^\circ$$

Problem 5
Does a regular hexagon have rotational and line symmetry? Use words to explain how you know.
Answer:

Yes, it has both.

Explanation:

Line Symmetry: A regular hexagon has 6 lines of symmetry (three connecting opposite vertices and three connecting the midpoints of opposite sides).
Rotational Symmetry: Because all 6 sides and angles are equal, rotating the hexagon by 60 degrees ($360 \div 6$) around its center perfectly maps it onto its original position.

Problem 6
Sketch a rectangle similar to a 4 cm by 7 cm rectangle. Make the scale factor from the original rectangle to your sketch 3. What is the perimeter of the rectangle you sketched?
Answer:

66 cm

Explanation:

The original rectangle has dimensions 4 cm and 7 cm. With a scale factor of 3, the new side lengths are $4 \times 3 = 12 \text{ cm}$ and $7 \times 3 = 21 \text{ cm}$.
The perimeter of the new rectangle is $12 + 21 + 12 + 21 = 66 \text{ cm}$.

Problem 7
What is the area of a right triangle with legs of 6 cm and 8 cm, and a hypotenuse of 10 cm?
Answer:

24 cm²

Explanation:

The area of a right triangle is calculated using the two legs as the base and height (ignore the hypotenuse for area calculations).
$$Area = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = \frac{1}{2} \times 48 = 24 \text{ cm}^2$$

Problem 8
Draw a ratio box for this problem. Then solve the problem using a proportion.

A pencil case contains 50 writing utensils. If 40% of the utensils are pens and the rest are pencils, how many pencils are in the case?
Answer:

30 pencils

Explanation:

If 40% are pens, then 60% are pencils (100% - 40%). Set up a proportion comparing the pencil percentage to the total: $$\frac{\text{Percent}}{\text{Total}} \Rightarrow \frac{60}{100} = \frac{x}{50}$$ Cross-multiplying gives: $100x = 3000$, so $x = 30$.
There are 30 pencils.

Problem 9
Convert the fraction $3 \frac{3}{4}$ to a decimal (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. 3.75    b. 375%

Explanation:

$\frac{3}{4}$ is equivalent to 0.75, so $3 \frac{3}{4} = 3.75$. To find the percent, multiply the decimal by 100 to get 375%.

Problem 10
Convert the decimal 2.8 to a mixed fraction (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. $2 \frac{4}{5}$    b. 280%

Explanation:

2.8 is "two and eight-tenths" ($2 \frac{8}{10}$), which simplifies to $2 \frac{4}{5}$ by dividing numerator and denominator by 2. Multiply 2.8 by 100 to find the percent: 280%.

Problem 11
Convert 45% to a fraction (a) and a decimal (b).
Answer:

a. $\frac{9}{20}$    b. 0.45

Explanation:

45% as a decimal is 0.45. As a fraction, it is $\frac{45}{100}$, which reduces by dividing numerator and denominator by 5 to get $\frac{9}{20}$.

Problem 12
$$10 \frac{1}{4} + 5 \frac{1}{2} + 3 \frac{1}{8}$$
Answer:

$18 \frac{7}{8}$

Explanation:

Find a common denominator for the fractions, which is 8: $$10 \frac{2}{8} + 5 \frac{4}{8} + 3 \frac{1}{8}$$ Add the whole numbers: $10 + 5 + 3 = 18$.
Add the fractions: $\frac{2}{8} + \frac{4}{8} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{7}{8}$.
Total: $18 \frac{7}{8}$.

Problem 13
$$\left(4 \frac{1}{2} \div 3\right) \div 1 \frac{1}{5}$$
Answer:

$1 \frac{1}{4}$

Explanation:

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions: $$\left(\frac{9}{2} \div \frac{3}{1}\right) \div \frac{6}{5}$$ Follow PEMDAS, evaluate parentheses by multiplying by the reciprocal: $$\left(\frac{9}{2} \times \frac{1}{3}\right) \div \frac{6}{5} = \frac{9}{6} \div \frac{6}{5} = \frac{3}{2} \div \frac{6}{5}$$ Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction: $$\frac{3}{2} \times \frac{5}{6} = \frac{15}{12} = \frac{5}{4} = 1 \frac{1}{4}$$

Subtract from 20: $20.00 - 7.75 = 12.25$.

Problem 17
Round the quotient to the nearest hundredth: $$0.086 \div 5$$
Answer:

0.02

Explanation:

Divide: $$0.086 \div 5 = 0.0172$$ The hundredths place is 1. The next digit is 7, which is 5 or greater, so round up the 1 to 2. The rounded answer is 0.02.

Problem 18
Write the prime factorization of 400 using exponents.
Answer:

$2^4 \times 5^2$

Explanation:

Break down 400: $400 = 4 \times 100$.
$4 = 2 \times 2 = 2^2$.
$100 = 10 \times 10 = (2 \times 5) \times (2 \times 5) = 2^2 \times 5^2$.
Combine them in numerical order: $2^4 \times 5^2$.

Problem 19
"All equilateral triangles are similar." True or false?
Answer:

True

Explanation:

All equilateral triangles have three exactly equal angles of 60 degrees. Because their corresponding angles are always identical, their side lengths are always proportional, making them universally similar shapes.

Problem 20
$$4^3 - 4^2 \div 4 - 4 \times 2$$
Answer:

52

Explanation:

Follow the order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS):
Exponents first: $64 - 16 \div 4 - 4 \times 2$
Multiplication and Division (left to right): $64 - 4 - 8$
Addition and Subtraction (left to right): $60 - 8 = 52$.

Problem 21
What is the perimeter of this polygon? (An L-shaped polygon with top horizontal edge = 16 cm, left vertical edge = 12 cm, right vertical edge = 5 cm, and bottom-inner horizontal edge = 10 cm).
Answer:

56 cm

Explanation:

To find the perimeter, add all the outer boundaries.
Top edge = 16 cm
Left edge = 12 cm
Right edge = 5 cm
Bottom-inner horizontal edge = 10 cm
The missing bottom horizontal segment is $16 \text{ cm} - 10 \text{ cm} = 6 \text{ cm}$.
The missing inner vertical "step-down" segment is $12 \text{ cm} - 5 \text{ cm} = 7 \text{ cm}$.
$$Perimeter = 16 + 12 + 6 + 7 + 10 + 5 = 56 \text{ cm}$$

Problem 22
What is the area of the polygon from problem 21?
Answer:

122 cm²

Explanation:

Split the polygon into two rectangles.
Rectangle 1 (left side column): Width = 6 cm, Height = 12 cm. Area = $6 \times 12 = 72 \text{ cm}^2$.
Rectangle 2 (right side step): Width = 10 cm, Height = 5 cm. Area = $10 \times 5 = 50 \text{ cm}^2$.
Total Area = $72 + 50 = 122 \text{ cm}^2$.

Problem 23
Figures A and B are congruent right triangles. Figure A is pointing to the right, while Figure B is pointing left and has been shifted downwards. Name the transformations that would position Figure A on Figure B.
Answer:

Reflection and Translation (or Rotation and Translation)

Explanation:

Because the figure has had its directional orientation flipped backwards, a reflection over a vertical axis (or a 180-degree rotation depending on visual orientation) is required. To then slide it perfectly into the lower location of Figure B, a translation is needed.

Problem 24
The perimeter of a rectangle is 50 cm. If the width of the rectangle is 10 cm, what is the length?
Answer:

15 cm

Explanation:

The perimeter formula for a rectangle is $P = 2l + 2w$.
$$50 = 2l + 2(10)$$ $$50 = 2l + 20$$ $$30 = 2l$$ $$l = 15 \text{ cm}$$

Problem 25
A circular track has a diameter of 300 m. Find the circumference of the track to the nearest ten meters.
Answer:

940 m

Explanation:

Circumference formula is $C = \pi \times d$. Using 3.14 for $\pi$:
$$C \approx 3.14 \times 300 = 942 \text{ m}$$
The question asks to round to the nearest ten meters. 942 rounded to the nearest ten is 940 m.

Problem 26
Use a ruler to draw line segment $\overline{CD}$ $5 \frac{1}{2}$ inches long. Then draw a dot at the midpoint of $CD$, and label the point $M$. How long is $\overline{CM}$?
Answer:

$2 \frac{3}{4}$ inches

Explanation:

The midpoint divides the segment exactly in half. Divide $5 \frac{1}{2}$ by 2: $$5 \frac{1}{2} = \frac{11}{2}$$ $$\frac{11}{2} \div 2 = \frac{11}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{11}{4} = 2 \frac{3}{4}$$

Problem 27
Use a compass to draw a circle on a coordinate plane. Make the center of the circle the origin, and make the radius 7 units. At which two points does the circle cross the y-axis?
Answer:

(0, 7) and (0, -7)

Explanation:

Since the circle is centered at the origin (0,0) and has a radius of 7, it will extend 7 units straight up and 7 units straight down along the y-axis.

Problem 28
What is the area of the circle in problem 27? (Use 3.14 for $\pi$.)

153.86 square units

Explanation:

The area formula for a circle is $A = \pi \times r^2$.
Radius $r = 7$, so $r^2 = 49$.
$$A = 3.14 \times 49 = 153.86$$

Problem 30
If Sarah rolls a standard 6-sided die three times, what is the probability that she rolls an even number all three times?
Answer:

$\frac{1}{8}$

Explanation:

The probability of rolling an even number (2, 4, or 6) on a single roll is $\frac{3}{6}$, which simplifies to $\frac{1}{2}$. Since the three rolls are independent events, multiply the probabilities together:
$$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{8}$$
