Lesson Practice

Divide \(55\) by \(4\) and write the answer…

a. With a remainder

Divide \(55\) by \(4\) and write the answer with a remainder.

\(4\) goes into \(55\) thirteen times, because \(4 \times 13 = 52\).

\[55 - 52 = 3\]

Check: \(55 = 4 \times 13 + 3\).

Answer: \(\boxed{13 \text{ R } 3}\)

b. As a mixed number

Divide \(55\) by \(4\) and write the answer as a mixed number.

Write the division as a fraction and simplify into a mixed number:

\[\frac{55}{4} = 13\frac{3}{4}\]

The remainder \(3\) becomes the numerator over the divisor \(4\).

Answer: \(\boxed{13\frac{3}{4}}\)

c. As a decimal number

Divide \(55\) by \(4\) and write the answer as a decimal number.

\[\frac{55}{4} = \frac{55 \times 25}{4 \times 25} = \frac{1375}{100} = 13.75\]

Answer: \(\boxed{13.75}\)

d. Divide and round

Divide \(5.5\) by \(3\) and round the answer to three decimal places.

\[5.5 \div 3 = 1.8333\ldots\]

The digit after the third decimal place is \(3\), so we round down and keep \(1.833\).

Answer: \(\boxed{1.833}\)

e. Classrooms GENERALIZE

Ninety-three students are assigned to four classrooms as equally as possible. How many students are in each of the four classrooms?

\[93 \div 4 = 23 \text{ R } 1\]

Each classroom gets \(23\) students, and the \(1\) leftover student goes into one classroom, making it \(24\).

Check: \(23 + 23 + 23 + 24 = 93\). ✓

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{Three classrooms have } 23 \text{ students, and one classroom has } 24.}\)

f. Grapes

Toby bought only one pound of grapes. How much did he pay? Explain your answer.

Find the price for one pound (the unit price):

\[\$2 \div 3 = \frac{2}{3} \text{ of a dollar} \approx \$0.67\]

Since \(1\) pound is one third of \(3\) pounds, Toby pays one third of \(\$2\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{About } \$0.67}\)

g. Photo album JUSTIFY

Nita is putting photos in an album. She places \(6\) photos on each page. If she has \(74\) photos, how many pages of photos are in the book? Explain how you found the answer.

\[74 \div 6 = 12 \text{ R } 2\]

That is \(12\) full pages, with \(2\) photos left over. The \(2\) leftover photos still need a page of their own, so add one more page:

\[12 + 1 = 13\]

Answer: \(\boxed{13 \text{ pages}}\)

Written Practice

1. Ratio of a rectangle

The rectangle was \(24\) inches long and \(18\) inches wide. What was the ratio of its length to its width?

\[\text{length} : \text{width} = 24 : 18\]

Divide both numbers by \(6\) to reduce:

\[24 : 18 = 4 : 3\]

Answer: \(\boxed{4:3}\)

2. Mean bowling score

Lakeisha participates in a bowling league. In her first ten games her scores were \(90, 95, 90, 85, 80, 85, 90, 80, 95,\) and \(100\). What was her mean (average) score?

Add the ten scores:

\[90+95+90+85+80+85+90+80+95+100 = 890\]

Divide by the number of games:

\[\text{mean} = \frac{890}{10} = 89\]

Answer: \(\boxed{89}\)

3. Choosing a graph

Which would be a better graph for displaying her scores (from problem 2), a circle graph or a bar graph? Why?

A bar graph is better. Each score is a separate value for one game, and bars make it easy to compare the games one by one. A circle graph shows parts of a whole, but these scores do not add up to a meaningful whole.

Score in each game, 1–10

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{A bar graph}}\)

4. Stamps and change

Rachel bought a sheet of fifty \(39\)-cent stamps from the post office. She paid for the stamps with a \(\$20\) bill. How much money should she get back?

Cost of the stamps:

\[50 \times 39\text{ cents} = 1950\text{ cents} = \$19.50\]

Change:

\[\$20.00 - \$19.50 = \$0.50\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\$0.50}\)

5. Difference in words

Ninety-seven thousandths is how much less than two and ninety-eight hundredths? Write the answer in words.

Write both numbers as decimals and subtract:

\[2.98 - 0.097 = 2.883\]

\(2.883\) is read as “two and eight hundred eighty-three thousandths.”

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{two and eight hundred eighty-three thousandths}}\)

6. Motorcycles on the lot

Read this statement. Then answer the questions that follow.

Five sixths of the thirty motorcycles on the lot were new.

a. How many motorcycles were used?
b. What was the ratio of new motorcycles to used motorcycles?

Number of new motorcycles:

\[\frac{5}{6} \times 30 = 25\]

Number used: \(30 - 25 = 5\).

Ratio of new to used:

\[25 : 5 = 5 : 1\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 5 \text{ used} \qquad \text{b. } 5:1}\)

7. Perimeter of a polygon

Copy this figure on your paper. Find the length of each unmarked side, and find the perimeter of the polygon. Dimensions are in meters. All angles are right angles.

Dimensions in meters

Bottom of the notch: the whole width is \(16\) m. Take away the \(5\) m on the left and the \(4\) m on the right:

\[16 - 5 - 4 = 7 \text{ m}\]

Right side of the notch: the notch bottom is \(18 - 8 = 10\) m above the base. The right side of the figure is \(15\) m tall, so:

\[15 - 10 = 5 \text{ m}\]

Perimeter — add all eight sides:

\[P = 5 + 8 + 7 + 5 + 4 + 15 + 16 + 18 = 78 \text{ m}\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{Unmarked sides: } 7 \text{ m and } 5 \text{ m; perimeter } = 78 \text{ m}}\)

8. Fractions, decimals, and percents

a. Write \(0.75\) as a simplified fraction.
b. Write \(\dfrac{5}{8}\) as a decimal number.
c. Write \(125\%\) as a decimal number.

a. \(0.75 = \dfrac{75}{100} = \dfrac{3}{4}\) (divide top and bottom by \(25\)).

b. \(5 \div 8 = 0.625\).

c. Move the decimal point two places left: \(125\% = 1.25\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } \frac{3}{4} \qquad \text{b. } 0.625 \qquad \text{c. } 1.25}\)

9. Probability ANALYZE

Samuel has \(52\) marbles in a bag. Fourteen are red, \(13\) are green and \(25\) are blue. If a marble is chosen at random, what is the probability that it is green?

\[P(\text{green}) = \frac{\text{green marbles}}{\text{all marbles}} = \frac{13}{52} = \frac{1}{4}\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\frac{1}{4}}\)

10. Distributive property

The expression \(2(3 + 4)\) equals which of the following?

A \((2 \cdot 3) + 4\)    B \((2 \cdot 3) + (2 \cdot 4)\)
C \(2 + 7\)    D \(2^3 + 2^4\)

The distributive property multiplies the \(2\) by both numbers inside the parentheses:

\[2(3 + 4) = (2 \cdot 3) + (2 \cdot 4) = 6 + 8 = 14\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{B}}\)

11. Triangle numbers ANALYZE

“Triangle numbers” are the numbers in this sequence: \(1,\ 3,\ 6,\ 10,\ \ldots\)

a. Find the next two terms in the sequence.
b. The rule in this function table shows how to find a term if we know its position. The fifth term is \(15\) because \(\dfrac{5^2 + 5}{2} = 15\). Use the rule to find the tenth term.

Position \((n)\)1234567
Term \(\left(\dfrac{n^2+n}{2}\right)\)13610

a. Each new triangle adds one more row. The differences grow: \(+2, +3, +4\), so next add \(5\), then \(6\):

\[10 + 5 = 15, \qquad 15 + 6 = 21\]

b. Use the rule \(\dfrac{n^2+n}{2}\):

\[n=5:\ \frac{25+5}{2}=15 \qquad n=6:\ \frac{36+6}{2}=21 \qquad n=7:\ \frac{49+7}{2}=28\]

Tenth term:

\[\frac{10^2 + 10}{2} = \frac{110}{2} = 55\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 15,\ 21 \qquad \text{b. Table: } 15,\ 21,\ 28; \text{ tenth term } = 55}\)

12. Repeating decimal

Divide \(5.4\) by \(11\) and write the answer a. with a bar over the repetend. b. rounded to the nearest thousandth.

\[5.4 \div 11 = 0.490909\ldots\]

a. After the \(4\), the digits \(90\) repeat forever: \(0.4\overline{90}\).

b. The fourth decimal place is \(9\), so \(0.490\) rounds up to \(0.491\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 0.4\overline{90} \qquad \text{b. } 0.491}\)

13. Product of primes

What composite number is equal to the product of the first four prime numbers?

The first four prime numbers are \(2, 3, 5,\) and \(7\):

\[2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 = 6 \times 35 = 210\]

Answer: \(\boxed{210}\)

14. Ordering numbers ANALYZE CLASSIFY

a. Arrange these numbers in order from least to greatest:

\[1.2,\quad -12,\quad 0.12,\quad 0,\quad \frac{1}{2},\quad 1.\overline{2}\]

b. Which numbers in a are integers?

a. Compare as decimals: \(\dfrac{1}{2} = 0.5\) and \(1.\overline{2} = 1.222\ldots\), which is a little more than \(1.2\).

\[-12 \;<\; 0 \;<\; 0.12 \;<\; \frac{1}{2} \;<\; 1.2 \;<\; 1.\overline{2}\]

b. Integers are whole numbers and their opposites. Here those are \(-12\) and \(0\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } -12,\ 0,\ 0.12,\ \tfrac{1}{2},\ 1.2,\ 1.\overline{2} \qquad \text{b. } -12 \text{ and } 0}\)

15. Stack of math books

Each math book is \(1\frac{1}{2}\) inches thick.

a. A stack of \(12\) math books would stand how many inches tall?
b. How many math books would make a stack \(1\) yard tall?

a. \[12 \times 1\frac{1}{2} = 12 \times 1.5 = 18 \text{ inches}\]

b. One yard is \(36\) inches:

\[36 \div 1.5 = 24 \text{ books}\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 18 \text{ inches} \qquad \text{b. } 24 \text{ books}}\)

16. Number line

What is the sum of the numbers represented by points \(M\) and \(N\) on this number line?

The ticks are hundredths (\(0.01\)) apart. Count the ticks:

\(M\) is \(6\) ticks past \(2.4\), so \(M = 2.46\).   \(N\) is \(4\) ticks past \(2.5\), so \(N = 2.54\).

\[2.46 + 2.54 = 5.00\]

Answer: \(\boxed{5.00}\)

17. Estimating \(\pi d\) CONCLUDE

Estimate the value of \(\pi d\) when \(\pi\) is \(3.14159\) and \(d\) is \(9.847\) meters.

Round each number to make the multiplication easy: \(\pi \approx 3\) and \(d \approx 10\) m.

\[\pi d \approx 3 \times 10 = 30 \text{ meters}\]

(The exact product is about \(30.94\) m, so about \(30\) m is a good estimate.)

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{About } 30 \text{ meters}}\)

18. Square

Draw a square with sides \(2.5\) cm long.

a. What is the area of the square?
b. What is the perimeter of the square?

a. Area: \(A = 2.5 \times 2.5 = 6.25 \text{ cm}^2\).

b. Perimeter: \(P = 4 \times 2.5 = 10 \text{ cm}\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 6.25 \text{ cm}^2 \qquad \text{b. } 10 \text{ cm}}\)

19. Area of a triangle on a grid

The coordinates of the vertices of a triangle are \((-2, 0)\), \((4, 0)\), and \((3, 3)\). What is the area of the triangle?

Plot the points. The side from \((-2,0)\) to \((4,0)\) lies on the \(x\)-axis and is the base:

\[\text{base} = 4 - (-2) = 6 \text{ units}\]

The third vertex \((3,3)\) is \(3\) units above the base, so the height is \(3\).

\[A = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 3 = 9\]

Answer: \(\boxed{9 \text{ square units}}\)

\[5\frac{3}{4} + \frac{5}{6} + 2\frac{1}{2}\]

30. Rectangle angles JUSTIFY

Figure \(ABCD\) is a rectangle. The measure of \(\angle ADB\) is \(35^\circ\). Find the measure of each angle below. Defend how you found the answers.

a. \(\angle ABD\)    b. \(\angle CBD\)    c. \(\angle BDC\)

Diagonal \(DB\); \(\angle ADB = 35^\circ\)

a. In \(\triangle ABD\), the angle at \(A\) is a right angle (\(90^\circ\)), because \(ABCD\) is a rectangle. The angles of a triangle add to \(180^\circ\):

\[\angle ABD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ\]

b. The corner angle \(\angle ABC\) is \(90^\circ\). Take away \(\angle ABD\):

\[\angle CBD = 90^\circ - 55^\circ = 35^\circ\]

(This also matches \(\angle ADB\) because \(AD \parallel BC\) — alternate interior angles are equal.)

c. In \(\triangle BCD\), the angle at \(C\) is \(90^\circ\), and \(\angle CBD = 35^\circ\) from part b:

\[\angle BDC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ\]

(It also equals \(\angle ABD\) by alternate interior angles.)

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } \angle ABD = 55^\circ \qquad \text{b. } \angle CBD = 35^\circ \qquad \text{c. } \angle BDC = 55^\circ}\)
