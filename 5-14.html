Two-Step Equations (Lesson 106)

1. Solve: \( 4n + 2 = 30 \)
Explanation:

Subtract 2 from both sides:
\( 4n = 30 - 2 \)
\( 4n = 28 \)

Divide by 4:
\( n = \frac{28}{4} \)

Answer: \( n = 7 \)
2. Solve: \( 3m - 3 = 21 \)
Explanation:

Add 3 to both sides:
\( 3m = 21 + 3 \)
\( 3m = 24 \)

Divide by 3:
\( m = \frac{24}{3} \)

Answer: \( m = 8 \)
3. Solve: \( 5y + 4 = 34 \)
Explanation:

Subtract 4 from both sides:
\( 5y = 34 - 4 \)
\( 5y = 30 \)

Divide by 5:
\( y = \frac{30}{5} \)

Answer: \( y = 6 \)
4. Solve: \( 7w + 3 = 17 \)
Explanation:

Subtract 3 from both sides:
\( 7w = 17 - 3 \)
\( 7w = 14 \)

Divide by 7:
\( w = \frac{14}{7} \)

Answer: \( w = 2 \)
5. Solve: \( 6k - 2 = 16 \)
Explanation:

Add 2 to both sides:
\( 6k = 16 + 2 \)
\( 6k = 18 \)

Divide by 6:
\( k = \frac{18}{6} \)

Answer: \( k = 3 \)
6. Solve: \( 3z + 1 = 28 \)
Explanation:

Subtract 1 from both sides:
\( 3z = 28 - 1 \)
\( 3z = 27 \)

Divide by 3:
\( z = \frac{27}{3} \)

Answer: \( z = 9 \)

Area of a Circle (Lesson 86)

Note: Use 3.14 for \( \pi \)

1. What is the area of the circle? (Radius = 5 cm)
Explanation:

Formula for area: \( A = \pi r^2 \)

\( A = 3.14 \times (5)^2 \)
\( A = 3.14 \times 25 \)
\( A = 78.5 \)

Answer: 78.5 cm²
2. If the radius of a circle is 4 cm, what is its area?
Explanation:

Formula for area: \( A = \pi r^2 \)

\( A = 3.14 \times (4)^2 \)
\( A = 3.14 \times 16 \)
\( A = 50.24 \)

Answer: 50.24 cm²
3. If the radius of a circle is 2 cm, what is its area?
Explanation:

Formula for area: \( A = \pi r^2 \)

\( A = 3.14 \times (2)^2 \)
\( A = 3.14 \times 4 \)
\( A = 12.56 \)

Answer: 12.56 cm²
4. The diameter of a circle target is 14 inches. What is the area of the target?
Explanation:

First, find the radius. Radius is half the diameter: \( r = \frac{14}{2} = 7 \) inches.

Now, calculate area: \( A = \pi r^2 \)
\( A = 3.14 \times (7)^2 \)
\( A = 3.14 \times 49 \)
\( A = 153.86 \)

Answer: 153.86 sq inches
5. The diameter of a circular tray is 24 inches. What is the area of the tray?
Explanation:

First, find the radius. Radius is half the diameter: \( r = \frac{24}{2} = 12 \) inches.

Now, calculate area: \( A = \pi r^2 \)
\( A = 3.14 \times (12)^2 \)
\( A = 3.14 \times 144 \)
\( A = 452.16 \)

Answer: 452.16 sq inches

Perimeter of Complex Shapes (Lesson 103)

1. What is the perimeter of this figure? (Labeled sides: Top=10m, Right=7m, Bottom=5m, Left=4m)
Explanation:

To find the perimeter, all side lengths are needed. Two inner sides are missing.

Inner vertical side = Right side - Left side = \( 7\text{m} - 4\text{m} = 3\text{m} \)

Inner horizontal side = Top side - Bottom side = \( 10\text{m} - 5\text{m} = 5\text{m} \)

Add all the sides: \( 10 + 7 + 5 + 3 + 5 + 4 = 34 \)

Answer: 34 m
2. What is the perimeter of this figure? (Labeled sides: Top=5m, Right=4m, Bottom=15m, Left=8m)
Explanation:

Find the missing inner sides.

Inner vertical side = Left side - Right side = \( 8\text{m} - 4\text{m} = 4\text{m} \)

Inner horizontal side = Bottom side - Top side = \( 15\text{m} - 5\text{m} = 10\text{m} \)

Add all the sides: \( 5 + 4 + 10 + 4 + 15 + 8 = 46 \)

Answer: 46 m
3. What is the perimeter of the hexagon? (Labeled sides: Left=8in, Bottom=3in, Right-top=3in, Inner-horizontal=3in, Inner-vertical=? missing)
Explanation:

We trace the L-shaped hexagon. The total left height is 8 in. The right side is composed of a 3 in segment and an inner vertical segment.

Inner vertical segment = \( 8\text{in} - 3\text{in} = 5\text{in} \)

The total top width equals the sum of the bottom width and the inner horizontal width: \( 3\text{in} + 3\text{in} = 6\text{in} \)

Add all the sides: \( 8\text{ (left)} + 6\text{ (top)} + 3\text{ (right)} + 3\text{ (inner horizontal)} + 5\text{ (inner vertical)} + 3\text{ (bottom)} = 28 \)

Answer: 28 in
4. What is the perimeter of the hexagon? (Labeled sides: Bottom=5cm, Right=3cm, Top=3cm, Left=2cm, Top-inner-horizontal=2cm)
Explanation:

Find the missing inner vertical side.

Inner vertical side = Right side - Left side = \( 3\text{cm} - 2\text{cm} = 1\text{cm} \)

Verify horizontal: Bottom (5cm) = Top (3cm) + Inner horizontal (2cm). This matches perfectly.

Add all the sides: \( 5\text{ (bottom)} + 3\text{ (right)} + 3\text{ (top)} + 1\text{ (inner vertical)} + 2\text{ (inner horizontal)} + 2\text{ (left)} = 16 \)

Answer: 16 cm

Area of Complex Shapes (Lesson 107)

1. What is the area of the hexagon? (Sides: Top=10m, Right=4m, Bottom=7m, Left=1m)
Explanation:

Divide the shape vertically into two rectangles.

Left rectangle: Width = \( 10\text{m} - 7\text{m} = 3\text{m} \). Height = \( 1\text{m} \). Area = \( 3 \times 1 = 3\text{ m}^2 \).

Right rectangle: Width = \( 7\text{m} \). Height = \( 4\text{m} \). Area = \( 7 \times 4 = 28\text{ m}^2 \).

Total Area = \( 3 + 28 = 31 \)

Answer: 31 m²
2. What is the area of this figure? (Sides: Left=16cm, Bottom=12cm, Right=8cm, Top=6cm)
Explanation:

Divide the shape vertically into two rectangles.

Left rectangle: Width = \( 6\text{cm} \). Height = \( 16\text{cm} \). Area = \( 6 \times 16 = 96\text{ cm}^2 \).

Right rectangle: Width = \( 12\text{cm} - 6\text{cm} = 6\text{cm} \). Height = \( 8\text{cm} \). Area = \( 6 \times 8 = 48\text{ cm}^2 \).

Total Area = \( 96 + 48 = 144 \)

Answer: 144 cm²
3. What is the area of this figure? (Sides: Top=8mm, Right=2mm, Bottom=2mm, Inner vertical=4mm)
Explanation:

Divide the L-shape into two rectangles: a horizontal top bar and a vertical bottom leg.

Top rectangle: Width = \( 8\text{mm} \), Height = \( 2\text{mm} \). Area = \( 8 \times 2 = 16\text{ mm}^2 \).

Bottom rectangle (leg): Width = \( 2\text{mm} \) (from bottom), Height = \( 4\text{mm} \) (inner vertical). Area = \( 2 \times 4 = 8\text{ mm}^2 \).

Total Area = \( 16 + 8 = 24 \)

Answer: 24 mm²
4. Find the area of this figure. (Trapezoid: Top base=6in, Bottom base=8in, Height=3in)
Explanation:

You can use the formula for the area of a trapezoid: \( A = \frac{b_1 + b_2}{2} \times h \)

\( A = \frac{6 + 8}{2} \times 3 \)
\( A = \frac{14}{2} \times 3 \)
\( A = 7 \times 3 = 21 \)

Answer: 21 in²

Compound Experiments (Lesson Inv. 10)

1. A bag contains 3 red marbles and 2 blue marbles. If one marble is drawn and not replaced and another marble is drawn, what is the probability that both marbles will be blue?
Explanation:

Total marbles = \( 3 + 2 = 5 \).

Probability first is blue = \( \frac{2}{5} \)

After keeping the first blue marble, 4 marbles remain, and 1 is blue.
Probability second is blue = \( \frac{1}{4} \)

Multiply: \( \frac{2}{5} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{20} = \frac{1}{10} \)

Answer: \( \frac{1}{10} \)
2. What is the probability of rolling a 2 twice with two rolls of a number cube?
Explanation:

Probability of rolling a 2 on the first roll = \( \frac{1}{6} \)

Probability of rolling a 2 on the second roll = \( \frac{1}{6} \)

Multiply: \( \frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{36} \)

Answer: \( \frac{1}{36} \)
3. A bag contains 5 red, 2 blue, and 3 yellow marbles. If one marble is drawn and not replaced and another marble is drawn, what is the probability that both marbles will be blue?
Explanation:

Total marbles = \( 5 + 2 + 3 = 10 \).

Probability first is blue = \( \frac{2}{10} = \frac{1}{5} \)

After keeping the first blue marble, 9 marbles remain, and 1 is blue.
Probability second is blue = \( \frac{1}{9} \)

Multiply: \( \frac{1}{5} \times \frac{1}{9} = \frac{1}{45} \)

Answer: \( \frac{1}{45} \)
4. In a bag there are four white marbles and four blue marbles. If two marbles are drawn from the bag at the same time, what is the probability that both marbles will be white?
Explanation:

Drawing two at the same time has the same probability as drawing one, not replacing it, and drawing another.

Total marbles = 8.

Probability first is white = \( \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2} \)

Probability second is white = \( \frac{3}{7} \)

Multiply: \( \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{7} = \frac{3}{14} \)

Answer: \( \frac{3}{14} \)

Sum of the Angle Measures of Triangles and Quadrilaterals (Lesson 98)

1. Triangle FGH is an isosceles triangle. Angles G and H each measure 65°. What is the measure of angle F?
Explanation:

The sum of interior angles in a triangle is 180°.

\( \angle F = 180^\circ - (65^\circ + 65^\circ) \)
\( \angle F = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ \)

Answer: 50°
2. Triangle ABC is a right triangle. Angle A measures 90° and angle B measures 50°. What is the measure of angle C?
Explanation:

The sum of interior angles in a triangle is 180°.

\( \angle C = 180^\circ - (90^\circ + 50^\circ) \)
\( \angle C = 180^\circ - 140^\circ = 40^\circ \)

Answer: 40°
3. What is the measure of \( \angle K\) in quadrilateral JKLM? (Diagram shows J=80°, M=100°, L=120°)
Explanation:

The sum of interior angles in a quadrilateral is 360°.

\( \angle K = 360^\circ - (80^\circ + 100^\circ + 120^\circ) \)
\( \angle K = 360^\circ - 300^\circ = 60^\circ \)

Answer: 60°
4. What is the sum of the measures of the four interior angles of a square?
Explanation:

A square is a quadrilateral, so its sum is 360°. Specifically, it has four 90° angles, and \( 4 \times 90^\circ = 360^\circ \).

Answer: 360°

Fraction-Decimal-Percent Equivalents (Lesson 99)

Complete the table for the missing values.
Fraction Decimal Percent
1. \( \frac{4}{5} \) b. ? b. ?
2. a. ? a. ? 6%
3. a. ? 1.7 b. ?
Explanation & Answers:

Row 1: Given Fraction \( \frac{4}{5} \)
To find Decimal: \( 4 \div 5 = 0.8 \)
To find Percent: \( 0.8 \times 100\% = 80\% \)

Row 2: Given Percent \( 6\% \)
To find Decimal: Move decimal two places left = \( 0.06 \)
To find Fraction: \( \frac{6}{100} \) which simplifies to \( \frac{3}{50} \)

Row 3: Given Decimal \( 1.7 \)
To find Fraction: \( \frac{17}{10} \) or \( 1 \frac{7}{10} \)
To find Percent: Move decimal two places right = \( 170\% \)

Completed Table Values:
  • 1. Decimal: 0.8, Percent: 80%
  • 2. Fraction: 3/50 (or 6/100), Decimal: 0.06
  • 3. Fraction: 17/10 (or 1 7/10), Percent: 170%
