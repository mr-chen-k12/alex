Lesson Practice

Lesson Practice a
In $217,534,896,000,000$, which digit is in the ten-billions place?
Final Answer: 3
Breaking the number down by periods from right to left:
  • Ones period: $000$
  • Thousands period: $000$
  • Millions period: $896$
  • Billions period: $534$ (4 is billions, 3 is ten-billions, 5 is hundred-billions)
  • Trillions period: $217$
Therefore, the digit in the ten-billions place is 3.
Lesson Practice b
In $9,876,543,210,000$, what is the place value of the digit 6?
Final Answer: Billions (or 6 Billion)
In $9,876,543,210,000$, $876$ is in the billions period. The digit $6$ is the rightmost digit in that period, making its place value the billions place ($6,000,000,000$).
Lesson Practice c
Write $2500$ in expanded notation.
Final Answer: $(2 \times 1000) + (5 \times 100)$
Expanded notation expresses a number as the sum of each non-zero digit multiplied by its place value: $$\text{Expanded Notation} = (2 \times 1000) + (5 \times 100)$$

Lesson Practice f (EXPLAIN)
How do we know where to place commas when writing the numbers in d and e as words?
Final Answer: Commas are placed in the word form where they appear between periods in digit form.
We place commas in word form to separate periods (millions, thousands, ones), exactly matching the location of commas in standard digit form.

Lesson Practice i

Final Answer: $\$15,000,000,000$

By combining digits with the word "million", we can write $\$15\text{ million}$, which avoids writing long strings of zeros.

Written Practice 7

Negative numbers are smallest (with smaller numbers further left on the number line), followed by $0$, then positive numbers in ascending order.
Written Practice 8 (MODEL)
Show this subtraction problem on a number line: $5 - 4$.
Final Answer: Result = 1
Number Line Steps:
  1. Start at $0$.
  2. Move $5$ units to the right to land on $+5$.
  3. Move $4$ units to the left (subtract $4$) to land on $+1$.
0 --(+5)--> 5 --(-4)--> 1
Written Practice 9
The rule of a certain sequence is $k = 3n$. Find the first four terms of the sequence.
Final Answer: $3, 6, 9, 12$
Substitute $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$ into $k = 3n$:
  • For $n = 1$: $k = 3(1) = 3$
  • For $n = 2$: $k = 3(2) = 6$
  • For $n = 3$: $k = 3(3) = 9$
  • For $n = 4$: $k = 3(4) = 12$
Written Practice 10
Find the value of $n$: $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot n = 960$
Final Answer: $n = 32$
  1. Multiply known factors: $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30$.
  2. Equation becomes: $30n = 960$.
  3. Divide both sides by $30$: $n = 960 \div 30 = 32$.

Written Practice 27
Which property can we use to find each unknown number?
a. $365n = 365$
b. $52 \cdot 7 = 7m$
a. Identity Property of Multiplication
b. Commutative Property of Multiplication
  • a. $365 \times 1 = 365$. The Identity Property of Multiplication states that multiplying any number by 1 yields that number ($n = 1$).
  • b. $52 \cdot 7 = 7 \cdot 52$. The Commutative Property of Multiplication states that changing the order of factors does not change the product ($m = 52$).
Written Practice 28 (GENERALIZE)
Use words to describe the rule of the following sequence. Then find the next three terms of the sequence: $$\dots, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, \dots$$
Rule: Subtract 2 from each term (or count down by 2s).
Next three terms: $0, -2, -4$
Each term decreases by $2$:
  • $2 - 2 = 0$
  • $0 - 2 = -2$
  • $-2 - 2 = -4$
