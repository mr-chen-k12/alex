Written Practice (Problems 1–30)

*1. Visitor Count
As the day of the festival drew near, there were 200,000 people in the city. If the usual population of the city was 85,000, how many visitors had come to the city?

Subtract the usual population from the total festival population:

\[\text{Visitors} = 200{,}000 - 85{,}000 = 115{,}000\]

Answer: \(115{,}000\text{ visitors}\)

*2. Money Spent
Syd returned from the store with $12.47. He had spent $98.03 on groceries. How much money did he have when he went to the store?

Add the remaining money to the spent money to calculate the original total:

\[\text{Initial Amount} = \$12.47 + \$98.03 = \$110.50\]

Answer: \(\$110.50\)

*3. Marathon Finishers
Exactly 17,926 runners began the 2004 Boston Marathon. If only 16,733 runners finished the marathon, how many dropped out along the way?

Subtract the finishers from total starters:

\[\text{Dropped Out} = 17{,}926 - 16{,}733 = 1{,}193\]

Answer: \(1{,}193\text{ runners}\)

*4. Circle Fractions
Refer to the circle diagram (8 total smaller circles, 7 shaded, 1 unshaded):
  1. What fraction of the group is shaded?
  2. What fraction of the group is not shaded?
  3. What percent of the group is not shaded?

a. \(7\) out of \(8\) circles are shaded \(\implies \frac{7}{8}\)

b. \(1\) out of \(8\) circles is unshaded \(\implies \frac{1}{8}\)

c. Convert \(\frac{1}{8}\) to percent:

\[\frac{1}{8} \times 100\% = 12.5\%\quad \text{or}\quad 12\frac{1}{2}\%\]
*5. Ordering & Integers
  1. Arrange these numbers in order from least to greatest: \(\frac{1}{2}, 0, -2, 1\)
  2. Which of these numbers is not an integer?

a. Least to greatest: \(-2, 0, \frac{1}{2}, 1\)

b. Non-integer: \(\frac{1}{2}\) (integers are whole numbers and their negatives)

*6. Cutting Ribbon
A 35-inch ribbon was cut into 8 equal lengths. How long was each piece? Describe how the ribbon could be cut into eighths.

Divide total length by pieces:

\[\text{Length per piece} = \frac{35}{8} = 4\frac{3}{8}\text{ inches} \quad (\text{or } 4.375\text{ in.})\]

Cutting Method: Fold the ribbon in half three consecutive times to divide it into eight equal segments, then cut along the folds.

*10. Word Problem Equation

Word Problem: Maya went to the store with $20.00. After purchasing a book for \(k\) dollars, she had $12.50 left. How much did the book cost?

Solution:

\[k = \$20.00 - \$12.50 = \$7.50\]

*27. Sequence Pattern
What are the next four numbers in this sequence? \[\frac{1}{8}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{8}, \frac{1}{2}, \dots\]

Express with common denominator of 8: \(\frac{1}{8}, \frac{2}{8}, \frac{3}{8}, \frac{4}{8}, \dots\)

Adding \(\frac{1}{8}\) each step gives:

\[\frac{5}{8}, \quad \frac{6}{8}=\frac{3}{4}, \quad \frac{7}{8}, \quad \frac{8}{8}=1\]

*29. Line Segment Subtraction
Use an inch ruler to draw \(\overline{AC}\) \(3\frac{1}{2}\) inches long. On \(\overline{AC}\) mark point \(B\) so that \(AB\) is \(1\frac{7}{8}\) inches. Now find \(BC\).
\[BC = 3\frac{1}{2} - 1\frac{7}{8} = 3\frac{4}{8} - 1\frac{7}{8} = 2\frac{12}{8} - 1\frac{7}{8} = 1\frac{5}{8}\text{ inches}\]
*30. Reciprocal Equations
If \(n \div m\) equals \(\frac{7}{8}\), what does \(m \div n\) equal?

Taking the reciprocal of \(\frac{n}{m} = \frac{7}{8}\):

\[m \div n = \frac{m}{n} = \frac{8}{7} = 1\frac{1}{7}\]

Specialized Extra Problems

E1. Percentage Discount Calculation
A winter coat originally priced at $80.00 is on sale for 25% off.
  1. What is the dollar amount of the discount?
  2. What is the final sale price before tax?

a. Discount amount:

\[25\% \text{ of } \$80.00 = 0.25 \times 80 = \$20.00\]

b. Final sale price:

\[\$80.00 - \$20.00 = \$60.00\]
E2. Terminating Decimal Division
Divide 7 by 16 using long division. Express the exact quotient as a terminating decimal.

Perform decimal long division:

\[7 \div 16 = \frac{7}{16} = 0.4375\]

Since the remainder reaches zero after four decimal places, \(0.4375\) is a terminating decimal.

E3. Infinite Repeating Decimal Division
Divide 5 by 11 using long division. Express the quotient using repeating decimal notation (vinculum bar).

Dividing \(5.000\dots\) by \(11\):

\[5 \div 11 = 0.454545\dots = 0.\overline{45}\]

The digits \(45\) repeat infinitely because the remainders alternate continuously between \(6\) and \(5\).

E4. Order of Operations (Mixed Numbers & Decimals)
Evaluate the expression following PEMDAS rules: \[\left(\frac{3}{4} + 0.5\right) \times 1.2 - \frac{2}{5}\]

Step 1 (Parentheses): Convert fraction to decimal and add inside brackets:

\[\frac{3}{4} = 0.75 \implies (0.75 + 0.5) = 1.25\]

Step 2 (Multiplication):

\[1.25 \times 1.2 = 1.5\]

Step 3 (Subtraction): Convert \(\frac{2}{5} = 0.4\):

\[1.5 - 0.4 = 1.1 \quad \left(\text{or } 1\frac{1}{10} = \frac{11}{10}\right)\]

Real-World Connection & Motivating Mindset

Real-World Connection: Mrs. Chen's Pencils
Mrs. Chen purchased 30 packets of #2 pencils. Each packet contains 12 pencils. She has 50 students and wants to share the pencils equally among the students.
  1. How many pencils should each student get? Show your work.
  2. Will there be any pencils left over? If so, how many?
  3. Express the relationship of the pencils left over to the total number of pencils as a fraction in simplest form.

Total Pencils = \(30 \times 12 = 360\)

a. \(360 \div 50 = 7\) remainder \(10 \implies\) Each student receives 7 pencils.

b. Yes, 10 pencils are left over.

c. Fraction leftover: \(\frac{10}{360} = \frac{1}{36}\)
