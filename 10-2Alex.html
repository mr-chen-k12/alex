Written Practice — Problems 1–4

1. (14)

Two thirds of the students wore green on St. Patrick's Day. What fraction of the students did not wear green on St. Patrick's Day?

Answer: \(\frac{1}{3}\).

Solution: The whole class is 1 (or \(\frac{3}{3}\)). Subtract the fraction that wore green: \(1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{3} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}\).

2. (13)

Three hundred forty-three quills were carefully placed into 7 compartments. If each compartment held the same number of quills, how many quills were in each compartment?

Answer: \(49\) quills.

Solution: Divide the quills equally: \(343 \div 7 = 49\). Check: \(7 \times 49 = 343\) ✓

3. (19, 20)

Choose the formula for the perimeter of a rectangle.

A \(P = 2L + 2W\)    B \(P = 4s\)    C \(A = LW\)    D \(A = s2\)

Answer: A.

Solution: The perimeter of a rectangle is twice the length plus twice the width: \(P = 2L + 2W\). Choices B is the perimeter of a square, and C and D are area formulas.

4. (20)

Write a squaring fact and a square root fact illustrated by this square.

Answer: Squaring fact: \(5^2 = 25\). Square root fact: \(\sqrt{25} = 5\).

Explanation: The square is 5 units by 5 units and contains \(5 \times 5 = 25\) small squares, so \(5^2 = 25\) and \(\sqrt{25} = 5\).

Written Practice — Problems 5–8

5. (15)

Write each number as a reduced fraction or mixed number:

a. \(3\frac{12}{21}\)    b. \(\frac{12}{48}\)    c. \(12\%\)

Answer: a. \(3\frac{4}{7}\)    b. \(\frac{1}{4}\)    c. \(\frac{3}{25}\).

Solution: a. Reduce \(\frac{12}{21}\) by dividing by 3: \(\frac{4}{7}\), so \(3\frac{4}{7}\). b. \(\frac{12}{48} = \frac{1}{4}\) (divide by 12). c. \(12\% = \frac{12}{100} = \frac{3}{25}\) (divide by 4).

*6. (21)

List the prime numbers between 50 and 60.

Answer: \(53, 59\).

Solution: Check each number from 51 to 59: 51 (÷3), 52 (÷2), 53 (prime), 54 (÷2), 55 (÷5), 56 (÷2), 57 (÷3), 58 (÷2), 59 (prime). The primes are 53 and 59.

*7. (21)

Write the prime factorization of each number:

a. 50    b. 60    c. 300

Answer: a. \(2 \times 5^2\)    b. \(2^2 \times 3 \times 5\)    c. \(2^2 \times 3 \times 5^2\).

Solution: a. \(50 = 2 \times 25 = 2 \times 5^2\). b. \(60 = 6 \times 10 = 2 \times 3 \times 2 \times 5 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5\). c. \(300 = 3 \times 100 = 3 \times 2^2 \times 5^2 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5^2\).

8. (4) JUSTIFY

Which point could represent 1610 on this number line? How did you decide?

Answer: Point \(C\).

Solution: 1610 is between 1000 and 2000, closer to 2000 than to 1000, and just past the middle (1500). Point \(C\) is the marked point nearest to where 1610 would fall on the scale.

Written Practice — Problems 9–12

9. (15)

Complete each equivalent fraction:

a. \(\frac{2}{3} = \frac{?}{15}\)    b. \(\frac{3}{5} = \frac{?}{15}\)    c. \(\frac{?}{3} = \frac{8}{12}\)

d. What property of multiplication do we use to rename fractions?

Answer: a. \(\frac{10}{15}\)    b. \(\frac{9}{15}\)    c. \(\frac{2}{3}\)    d. The identity property of multiplication.

Solution: a. Multiply by \(\frac{5}{5}\): \(\frac{2 \times 5}{3 \times 5} = \frac{10}{15}\). b. Multiply by \(\frac{3}{3}\): \(\frac{3 \times 3}{5 \times 3} = \frac{9}{15}\). c. \(\frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}\) (divide by 4). d. We multiply by 1 (in the form \(\frac{n}{n}\)), so the value is unchanged — the identity property.

10. (9)

a. How many \(\frac{1}{3}\)s are in 1?

b. How many \(\frac{1}{3}\)s are in 3?

Answer: a. \(3\)    b. \(9\).

Solution: a. \(1 \div \frac{1}{3} = 3\). b. \(3 \div \frac{1}{3} = 3 \times 3 = 9\).

*11. (20)

The perimeter of a regular quadrilateral is 12 inches. What is its area?

Answer: \(9\) in\(^2\).

Solution: A regular quadrilateral is a square. Each side \(= \frac{12}{4} = 3\) in. Area \(= 3^2 = 9\) square inches.

*12. (8, 19) REPRESENT

Use a ruler to draw a rectangle that is \(\frac{3}{4}\) in. wide and twice as long as it is wide.

a. How long is the rectangle?

b. What is the perimeter of the rectangle?

Answer: a. \(1\frac{1}{2}\) in.    b. \(4\frac{1}{2}\) in.

Solution: a. Length \(= 2 \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{4} = 1\frac{1}{2}\) in. b. Perimeter \(= 2\left(\frac{3}{4} + 1\frac{1}{2}\right) = 2\left(\frac{3}{4} + \frac{6}{4}\right) = 2 \times \frac{9}{4} = \frac{9}{2} = 4\frac{1}{2}\) in.

Written Practice — Problems 13–14

*13. (19)

Find the perimeter of this hexagon:

Answer: \(46\) in.

Solution: The 6 sides: top \(15\), right side \(5\), inner step \(12\), inner down \(3\), bottom-left \(3\), left side \(8\). (The top is \(12 + 3 = 15\) in.) Adding: \(15 + 5 + 12 + 3 + 3 + 8 = 46\) in. — the same as the \(15 \times 8\) bounding rectangle's perimeter.

14. (14)

A number cube is rolled once. What is the probability of getting an odd number greater than 5?

Answer: \(0\).

Solution: The faces are 1–6. The odd faces are 1, 3, and 5 — none is greater than 5, so there are 0 favorable outcomes: \(\frac{0}{6} = 0\). It is an impossible event.

Solution: \(p = 1 - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{5}{5} - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}\). Check: \(\frac{2}{5} + \frac{3}{5} = 1\) ✓

\(\frac{3}{5}q = 1\)

Answer: \(q = 1\frac{2}{3}\).

Solution: \(q = 1 \div \frac{3}{5} = \frac{5}{3} = 1\frac{2}{3}\). Check: \(\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{3} = 1\) ✓

17. (3)

\(\frac{w}{25} = 50\)

Answer: \(w = 1250\).

Solution: \(w = 50 \times 25 = 1250\). Check: \(\frac{1250}{25} = 50\) ✓

18. (9, 15)

\(\frac{1}{6} + f = \frac{5}{6}\)

Answer: \(f = \frac{2}{3}\).

Solution: \(f = \frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}\). Check: \(\frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{4}{6} = \frac{5}{6}\) ✓

19. (10)

\(m - 3\frac{2}{3} = 1\frac{2}{3}\)

Answer: \(m = 5\frac{1}{3}\).

Solution: \(m = 1\frac{2}{3} + 3\frac{2}{3} = 4\frac{4}{3} = 5\frac{1}{3}\). Check: \(5\frac{1}{3} - 3\frac{2}{3} = 4\frac{4}{3} - 3\frac{2}{3} = 1\frac{2}{3}\) ✓

Written Practice — Problems 23–26

*23. (21)

a. Write the prime factorization of 225.

b. GENERALIZE Find \(\sqrt{225}\) and write its prime factorization.

Answer: a. \(3^2 \times 5^2\)    b. \(\sqrt{225} = 15 = 3 \times 5\).

Solution: a. \(225 = 9 \times 25 = 3^2 \times 5^2\). b. \(\sqrt{225} = 15\), and \(15 = 3 \times 5\).

24. (15)

Describe how finding the greatest common factor of the numerator and denominator of a fraction can help reduce the fraction.

Answer: Divide the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.

Solution: Sample explanation: Dividing the numerator and denominator by their GCF is the same as dividing the fraction by \(\frac{\text{GCF}}{\text{GCF}} = 1\), so the value doesn't change — but the fraction is reduced to lowest terms in one step.

25. (17)

Draw \(\overline{AB}\) \(2\frac{1}{2}\) inches long. Then draw \(\overline{BC}\) \(2\frac{1}{2}\) inches long perpendicular to \(\overline{AB}\). Complete the triangle by drawing \(\overline{AC}\). Use a protractor to find the measure of \(\angle A\).

Answer: \(\angle A = 45^\circ\).

Solution: The two legs are equal (both \(2\frac{1}{2}\) in.) and meet at a right angle at \(B\), so the triangle is an isosceles right triangle. Its two acute angles are equal and sum to \(90^\circ\), so \(\angle A = 45^\circ\).

26. (9, 10)

Write \(1\frac{3}{4}\) as an improper fraction. Multiply the improper fraction by the reciprocal of \(\frac{2}{3}\). Then write the product as a mixed number.

Answer: \(2\frac{5}{8}\).

Solution: \(1\frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{4}\). The reciprocal of \(\frac{2}{3}\) is \(\frac{3}{2}\). Multiply: \(\frac{7}{4} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{21}{8} = 2\frac{5}{8}\).

Written Practice — Problems 27–30

*27. (Inv. 2) CLASSIFY

Refer to the circle at right with center at point \(M\) to answer a–d.

a. Which segment is a diameter?

b. Which segment is a chord but not a diameter?

c. Which two segments are radii?

d. Which angle is an inscribed angle?

Answer: a. \(\overline{CB}\)    b. \(\overline{AB}\)    c. \(\overline{MA}\) and \(\overline{MB}\)    d. \(\angle CAB\).

Solution: a. \(\overline{CB}\) passes through the center \(M\) and has both endpoints on the circle — a diameter. b. \(\overline{AB}\) has endpoints on the circle but does not pass through the center — a chord, not a diameter. c. \(\overline{MA}\) and \(\overline{MB}\) each join the center to the circle — radii (\(\overline{MC}\) is one too). d. \(\angle CAB\) has its vertex on the circle with sides that are chords — an inscribed angle.

28. (16)

Alicia's father asked her to buy a gallon of milk at the store. The store had milk only in quart-sized containers. What percent of a gallon is a quart? How many quart containers did Alicia have to buy?

Answer: A quart is \(25\%\) of a gallon; she had to buy \(4\) quart containers.

Solution: There are 4 quarts in a gallon, so one quart \(= \frac{1}{4}\) of a gallon \(= 25\%\). To make a full gallon she needs \(4\) quarts.

29. (2)

a. Compare: \(a + b \bigcirc b + a\)

b. What property of operations applies to part a of this problem?

Answer: a. \(a + b = b + a\)    b. The commutative property of addition.

Solution: The order of addends does not change the sum — the commutative property of addition.

*30. (18) ANALYZE

Refer to the triangles below to answer a–c.

a. Which triangle appears to be congruent to \(\triangle ABC\)?

b. Which triangle is not similar to \(\triangle ABC\)?

c. Which angle in \(\triangle QRS\) corresponds to \(\angle A\) in \(\triangle ABC\)?

Answer: a. \(\triangle QRS\)    b. \(\triangle DEF\)    c. \(\angle S\).

Solution: a. \(\triangle QRS\) is the same size and shape as \(\triangle ABC\) — congruent. b. \(\triangle DEF\) is obtuse and a different shape, so it is not similar to the right triangle \(\triangle ABC\) (\(\triangle JKL\) is similar but smaller). c. \(\angle S\) sits at the top of the short leg, matching the position of \(\angle A\), so \(\angle S\) corresponds to \(\angle A\).
