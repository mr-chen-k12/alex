Practice i
Point \(M\) is located at \(5\frac{1}{2}\text{ inches}\) and Point \(N\) is located at \(9\frac{3}{4}\text{ inches}\) on a ruler. Find the distance \(MN\).
Final Answer
\(MN = 4\frac{1}{4}\text{ inches}\)

Subtract the smaller coordinate from the larger coordinate:

$$9\frac{3}{4} - 5\frac{1}{2} = 9\frac{3}{4} - 5\frac{2}{4} = 4\frac{1}{4}\text{ inches}$$

Final Answer
\(\frac{1}{64}\text{ inch}\)

The maximum measurement error is half of the smallest unit mark on the ruler scale:

$$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{32} = \frac{1}{64}\text{ inch}$$

Problem 2
Point \(R\) is at \(1\frac{3}{4}\text{ in.}\) and point \(S\) is at \(6\frac{1}{8}\text{ in.}\) on a ruler. Find length \(RS\).
Final Answer
\(4\frac{3}{8}\text{ inches}\)

Subtract position \(R\) from position \(S\):

$$6\frac{1}{8} - 1\frac{3}{4} = 6\frac{1}{8} - 1\frac{6}{8} = 5\frac{9}{8} - 1\frac{6}{8} = 4\frac{3}{8}\text{ in.}$$

Problem 3
What is the quotient when the product of 40 and 40 is divided by the sum of 20 and 20?
Final Answer
\(40\)

Step 1 (Product): \(40 \times 40 = 1600\)

Step 2 (Sum): \(20 + 20 = 40\)

Step 3 (Quotient): \(\frac{1600}{40} = 40\)

Problem 4 ANALYZE
List all single-digit divisors of 3600.
Final Answer
\(1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9\)

Check digits 1 through 9:

• Divisible by 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, and 9 (since sum of digits \(3+6+0+0 = 9\)).

• Check 7: \(3600 \div 7 = 514.28\dots\) (Not divisible by 7).

Therefore, all single digits except 7 are divisors of 3600.

Problem 6b ANALYZE
What arithmetic properties are illustrated by the expression \(6 + 0 = 6 \times 1\)?
Final Answer

• Adding zero to a number leaves it unchanged (Identity Property of Addition).

• Multiplying a number by one leaves it unchanged (Identity Property of Multiplication).

Problem 8a

Final Answer

Problem 17 JUSTIFY
Provide reasons for steps (a) and (b) in solving \(12 + (p + 3) = 30\): $$\begin{align*} 12 + (p + 3) &= 30 && \text{Given equation} \\ 12 + (3 + p) &= 30 && \text{a. }\underline{\quad\quad\quad\quad} \\ (12 + 3) + p &= 30 && \text{b. }\underline{\quad\quad\quad\quad} \end{align*}$$
Final Answer

a. Commutative property of addition

b. Associative property of addition

• Step a reorders \(p + 3\) to \(3 + p\) (Commutative Property).

• Step b changes grouping from \(12 + (3 + p)\) to \((12 + 3) + p\) (Associative Property).

Problem 26a
The sequence \(3, 8, 13, 18, \dots\) follows the rule \(k = 5n - 2\). Find the tenth term of this sequence.
Final Answer
\(48\)

Substitute \(n = 10\) into the formula \(k = 5n - 2\):

$$k = 5(10) - 2 = 50 - 2 = 48$$

Problem 26b ANALYZE
What pattern do you recognize in this sequence?
Final Answer
Each term is 5 greater than the preceding term (adds 5 each step).

Difference between consecutive terms: \(8 - 3 = 5\), \(13 - 8 = 5\), \(18 - 13 = 5\).

Problem 27
Compare: \(500 - (150 + 50) \bigcirc (500 - 150) + 50\)
Final Answer
\(<\)

Left side: \(500 - (150 + 50) = 500 - 200 = 300\)

Right side: \((500 - 150) + 50 = 350 + 50 = 400\)

Since \(300 < 400\), the comparison symbol is \(<\).

Problem 28
Rhombus \(JKLM\) has two opposite acute angles (\(\angle J\) and \(\angle L\)) and two opposite obtuse angles (\(\angle K\) and \(\angle M\)). Identify which angles are acute and which are obtuse.
Final Answer

Acute angles: \(\angle J\) and \(\angle L\)

Obtuse angles: \(\angle K\) and \(\angle M\)

• Angles measuring less than \(90^\circ\) are acute (\(\angle J, \angle L\)).

• Angles measuring between \(90^\circ\) and \(180^\circ\) are obtuse (\(\angle K, \angle M\)).

Problem 29
Refer to rectangle \(ABCD\):
a. Name a segment parallel to \(\overline{AB}\).
b. Name a segment perpendicular to \(\overline{AB}\).
Final Answer

a. \(\overline{DC}\) (or \(\overline{CD}\))

b. \(\overline{AD}\) (or \(\overline{BC}\))

a. Opposite sides of a rectangle are parallel (\(\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{DC}\)).

b. Adjacent sides meet at right angles (\(90^\circ\)), making \(\overline{AD}\) and \(\overline{BC}\) perpendicular to \(\overline{AB}\).

Problem 30 EXPLAIN
Explain the difference in mathematical meaning between the notations \(\overleftrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overline{AB}\).
Final Answer
\(\overleftrightarrow{AB}\) represents a line extending infinitely in both directions, whereas \(\overline{AB}\) represents a line segment bounded by endpoints \(A\) and \(B\).

• \(\overleftrightarrow{AB}\) has arrows on both ends indicating endless continuation in two directions.

• \(\overline{AB}\) has no arrows and represents only the section of the line connecting endpoints \(A\) and \(B\).
