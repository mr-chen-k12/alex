Lesson Practice

Problem a
Only $39\%$ of the lights were on. What percent of the lights were off? Include the equation you used to solve the problem.
Answer: $61\%$ (Equation: $39\% + p = 100\%$ or $100\% - 39\% = p$)
The whole set of lights represents $100\%$. $$\text{Equation: } 39\% + p = 100\%$$ $$p = 100\% - 39\% = 61\%$$
Problem b
Two fifths of the students did not go to the museum. What fraction of the students did go to the museum? Include the equation you used to solve the problem.
Answer: $\frac{3}{5}$ (Equation: $\frac{2}{5} + f = 1$ or $1 - \frac{2}{5} = f$)
The total student body represents $1$ whole ($\frac{5}{5}$). $$\text{Equation: } \frac{2}{5} + f = 1$$ $$f = \frac{5}{5} - \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$$

Answer: Sample problem provided below ($g = 55\%$)

Problem d
Rolling a number cube once, what is the probability of rolling a number less than $4$?
Answer: $\frac{1}{2}$
A standard number cube has $6$ faces: $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.
Outcomes less than $4$ are $\{1, 2, 3\}$ ($3$ favorable outcomes). $$P(\text{less than } 4) = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$
Problem e
This spinner is divided into four equal sections labeled 1, 2, 3, and 4.
What is the probability of this spinner stopping on $3$?
Answer: $\frac{1}{4}$
There are $4$ equal sections in total, and $1$ section is labeled $3$. $$P(3) = \frac{1}{4}$$
Problem f
This spinner is divided into four equal sections labeled 1, 2, 3, and 4.
What is the probability of this spinner stopping on $5$?
Answer: $0$
Since $5$ is not on the spinner, there are $0$ favorable outcomes out of $4$. $$P(5) = \frac{0}{4} = 0$$
Problem g
This spinner is divided into four equal sections labeled 1, 2, 3, and 4.
What is the probability of this spinner stopping on a number less than $6$?
Answer: $1$
All $4$ numbers $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ are less than $6$. $$P(\text{less than } 6) = \frac{4}{4} = 1$$
Problem h
This spinner is divided into one half (C) and two fourths (A and B).
What is the probability of this spinner stopping on $A$?
Answer: $\frac{1}{4}$
Section $A$ represents one-fourth of the total circle area. $$P(A) = \frac{1}{4}$$
Problem i
This spinner is divided into one half (C) and two fourths (A and B).
What is the probability of this spinner not stopping on $B$?
Answer: $\frac{3}{4}$
The probability of stopping on $B$ is $\frac{1}{4}$. The probability of NOT stopping on $B$ is the complement: $$P(\text{not } B) = 1 - P(B) = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$$

Problem 1
The USDA recommends that adults eat at least $85\text{ grams}$ of whole grain products each day. Ryan ate $63\text{ grams}$ of whole-grain cereal. How many more grams of whole grain products should he eat?
Answer: $22\text{ grams}$
Subtract the amount eaten from the total recommended amount: $$85 - 63 = 22\text{ grams}$$
Problem 2
Seven tenths of the new recruits did not like their first haircut. What fraction of the new recruits did like their first haircut?
Answer: $\frac{3}{10}$
Subtract the fraction that did not like it from the whole ($1$ or $\frac{10}{10}$): $$\frac{10}{10} - \frac{7}{10} = \frac{3}{10}$$
Problem 3
The Declaration of Independence was signed in $1776$. The U.S. Constitution was ratified in $1789$. How many years passed between these two events?
Answer: $13\text{ years}$
Subtract the earlier year from the later year: $$1789 - 1776 = 13\text{ years}$$

Problem 5
In $2000$, nearly $18\%$ of cars sold in North America were silver. What percent of cars sold were not silver?
Answer: $82\%$
Subtract the percent of silver cars from $100\%$: $$100\% - 18\% = 82\%$$
Problem 6
MODEL Draw and shade circles to show that $3\frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$.
Answer: $4$ circles total: $3$ fully shaded circles and $1$ circle with $1$ of $3$ equal parts shaded.
Draw $4$ circles, each divided into $3$ equal thirds:
• $3$ whole circles fully shaded $= 3 \times 3 = 9$ thirds.
• $1$ circle with $1$ third shaded $= 1$ third.
Total shaded thirds $= 9 + 1 = 10$ thirds, which equals $\frac{10}{3}$.

Problem 10

Problem 16
Solve for $n$:
5 8 7 6 5 9 4 3 6 4 7 8 5 n + 6 --- 89
Answer: $n = 6$
Sum all the known numbers: $$5 + 8 + 7 + 6 + 5 + 9 + 4 + 3 + 6 + 4 + 7 + 8 + 5 + 6 = 83$$ Set up the equation with the total: $$83 + n = 89 \implies n = 89 - 83 = 6$$

Problem 18
Simplify: $$3\frac{3}{5} + 2\frac{4}{5}$$
Answer: $6\frac{2}{5}$
Add whole numbers and fractions separately: $$(3 + 2) + \left(\frac{3}{5} + \frac{4}{5}\right) = 5 + \frac{7}{5}$$ Convert $\frac{7}{5}$ to mixed number $1\frac{2}{5}$: $$5 + 1\frac{2}{5} = 6\frac{2}{5}$$

Problem 29
How many $\frac{1}{6}$s are in $1$?

Problem 30
Rolling a number cube once, what is the probability of rolling a number greater than $4$?
Answer: $\frac{1}{3}$
Possible outcomes on a 6-sided die: $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.
Outcomes greater than $4$: $\{5, 6\}$ ($2$ favorable outcomes). $$P(\text{greater than } 4) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$$
