Math Problem Solutions & Explanations

Formulate
Formulate Example
Give an example of a word problem in which you would need to find \(g\) in the equation \(n \times g = t\).
Sample Answer:
"There are \(n = 5\) gift bags, and a total of \(t = 30\) candies distributed equally among them. How many candies \(g\) are in each gift bag?"
Equation: \(5 \times g = 30 \implies g = 6\).
Generalize
Problem a
Beverly bought two dozen juice bars for \(32\text{¢}\) each. How much did she pay for all the juice bars?
Answer: \(768\text{¢}\) or \(\$7.68\)
Step 1 (Identify quantities): Two dozen = \(2 \times 12 = 24\) juice bars.
Step 2 (Equation): Let \(t\) be total cost. \(t = 24 \times 32\text{¢}\).
Step 3 (Calculate): \(24 \times 32 = 768\text{¢}\) or \(\$7.68\).
Problem b
Workers in the orchard planted \(375\) trees. There were \(25\) trees in each row they planted. How many rows of trees did the workers plant?
Answer: \(15\) rows
Step 1 (Equation): Let \(r\) be the number of rows. \(25 \times r = 375\).
Step 2 (Solve): Divide both sides by \(25\):
\[r = \frac{375}{25} = 15\]
Problem c
Every day Arnold did the same number of push-ups. If he did \(1225\) push-ups in one week, then how many push-ups did he do each day?
Answer: \(175\) push-ups per day
Step 1 (Identify quantities): \(1\) week = \(7\) days.
Step 2 (Equation): Let \(p\) be push-ups per day. \(7 \times p = 1225\).
Step 3 (Solve):
\[p = \frac{1225}{7} = 175\]
Problem d
Write a word problem about equal groups that fits this equation: \(12x = \$3.00\).
Sample Answer:
"A pack of \(12\) identical pencils costs \(\$3.00\). What is the price \(x\) of each pencil?"
Solution: \(x = \frac{\$3.00}{12} = \$0.25\) (or \(25\text{¢}\)).
Practice Problems
Problem 1
In 1990, the population of Alabaster, Alabama was \(14,619\). By 2002, the population had increased to \(24,877\). How much greater was the population of Alabaster in 2002?
Answer: \(10,258\) people
Subtract the 1990 population from the 2002 population: \[24,877 - 14,619 = 10,258\]
Problem 2
Write \(2\frac{1}{2}\) as an improper fraction. Then multiply the improper fraction by \(\frac{1}{3}\). What is the product?
Answer: Improper fraction = \(\frac{5}{2}\); Product = \(\frac{5}{6}\)
Step 1: Convert \(2\frac{1}{2}\) to an improper fraction: \(2\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2 \times 2 + 1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}\).
Step 2: Multiply by \(\frac{1}{3}\):
\[\frac{5}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5 \times 1}{2 \times 3} = \frac{5}{6}\]
Problem 3
President Franklin D. Roosevelt was \(50\) years old when he was elected in \(1932\). In what year was he born?
Answer: \(1882\)
Subtract his age at election from the election year: \[1932 - 50 = 1882\]
Problem 4
The beach balls were packed \(12\) in each case. If \(75\) cases were delivered, how many beach balls were there in all?
Answer: \(900\) beach balls
Multiply the number of cases by the number of beach balls per case: \[75 \times 12 = 900\]
Problem 5
One hundred twenty poles were needed to construct the new pier. If each truckload contained eight poles, how many truckloads were needed?
Answer: \(15\) truckloads
Divide total poles by poles per truckload: \[\frac{120}{8} = 15\]
Problem 6
Write a word problem about equal groups that fits this equation: \(5t = \$63.75\). Then answer the problem.
Answer: \(\$12.75\) per ticket
Word Problem: Five identical concert tickets cost a total of \(\$63.75\). What is the price \(t\) of one ticket?
Solution:
\[t = \frac{\$63.75}{5} = \$12.75\]
Problem 7
The product of \(5\) and \(8\) is how much greater than the sum of \(5\) and \(8\)?
Answer: \(27\)
Product: \(5 \times 8 = 40\)
Sum: \(5 + 8 = 13\)
Difference: \(40 - 13 = 27\)
Problem 8
a. Three quarters make up what fraction of a dollar?
b. Three quarters make up what percent of a dollar?
Answer: a. \(\frac{3}{4}\)  |  b. \(75\%\)
a. One quarter is \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a dollar, so three quarters is \(\frac{3}{4}\).
b. \(\frac{3}{4} = \frac{75}{100} = 75\%\).
Problem 9
How many units is it from \(-5\) to \(+5\) on the number line?
Answer: \(10\) units
Distance = \(|+5 - (-5)| = 5 + 5 = 10\) units.

Problem 11
a. What whole numbers are factors of both \(24\) and \(36\)?
b. What is the GCF of \(24\) and \(36\)?
Answer: a. \(1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\)  |  b. \(12\)
Factors of 24: \(1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24\)
Factors of 36: \(1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36\)
a. Common factors: \(1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\)
b. Greatest Common Factor (GCF): \(12\)
Problem 12
a. What fractions or mixed numbers are represented by points A and B on this number line?
b. Find \(AB\).
Answer: a. \(A = \frac{3}{4}\), \(B = 1\frac{1}{2}\)  |  b. \(AB = \frac{3}{4}\)
The interval between whole numbers is divided into \(4\) equal parts (quarters).
a. Point A is at \(3\) tick marks past \(0 \implies \frac{3}{4}\).
Point B is at \(2\) tick marks past \(1 \implies 1\frac{2}{4} = 1\frac{1}{2}\).
b. \(AB = B - A = 1\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{4} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{4}\).

Problem 23
Simplify: \(3\frac{4}{5} - \left(\frac{2}{5} + 1\frac{1}{5}\right)\)
Answer: \(2\frac{1}{5}\)
Inside parentheses: \(\frac{2}{5} + 1\frac{1}{5} = 1\frac{3}{5}\)
Subtract: \(3\frac{4}{5} - 1\frac{3}{5} = (3-1) + \left(\frac{4}{5}-\frac{3}{5}\right) = 2\frac{1}{5}\)

Problem 26
Compare: \(1000 \div (100 \div 10) \bigcirc (1000 \div 100) \div 10\)
Answer: \(>\)
Left side: \(1000 \div (100 \div 10) = 1000 \div 10 = 100\)
Right side: \((1000 \div 100) \div 10 = 10 \div 10 = 1\)
Since \(100 > 1\), the answer is \(>\).
Problem 27
EXPLAIN: Mr. Lim bought \(6\) dozen eggs to make sandwiches for the school picnic. He used all but \(17\) eggs. How many eggs did he use? Explain how you found the answer.
Answer: \(55\) eggs
Step 1: Calculate total eggs bought: \(6 \times 12 = 72\) eggs.
Step 2: "Used all but 17" means \(17\) eggs were left unused.
Step 3: Subtract remaining eggs from total: \(72 - 17 = 55\) eggs.
Problem 28
a. What is the product of \(\frac{11}{12}\) and its reciprocal?
b. What property is illustrated by the multiplication in part a?
Answer: a. \(1\)  |  b. Inverse Property of Multiplication
a. The reciprocal of \(\frac{11}{12}\) is \(\frac{12}{11}\). Product: \(\frac{11}{12} \times \frac{12}{11} = 1\).
b. This demonstrates the Inverse Property of Multiplication (or Multiplicative Inverse Property).

Real-World Connection
Real-World Connection (a & b)
A small bakery wants to purchase a new oven that costs \(\$2,530.00\) plus \(8.75\%\) sales tax.

a. How much will the oven cost including tax?
b. A four-year service agreement before taxes costs \(\$455.40\). If the bakery purchases a service agreement which is also taxed, what will be their total cost for the oven and service agreement?
Answer: a. \(\$2,751.38\)  |  b. \(\$3,246.62\)
Part a:
\(\text{Tax} = \$2530.00 \times 0.0875 = \$221.375 \approx \$221.38\)
\(\text{Total for oven} = \$2530.00 + \$221.375 = \$2,751.375 \approx \$2,751.38\)

Part b:
\(\text{Total pre-tax cost} = \$2530.00 + \$455.40 = \$2,985.40\)
\(\text{Total tax} = \$2985.40 \times 0.0875 = \$261.2225 \approx \$261.22\)
\(\text{Overall Total} = \$2985.40 + \$261.2225 = \$3,246.6225 \approx \$3,246.62\)
