Problem a
Draw four squares. Then draw a different line of symmetry for each square.
Answer & Visual:
Explanation:

A square has exactly 4 lines of symmetry: one vertical line down the middle, one horizontal line across the middle, and two diagonal lines spanning from corner to corner.

Problem b
All but one of these letters can be drawn to have a line of symmetry. Which of these letters does not have a line of symmetry?

A   B   C   D   E   F
Answer:

F

Explanation:

A, B, C, D, and E all have horizontal or vertical lines of symmetry (A is vertical; B, C, D, E are horizontal). The letter F cannot be folded over any line to match perfectly.

Problem c
Which two of these letters have rotational symmetry? (Hint: Rotating your book might help you find the answer.)

L   M   N   O   P   Q
Answer:

N and O

Explanation:

If you rotate the letter N by 180°, it looks exactly the same. The letter O (assuming it is drawn uniformly) looks the same at multiple degrees of rotation. The others change their appearance when rotated.

Written Practice

Problem 1
When the greatest four-digit number is divided by the greatest two-digit number, what is the quotient?
Answer:

101

Explanation:

The greatest four-digit number is 9999. The greatest two-digit number is 99.
$$9999 \div 99 = 101$$

Problem 2
The ratio of the length to the width of the Alamo is about 5 to 3. If the width of the Alamo is approximately 63 ft, about how long is the Alamo?
Answer:

105 ft

Explanation:

Set up a proportion: $$\frac{Length}{Width} = \frac{5}{3} = \frac{L}{63}$$ Cross-multiply or find the scale factor: \(3 \times 21 = 63\), so \(5 \times 21 = 105\).

Problem 3
A box of crackers in the shape of a square prism had a length, width, and height of 4 inches, 4 inches, and 10 inches respectively. How many cubic inches was the volume of the box?
Answer:

160 cubic inches

Explanation:

Volume of a rectangular/square prism is \(Length \times Width \times Height\).
$$V = 4 \times 4 \times 10 = 16 \times 10 = 160 \text{ in}^3$$

Problem 4
A full turn is 360°. How many degrees is \(\frac{1}{6}\) of a turn?
Answer:

60°

Explanation:

Multiply the full turn by the fraction: $$360^\circ \times \frac{1}{6} = 60^\circ$$

Problem 5
Does the equilateral triangle have rotational and line symmetry? Use words or diagrams to explain how you know.
Answer:

Yes, it has both.

Explanation:

Line Symmetry: It has 3 lines of symmetry (one drawn from each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side).
Rotational Symmetry: Because all sides and angles are equal, rotating the triangle by 120° or 240° around its center maps it perfectly onto itself.

Problem 6
Sketch a triangle similar to the equilateral triangle. Make the scale factor from the equilateral triangle to your sketch 3. What is the perimeter of the triangle you sketched?
Answer:

18 cm

Explanation:

The original equilateral triangle has side lengths of 2 cm. With a scale factor of 3, the new side length is \(2 \text{ cm} \times 3 = 6 \text{ cm}\).
The perimeter of the new triangle is \(6 + 6 + 6 = 18 \text{ cm}\).

Problem 7
What is the area of the right triangle? (Legs are 3 cm and 4 cm, Hypotenuse is 5 cm).
Answer:

6 cm²

Explanation:

Area of a triangle is \(\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\). Using the legs as the base and height:
$$Area = \frac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 4 = \frac{1}{2} \times 12 = 6 \text{ cm}^2$$

Problem 8
Draw a ratio box for this problem. Then solve the problem using a proportion.

Ms. Mendez is sorting her photographs. She notes that 12 of the photos are black and white and that 40% of the photos are color. How many photos does she have?
Answer:

20 photos

Explanation:

If 40% are color, then 60% are black and white (100% - 40%). We can set up a proportion: $$\frac{\text{Percent}}{\text{Count}} \Rightarrow \frac{60}{12} = \frac{100}{x}$$ Cross-multiplying gives: \(60x = 1200\), so \(x = 20\).
She has a total of 20 photos.

Problem 9
Convert the fraction \(2 \frac{3}{4}\) to a decimal (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. 2.75    b. 275%

Explanation:

\(\frac{3}{4}\) is equivalent to 0.75, so \(2 \frac{3}{4}\) = 2.75. To find the percent, multiply the decimal by 100 to get 275%.

Problem 10
Convert the decimal 1.1 to a fraction (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. \(1 \frac{1}{10}\)    b. 110%

Explanation:

1.1 is "one and one-tenth", which is \(1 \frac{1}{10}\). Multiply 1.1 by 100 to find the percent: 110%.

Problem 11
Convert 64% to a fraction (a) and a decimal (b).
Answer:

a. \(\frac{16}{25}\)    b. 0.64

Explanation:

64% as a decimal is 0.64. As a fraction, it is \(\frac{64}{100}\), which reduces by dividing numerator and denominator by 4 to get \(\frac{16}{25}\).

Problem 12
$$24 \frac{1}{6} + 23 \frac{1}{3} + 22 \frac{1}{2}$$
Answer:

70

Explanation:

Find a common denominator for the fractions, which is 6: $$24 \frac{1}{6} + 23 \frac{2}{6} + 22 \frac{3}{6}$$ Add the whole numbers: \(24 + 23 + 22 = 69\).
Add the fractions: \(\frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{6}{6} = 1\).
Total: \(69 + 1 = 70\).

Problem 13
$$(1 \frac{1}{5} \div 2) \div 1 \frac{2}{3}$$
Answer:

\(\frac{9}{25}\)

Explanation:

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions: $$(\frac{6}{5} \div \frac{2}{1}) \div \frac{5}{3}$$ Follow PEMDAS and do the parentheses first (multiply by reciprocal): $$(\frac{6}{5} \times \frac{1}{2}) \div \frac{5}{3} = \frac{3}{5} \div \frac{5}{3}$$ Now, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction: $$\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{25}$$

Problem 14
$$9 - (6.2 + 2.79)$$
Answer:

0.01

Explanation:

First, add the numbers in parentheses: \(6.2 + 2.79 = 8.99\).
Subtract from 9: \(9.00 - 8.99 = 0.01\).

Problem 16
Find 6.5% of $24.89 by multiplying 0.065 by $24.89. Round the product to the nearest cent.
Answer:

$1.62

Explanation:

$$0.065 \times 24.89 = 1.61785$$
To round to the nearest cent (hundredths place), look at the third decimal digit (7). Since it is 5 or greater, round up the 1 to a 2. Thus, $1.62.

Problem 17
Round the quotient to the nearest thousandth: $$0.065 \div 4$$
Answer:

0.016

Explanation:

Divide: $$0.065 \div 4 = 0.01625$$ The thousandths place is 6. The next digit is 2, which is less than 5, so keep the 6. The rounded answer is 0.016.

Problem 18
Write the prime factorization of 1000 using exponents.
Answer:

\(2^3 \times 5^3\)

Explanation:

\(1000 = 10 \times 10 \times 10\).
Since \(10 = 2 \times 5\), we have \((2 \times 5) \times (2 \times 5) \times (2 \times 5) = 2^3 \times 5^3\).

Problem 19
"All squares are similar." True or false?
Answer:

True

Explanation:

For polygons to be similar, their corresponding angles must be equal and the ratio of their corresponding side lengths must be consistent. Since all squares have four 90° angles and all sides on a given square are equal, any two squares will always scale perfectly to one another.

Problem 20
$$3^3 - 3^2 \div 3 - 3 \times 3$$
Answer:

15

Explanation:

Follow the order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS):
Exponents first: \(27 - 9 \div 3 - 3 \times 3\)
Multiplication and Division: \(27 - 3 - 9\)
Addition and Subtraction (left to right): \(24 - 9 = 15\).

Problem 21
What is the perimeter of this polygon? (The L-shaped polygon with top = 12 m, left = 8 m, right = 10 m, bottom-inner horizontal = 5 m).
Answer:

44 m

Explanation:

To find the perimeter, add all the outer boundaries.
Top edge = 12 m
Right edge = 10 m
Left edge = 8 m
Bottom-most horizontal edge = 5 m
The missing vertical "step-up" segment is \(10 \text{ m} - 8 \text{ m} = 2 \text{ m}\).
The missing horizontal segment going left is \(12 \text{ m} - 5 \text{ m} = 7 \text{ m}\).
$$Perimeter = 12 + 10 + 5 + 2 + 7 + 8 = 44 \text{ m}$$

Problem 22
What is the area of this polygon?
Answer:

106 m²

Explanation:

Split the polygon into two rectangles.
Rectangle 1 (left side): Width = 7 m (from \(12 - 5\)), Height = 8 m. Area = \(7 \times 8 = 56 \text{ m}^2\).
Rectangle 2 (right side): Width = 5 m, Height = 10 m. Area = \(5 \times 10 = 50 \text{ m}^2\).
Total Area = \(56 + 50 = 106 \text{ m}^2\).

Problem 23
Triangles I and II are congruent. Name the transformations that would position triangle I on triangle II.
Answer:

Translation and Rotation

Explanation:

Triangle I is resting on a horizontal leg. Triangle II is positioned further to the right and resting on its hypotenuse. To match them, you must translate (slide) Triangle I over to Triangle II's position, and rotate (turn) it so its orientation aligns properly.

Problem 24
The perimeter of each triangle is 24 cm. What is the length of the shortest side of each triangle?
Answer:

6 cm

Explanation:

The image shows the hypotenuse is 10 cm and one leg is 8 cm. Since the total perimeter is 24 cm, the missing leg \(x\) is:
$$x + 8 + 10 = 24$$ $$x + 18 = 24$$ $$x = 6 \text{ cm}$$
The sides are 6 cm, 8 cm, and 10 cm. The shortest side is 6 cm.

Problem 25
The first Ferris wheel was built in 1893 for the world's fair in Chicago. The diameter of the Ferris wheel was 250 ft. Find the circumference of the original Ferris wheel to the nearest hundred feet.
Answer:

800 ft

Explanation:

Circumference formula is \(C = \pi \times d\). Using 3.14 for \(\pi\):
$$C \approx 3.14 \times 250 = 785 \text{ ft}$$
The question asks to round to the nearest hundred feet. 785 rounded to the nearest hundred is 800 ft.

Problem 26
Use a ruler to draw \(\overline{AB}\) \(1 \frac{3}{4}\) inches long. Then draw a dot at the midpoint of \(AB\), and label the point \(M\). How long is \(\overline{AM}\)?
Answer:

\(\frac{7}{8}\) inches

Explanation:

The midpoint divides the segment exactly in half. Divide \(1 \frac{3}{4}\) by 2: $$1 \frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{4}$$ $$\frac{7}{4} \div 2 = \frac{7}{4} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{7}{8}$$

Problem 27
Use a compass to draw a circle on a coordinate plane. Make the center of the circle the origin, and make the radius five units. At which two points does the circle cross the x-axis?
Answer:

(5, 0) and (-5, 0)

Explanation:

Since the circle is centered at the origin (0,0) and has a radius of 5, it will extend 5 units right and 5 units left along the x-axis.

Problem 28
What is the area of the circle in problem 27? (Use 3.14 for \(\pi\).)

78.5 square units

Explanation:

The area formula for a circle is \(A = \pi \times r^2\).
Radius \(r = 5\), so \(r^2 = 25\).
$$A = 3.14 \times 25 = 78.5$$

Problem 30
If Freddy tosses a coin four times, what is the probability that the coin will turn up heads, tails, heads, tails in that order?
Answer:

\(\frac{1}{16}\)

Explanation:

The probability of tossing either heads or tails on any given flip is \(\frac{1}{2}\). Since each toss is an independent event, multiply the probabilities together:
$$P(\text{H, T, H, T}) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{16}$$
