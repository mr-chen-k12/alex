Lesson Practice

Converting percents, decimals, fractions, and mixed numbers (p. 313).

a.

Change the decimal number to a reduced fraction or mixed number: \(0.24\)

Solution

\(0.24\) is read “twenty-four hundredths,” so write it over \(100\) and reduce:

\[ 0.24 = \frac{24}{100} = \frac{24 \div 4}{100 \div 4} = \frac{6}{25} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\dfrac{6}{25}}\)

b.

Change the decimal number to a reduced fraction or mixed number: \(45.6\)

Solution

The digit \(6\) is in the tenths place:

\[ 45.6 = 45\frac{6}{10} = 45\frac{3}{5} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{45\dfrac{3}{5}}\)

c.

Change the decimal number to a reduced fraction or mixed number: \(2.375\)

Solution

\(0.375\) is three hundred seventy-five thousandths:

\[ 2.375 = 2\frac{375}{1000} = 2\frac{375 \div 125}{1000 \div 125} = 2\frac{3}{8} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{2\dfrac{3}{8}}\)

d.

Change the fraction to a decimal number: \(\dfrac{23}{4}\)

Solution

Divide the numerator by the denominator:

\[ \frac{23}{4} = 23 \div 4 = 5.75 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{5.75}\)

e.

Change the mixed number to a decimal number: \(4\dfrac{3}{5}\)

Solution

Convert the fraction part, then add the whole number:

\[ \frac{3}{5} = 3 \div 5 = 0.6 \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad 4\frac{3}{5} = 4.6 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{4.6}\)

f.

Change the fraction to a decimal number: \(\dfrac{5}{8}\)

Solution

\[ \frac{5}{8} = 5 \div 8 = 0.625 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{0.625}\)

g.

Change the fraction to a decimal number: \(\dfrac{5}{6}\)

Solution

Dividing gives a repeating decimal — the \(3\) repeats forever:

\[ \frac{5}{6} = 5 \div 6 = 0.8333\ldots = 0.8\overline{3} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{0.8\overline{3}}\)

h.

Convert the percent to a decimal number: \(8\%\)

Solution

Percent means “per hundred.” Divide by \(100\) (move the decimal point two places left):

\[ 8\% = \frac{8}{100} = 0.08 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{0.08}\)

i.

Convert the percent to a decimal number: \(12.5\%\)

Solution

\[ 12.5\% = \frac{12.5}{100} = 0.125 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{0.125}\)

j.

Convert the percent to a decimal number: \(150\%\)

Solution

\[ 150\% = \frac{150}{100} = 1.5 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{1.5}\)

k.

Convert the percent to a decimal number: \(6\dfrac{1}{2}\%\)

Solution

First write \(6\frac{1}{2}\%\) as \(6.5\%\), then divide by \(100\):

\[ 6\frac{1}{2}\% = 6.5\% = \frac{6.5}{100} = 0.065 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{0.065}\)

l.Connect

Two hundred percent \((200\%)\) is equivalent to what whole number? Describe the steps you took to find the answer.

Solution

Step 1: write the percent as a fraction over \(100\). Step 2: divide.

\[ 200\% = \frac{200}{100} = 2 \]

So \(200\%\) means two whole amounts — twice as much as \(100\%\).

Answer: \(\boxed{2}\)

Written Practice

Problems 1–30, in order (pp. 313–316).

1.

The ratio of weekend days to weekdays is \(2\) to \(5\). What fraction of the week consists of weekend days?

Solution

A week has \(2 + 5 = 7\) days in all. The weekend days are \(2\) of those \(7\) days:

\[ \frac{\text{weekend days}}{\text{all days}} = \frac{2}{7} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\dfrac{2}{7}}\) of the week

2.

Eric ran \(8\) laps in \(11\) minutes \(44\) seconds.

  1. How many seconds did it take Eric to run \(8\) laps?
  2. What is the average number of seconds it took Eric to run each lap?

Solution

a. Each minute is \(60\) seconds:

\[ 11 \times 60 + 44 = 660 + 44 = 704 \text{ seconds} \]

b. Divide the total time by the \(8\) laps:

\[ 704 \div 8 = 88 \text{ seconds per lap} \]

Answers: a. \(\boxed{704\text{ seconds}}\)   b. \(\boxed{88\text{ seconds per lap}}\)

3.

Some gas was still in the tank. Mr. Wang added \(13.3\) gallons of gas, which filled the tank. If the tank held a total of \(21.0\) gallons of gas, how much gas was in the tank before Mr. Wang added the gas?

Solution

Subtract what was added from the full-tank amount:

\[ 21.0 - 13.3 = 7.7 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{7.7\text{ gallons}}\)

4.

From 1750 to 1850, the estimated population of the world increased from seven hundred twenty-five million to one billion, two hundred thousand. About how many more people were living in the world in 1850 than in 1750?

Solution

Write both populations as numbers:

\[ \text{1750: } 725{,}000{,}000 \qquad \text{1850: } 1{,}000{,}200{,}000 \]

Subtract to find the increase:

\[ 1{,}000{,}200{,}000 - 725{,}000{,}000 = 275{,}200{,}000 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{about }275\text{ million more people}}\) \((275{,}200{,}000)\)

5.

Diagram this statement. Then answer the questions that follow.

The Beagles played two thirds of their \(15\) games in dry weather and the rest in rain.

  1. How many games did the team play in dry weather?
  2. What was the ratio of dry-weather games to rainy games?

Solution

Divide the bar for \(15\) games into \(3\) equal parts. Each part is \(15 \div 3 = 5\) games. Two parts are dry weather; one part is rain.

a. Dry-weather games: \(2 \times 5 = 10\).

b. Rainy games: \(15 - 10 = 5\). Ratio \(= 10 : 5\), which reduces to \(2 : 1\).

Answers: a. \(\boxed{10\text{ games}}\)   b. \(\boxed{2\text{ to }1}\)

6.Analyze

For the 100-meter finish times, find the

  1. median.
  2. lower quartile.
  3. upper quartile.

Solution

First list all \(15\) times in order (seconds):

\[ 11.2,\ 12.3,\ 12.4,\ 12.8,\ 13.0,\ 13.3,\ 13.4,\ 13.5,\ 13.6,\ 14.1,\ 14.4,\ 14.7,\ 14.8,\ 15.2,\ 15.5 \]

a. With \(15\) values, the median is the \(8\)th value: \(13.5\) s.

b. The lower quartile is the median of the \(7\) values below the median — the \(4\)th value: \(12.8\) s.

c. The upper quartile is the median of the \(7\) values above the median — the \(12\)th value: \(14.7\) s.

Answers: a. \(\boxed{13.5\text{ s}}\)   b. \(\boxed{12.8\text{ s}}\)   c. \(\boxed{14.7\text{ s}}\)

Make a box-and-whisker plot of the 100-meter finish times in the stem-and-leaf plot.

Solution

A box-and-whisker plot needs five values: minimum \(11.2\), lower quartile \(12.8\), median \(13.5\), upper quartile \(14.7\), and maximum \(15.5\) (from problem 6, plus the smallest and largest times).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{Min }11.2,\ Q_1\ 12.8,\ \text{Med }13.5,\ Q_3\ 14.7,\ \text{Max }15.5\text{ (seconds)}}\)

8.

A square and a regular hexagon share a common side, as shown. The perimeter of the hexagon is \(120\) mm. What is the perimeter of the square?

Solution

A regular hexagon has \(6\) equal sides:

\[ \text{side} = 120 \div 6 = 20 \text{ mm} \]

The square shares that side, so each side of the square is also \(20\) mm:

\[ \text{perimeter of square} = 4 \times 20 = 80 \text{ mm} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{80\text{ mm}}\)

9.

Write each of these numbers as a reduced fraction or mixed number:

  1. \(0.375\)
  2. \(5.55\)
  3. \(250\%\)

Solution

a. \(0.375 = \dfrac{375}{1000} = \dfrac{3}{8}\) (divide top and bottom by \(125\)).

b. \(5.55 = 5\dfrac{55}{100} = 5\dfrac{11}{20}\) (divide by \(5\)).

c. \(250\% = \dfrac{250}{100} = \dfrac{5}{2} = 2\dfrac{1}{2}\).

Answers: a. \(\boxed{\dfrac{3}{8}}\)   b. \(\boxed{5\dfrac{11}{20}}\)   c. \(\boxed{2\dfrac{1}{2}}\)

b. \(\dfrac{1}{8} = 1 \div 8 = 0.125\).

c. \(250\% = \dfrac{250}{100} = 2.5\).

11.

Round each number to the nearest thousandth:

  1. \(0.\overline{45}\)
  2. \(3.\overline{142857}\)

Solution

a. \(0.\overline{45} = 0.454545\ldots\) The digit after the thousandths place is \(5\), so round the \(4\) up:

\[ 0.4545\ldots \approx 0.455 \]

b. \(3.\overline{142857} = 3.142857142857\ldots\) The digit after the thousandths place is \(8\), so round the \(2\) up:

\[ 3.142857\ldots \approx 3.143 \]

Answers: a. \(\boxed{0.455}\)   b. \(\boxed{3.143}\)

12.

Divide \(1.9\) by \(12\) and write the quotient

  1. with a bar over the repetend.
  2. rounded to three decimal places.

Solution

\[ 1.9 \div 12 = 0.158333\ldots \]

Only the digit \(3\) repeats, so the bar goes over the \(3\) alone. For three decimal places, the next digit is \(3\), so the \(8\) stays.

Answers: a. \(\boxed{0.158\overline{3}}\)   b. \(\boxed{0.158}\)

13.

Four and five hundredths is how much greater than one hundred sixty-seven thousandths?

Solution

Write both numbers as decimals: four and five hundredths \(= 4.05\); one hundred sixty-seven thousandths \(= 0.167\). Subtract:

\[ 4.05 - 0.167 = 4.050 - 0.167 = 3.883 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{3.883}\) greater

14.

Draw \(\overline{AB}\) \(1\) inch long. Then draw \(\overline{AC}\) \(\dfrac{3}{4}\) inch long perpendicular to \(\overline{AB}\). Complete \(\triangle ABC\) by drawing \(\overline{BC}\). How long is \(\overline{BC}\)?

Solution

Draw \(AB\) horizontally and \(AC\) vertically from \(A\), so \(\angle A\) is a right angle. Then connect \(B\) to \(C\). Measuring \(\overline{BC}\) with a ruler gives \(1\frac{1}{4}\) inches.

Check with the Pythagorean theorem:

\[ BC = \sqrt{1^{2} + \left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^{2}} = \sqrt{1 + \tfrac{9}{16}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{25}{16}} = \frac{5}{4} = 1\frac{1}{4} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{1\dfrac{1}{4}\text{ inches}}\)

15.

A normal deck of cards is composed of four suits (red heart, red diamond, black spade, and black club) of \(13\) cards each (\(2\) through \(10\), jack, queen, king, and ace) for a total of \(52\) cards.

If one card is drawn from a normal deck of cards, what is the probability that the card will be a red card? Express the probability as a reduced fraction and as a decimal number.

Solution

Hearts and diamonds are red: \(13 + 13 = 26\) red cards out of \(52\) cards in all.

\[ P(\text{red}) = \frac{26}{52} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}\ =\ 0.5}\)

20.

Compare and explain the reason for your answer:

\[ \frac{32}{2} \ \bigcirc\ \frac{320}{20} \]

Solution

Evaluate each fraction:

\[ \frac{32}{2} = 16 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{320}{20} = 16 \]

They are equal because \(\dfrac{320}{20}\) is \(\dfrac{32}{2}\) with the numerator and denominator both multiplied by \(10\) — and multiplying top and bottom by the same number does not change a fraction’s value. They are equivalent fractions.

Answer: \(\boxed{\dfrac{32}{2} = \dfrac{320}{20}}\)

21.Analyze

Show two ways to evaluate \(a(b + c)\) if \(a = 2\), \(b = 3\), and \(c = 4\).

Solution

Way 1 — parentheses first (order of operations):

\[ a(b+c) = 2(3+4) = 2 \times 7 = 14 \]

Way 2 — distributive property (multiply first, then add):

\[ a(b+c) = ab + ac = 2 \times 3 + 2 \times 4 = 6 + 8 = 14 \]

Both ways give the same result, as they should.

Answer: \(\boxed{14}\) by both methods

\[ 18c = 10 \times 4.5 = 45 \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad c = 45 \div 18 = 2.5 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{c = 2.5}\)

25.

Simplify: \(3\dfrac{3}{10} - 1\dfrac{11}{15}\)

Solution

Use the common denominator \(30\). Since \(\frac{9}{30} < \frac{22}{30}\), borrow \(1\) from the \(3\):

\[ 3\frac{3}{10} - 1\frac{11}{15} = 3\frac{9}{30} - 1\frac{22}{30} = 2\frac{39}{30} - 1\frac{22}{30} = 1\frac{17}{30} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{1\dfrac{17}{30}}\)

26.

Simplify: \(5\dfrac{1}{2} + 6\dfrac{3}{10} + \dfrac{4}{5}\)

Solution

Use the common denominator \(10\) and add whole numbers and fractions separately:

\[ 5\frac{1}{2} + 6\frac{3}{10} + \frac{4}{5} = 5\frac{5}{10} + 6\frac{3}{10} + \frac{8}{10} = 11\frac{16}{10} = 12\frac{6}{10} = 12\frac{3}{5} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{12\dfrac{3}{5}}\)

27.

Simplify: \(7\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{4}{5} \div 5\)

Solution

Write the mixed numbers as improper fractions, change division to multiplication by the reciprocal, and cancel:

\[ \frac{15}{2} \cdot \frac{10}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{15 \times 10 \times 4}{2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5} = \frac{600}{150} = 4 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{4}\)

29.Conclude

Find the measures of \(\angle a\), \(\angle b\), and \(\angle c\) in the figure at right.

Solution

Angle \(a\): \(\angle a\) and the \(110^{\circ}\) angle form a straight line, so they are supplementary:

\[ a = 180^{\circ} - 110^{\circ} = 70^{\circ} \]

Angle \(b\): The three interior angles of a triangle sum to \(180^{\circ}\):

\[ b = 180^{\circ} - 50^{\circ} - 70^{\circ} = 60^{\circ} \]

Angle \(c\): \(\angle c\) is supplementary to \(\angle b\):

\[ c = 180^{\circ} - 60^{\circ} = 120^{\circ} \]

Answers: \(\boxed{a = 70^{\circ},\quad b = 60^{\circ},\quad c = 120^{\circ}}\)

30.

Refer to the figure at right to answer a–c:

  1. What is the measure of central angle \(AOB\)?
  2. What appears to be the measure of inscribed angle \(ACB\)?
  3. Chord \(AC\) is congruent to chord \(BC\). What appears to be the measure of inscribed angle \(ABC\)?

Solution

a. \(\overline{AB}\) passes through the center, so it is a straight line. Central angle \(AOB\) is a straight angle:

\[ \angle AOB = 180^{\circ} \]

b. \(\angle ACB\) is inscribed in a semicircle. An inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc — half of \(180^{\circ}\):

\[ \angle ACB = \tfrac{1}{2}(180^{\circ}) = 90^{\circ} \]

c. Equal chords \(AC\) and \(BC\) mean the base angles at \(A\) and \(B\) are equal. The angles of \(\triangle ABC\) sum to \(180^{\circ}\):

\[ \angle ABC = \frac{180^{\circ} - 90^{\circ}}{2} = \frac{90^{\circ}}{2} = 45^{\circ} \]

Answers: a. \(\boxed{180^{\circ}}\)   b. \(\boxed{90^{\circ}}\)   c. \(\boxed{45^{\circ}}\)
