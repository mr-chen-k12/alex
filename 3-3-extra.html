Interactive Math Mastery Quiz

Part 1: Finding Percentages

1.1 Sarah answered 15 out of 25 questions correctly on her test. What percent of the questions did she get correct?
Explanation:
Write the ratio as a fraction: \( \frac{15}{25} \). To find the percent, multiply the numerator and denominator by 4 to get a denominator of 100. \( \frac{15 \times 4}{25 \times 4} = \frac{60}{100} \), which is 60%.
1.2 A baker sold 30 out of 40 loaves of bread. What percentage of the bread was sold?
Explanation:
The fraction is \( \frac{30}{40} \). Simplify this to \( \frac{3}{4} \). We know that \( \frac{1}{4} \) is 25%, so \( \frac{3}{4} \) is \( 3 \times 25\% = 75\% \).
1.3 In a class of 50 students, 12 are left-handed. What percent of the class is left-handed?
Explanation:
The fraction is \( \frac{12}{50} \). To make the denominator 100, multiply the top and bottom by 2. \( \frac{12 \times 2}{50 \times 2} = \frac{24}{100} \), which is 24%.
1.4 A basketball team won 18 out of their 20 games. What is their winning percentage?
Explanation:
The fraction is \( \frac{18}{20} \). Multiply the top and bottom by 5 to get a denominator of 100. \( \frac{18 \times 5}{20 \times 5} = \frac{90}{100} \), which is 90%.
1.5 If 8 out of 32 apples in a basket are red, what percent of the apples are red?
Explanation:
The fraction is \( \frac{8}{32} \). Divide the top and bottom by 8 to simplify the fraction to \( \frac{1}{4} \). \( \frac{1}{4} \) is equivalent to 25%.

Part 2: Order of Operations

2.1 Evaluate: \( 8 + 12 \div 4 \times 2 \)
Explanation:
According to PEMDAS/BODMAS, perform multiplication and division from left to right before addition.
First: \( 12 \div 4 = 3 \)
Second: \( 3 \times 2 = 6 \)
Third: \( 8 + 6 = 14 \)
2.2 Evaluate: \( 15 - 3 \times 4 + 2 \)
Explanation:
Multiply first, then add/subtract from left to right.
First: \( 3 \times 4 = 12 \)
Second: \( 15 - 12 = 3 \)
Third: \( 3 + 2 = 5 \)
2.3 Evaluate: \( 20 \div 5 \times 2 + 3 \)
Explanation:
Perform division and multiplication from left to right.
First: \( 20 \div 5 = 4 \)
Second: \( 4 \times 2 = 8 \)
Third: \( 8 + 3 = 11 \)
2.4 Evaluate: \( 5 \times 4 - 8 \div 2 \)
Explanation:
Perform multiplication and division before subtraction.
First: \( 5 \times 4 = 20 \)
Second: \( 8 \div 2 = 4 \)
Third: \( 20 - 4 = 16 \)
2.5 Evaluate: \( 10 + 6 \div 2 - 4 \)
Explanation:
Divide first, then add and subtract from left to right.
First: \( 6 \div 2 = 3 \)
Second: \( 10 + 3 = 13 \)
Third: \( 13 - 4 = 9 \)

Part 3: Word Problems with Fractions

3.1 Mark bought 3 dozen eggs and used \( \frac{1}{4} \) of them to bake cakes. How many eggs are left?
Explanation:
First, find the total number of eggs: 3 dozen = \( 3 \times 12 = 36 \) eggs.
He used \( \frac{1}{4} \), so he has \( \frac{3}{4} \) left.
Calculate \( \frac{3}{4} \) of 36: \( 36 \div 4 = 9 \), and \( 9 \times 3 = 27 \) eggs left.
3.2 A florist has 4 dozen roses. If \( \frac{5}{8} \) of them are red, how many are NOT red?
Explanation:
Total roses: 4 dozen = \( 4 \times 12 = 48 \).
If \( \frac{5}{8} \) are red, then \( \frac{3}{8} \) are NOT red (\( 1 - \frac{5}{8} \)).
Calculate \( \frac{3}{8} \) of 48: \( 48 \div 8 = 6 \), and \( 6 \times 3 = 18 \) roses are not red.
3.3 Jenna made 60 cookies. She gave \( \frac{2}{5} \) of them to her friends. How many cookies does she have left?
Explanation:
If she gave away \( \frac{2}{5} \), she kept \( \frac{3}{5} \).
Calculate \( \frac{3}{5} \) of 60: \( 60 \div 5 = 12 \), and \( 12 \times 3 = 36 \) cookies left.
(Alternatively: \( \frac{2}{5} \) of 60 = 24. \( 60 - 24 = 36 \)).
3.4 A bookstore had 2 dozen copies of a new book. They sold \( \frac{2}{3} \) of them on the first day. How many copies are left?
Explanation:
Total books: 2 dozen = \( 2 \times 12 = 24 \).
If they sold \( \frac{2}{3} \), they have \( \frac{1}{3} \) left.
Calculate \( \frac{1}{3} \) of 24: \( 24 \div 3 = 8 \) books left.
3.5 Paul has 100 marbles. He loses \( \frac{3}{10} \) of them. How many marbles does he have remaining?
Explanation:
If he lost \( \frac{3}{10} \), he kept \( \frac{7}{10} \).
Calculate \( \frac{7}{10} \) of 100: \( 100 \div 10 = 10 \), and \( 10 \times 7 = 70 \) marbles remaining.

Part 4: Supplementary Angles & Ratios

4.1 Angles A and B are supplementary. If \( m\angle A = 70^\circ \), what is \( m\angle B \)?
Explanation:
Supplementary angles add up to 180°.
\( m\angle B = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ \).
4.2 Angles X and Y are supplementary. If \( m\angle X = 60^\circ \), what is the ratio of \( m\angle X \) to \( m\angle Y \) in simplest form?
Explanation:
First, find \( m\angle Y \): \( 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ \).
The ratio of X to Y is 60 to 120. Simplify by dividing both by 60 to get 1:2.
4.3 Two supplementary angles have a ratio of 1:5. What is the measure of the smaller angle?
Explanation:
Let the angles be \( 1x \) and \( 5x \). Since they are supplementary: \( 1x + 5x = 180^\circ \).
\( 6x = 180^\circ \)
\( x = 30^\circ \). The smaller angle is 30°.
4.4 Angles P and Q are supplementary. \( m\angle P \) is \( 135^\circ \). What is the ratio of \( m\angle Q \) to \( m\angle P \)?
Explanation:
First, find \( m\angle Q \): \( 180^\circ - 135^\circ = 45^\circ \).
The ratio of Q to P is 45 to 135. Both divide evenly by 45.
\( 45 \div 45 = 1 \) and \( 135 \div 45 = 3 \). The ratio is 1:3.
4.5 If angle M is \( 150^\circ \) and angle N is its supplement, what is the difference between them (\( m\angle M - m\angle N \))?
Explanation:
First, find \( m\angle N \): \( 180^\circ - 150^\circ = 30^\circ \).
Subtract N from M: \( 150^\circ - 30^\circ = 120^\circ \).
