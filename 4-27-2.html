1. Adding Positives and Negatives (Double Negatives)

1. Evaluate the expression: \(-6 + 5 - (-2) + 3\)
Explanation: Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive. \(-(-2)\) becomes \(+2\).
The equation becomes: \(-6 + 5 + 2 + 3\).
Solving left to right: \(-1 + 2 + 3 = 1 + 3 = 4\).
2. Evaluate the expression: \(12 - (-8) + (-5) - 3\)
Explanation: \(- (-8)\) becomes \(+8\). Adding a negative \(+ (-5)\) is just subtraction \(- 5\).
The equation becomes: \(12 + 8 - 5 - 3\).
Solving: \(20 - 5 - 3 = 15 - 3 = 12\).
3. Evaluate the expression: \(-10 - 4 - (-7) + 2\)
Explanation: \(- (-7)\) becomes \(+7\).
The equation becomes: \(-10 - 4 + 7 + 2\).
Solving: \(-14 + 7 + 2 = -7 + 2 = -5\).

2. Area of a Triangle

1. Find the area of a right triangle with a base of 8 cm and a height of 5 cm.
Explanation: The formula for the area of a triangle is \(A = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\).
\(A = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 5 = 4 \times 5 = 20 \text{ cm}^2\).
2. A triangle has a base of 12 inches and a height of 7 inches. What is its area?
Explanation: \(A = \frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 7\). Half of 12 is 6, and \(6 \times 7 = 42 \text{ in}^2\).
3. If the area of a triangle is \(30 \text{ m}^2\) and its base is 10 m, what is its height?
Explanation: Use the formula \(A = \frac{1}{2}bh\). Plug in the knowns: \(30 = \frac{1}{2}(10)h\).
\(30 = 5h\). Divide by 5 to get \(h = 6 \text{ m}\).

3. Coordinate Plane & Midpoints

1. Find the midpoint between the points \((2, 4)\) and \((6, 8)\).
Explanation: The midpoint formula is \((\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2})\).
X-coordinate: \(\frac{2 + 6}{2} = \frac{8}{2} = 4\).
Y-coordinate: \(\frac{4 + 8}{2} = \frac{12}{2} = 6\). Midpoint is \((4, 6)\).
2. What is the midpoint of the line segment with endpoints \((-4, 2)\) and \((2, -6)\)?
Explanation: X: \(\frac{-4 + 2}{2} = \frac{-2}{2} = -1\).
Y: \(\frac{2 + -6}{2} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2\). Midpoint is \((-1, -2)\).
3. Find the midpoint between \((-3, -5)\) and \((5, 3)\).
Explanation: X: \(\frac{-3 + 5}{2} = \frac{2}{2} = 1\).
Y: \(\frac{-5 + 3}{2} = \frac{-2}{2} = -1\). Midpoint is \((1, -1)\).

4. Order of Operations (PEMDAS & Square Roots)

1. Evaluate: \(3 + 2 \times 6 \div \sqrt{4} \times 8\)
Explanation: 1. Roots first: \(\sqrt{4} = 2\). The equation is \(3 + 2 \times 6 \div 2 \times 8\).
2. Multiply/Divide left-to-right: \(2 \times 6 = 12\). Equation: \(3 + 12 \div 2 \times 8\).
3. Next division: \(12 \div 2 = 6\). Equation: \(3 + 6 \times 8\).
4. Next multiplication: \(6 \times 8 = 48\). Equation: \(3 + 48\).
5. Add: \(51\).
2. Evaluate: \(10 - \sqrt{25} \times 2 + 4^2 \div 2\)
Explanation: 1. Exponents/Roots: \(\sqrt{25} = 5\) and \(4^2 = 16\). Equation: \(10 - 5 \times 2 + 16 \div 2\).
2. Multiply/Divide: \(5 \times 2 = 10\) and \(16 \div 2 = 8\). Equation: \(10 - 10 + 8\).
3. Add/Subtract left-to-right: \(0 + 8 = 8\).
3. Evaluate: \((\sqrt{81} - 3) \times 2 + 12 \div 4\)
Explanation: 1. Parentheses & Roots: \(\sqrt{81} = 9\), so \((9 - 3) = 6\). Equation: \(6 \times 2 + 12 \div 4\).
2. Multiply/Divide left-to-right: \(6 \times 2 = 12\) and \(12 \div 4 = 3\). Equation: \(12 + 3\).
3. Add: \(15\).

5. Area of an L-Shape

1. Find the total area of an L-shape that can be split into two rectangles: a vertical rectangle measuring 5cm by 2cm, and a horizontal rectangle measuring 6cm by 3cm.
Explanation: Area is additive. Area 1 = \(5 \times 2 = 10\). Area 2 = \(6 \times 3 = 18\). Total Area = \(10 + 18 = 28 \text{ cm}^2\).
2. An L-shape consists of a vertical stem (7m height, 4m width) and a horizontal base extending to the right (5m width, 4m height). What is the total area?
Explanation: Stem Area = \(7 \times 4 = 28\). Base Area = \(5 \times 4 = 20\). Total Area = \(28 + 20 = 48 \text{ m}^2\).
3. An L-shaped room is split into two sections: \(6 \text{ ft} \times 3 \text{ ft}\) and \(8 \text{ ft} \times 3 \text{ ft}\). What is the total area of the floor?
Explanation: Section 1 = \(6 \times 3 = 18\). Section 2 = \(8 \times 3 = 24\). Total Area = \(18 + 24 = 42 \text{ sq ft}\).

6. Solving Ratios using Cross Products

1. Solve for \(x\): \(\frac{6}{10} = \frac{x}{25}\)
Explanation: Use cross multiplication: \(10 \times x = 6 \times 25\).
\(10x = 150\). Divide by 10 to isolate \(x\).
\(x = 15\).
2. Solve for \(x\): \(\frac{4}{x} = \frac{12}{21}\)
Explanation: Cross multiply: \(12 \times x = 4 \times 21\).
\(12x = 84\). Divide by 12.
\(x = 7\).
3. Solve for \(x\): \(\frac{x}{8} = \frac{9}{24}\)
Explanation: Cross multiply: \(24 \times x = 8 \times 9\).
\(24x = 72\). Divide by 24.
\(x = 3\).

7. Converting to Simplified Fractions and Decimals

1. Convert 65% to a simplified fraction and a decimal.
Explanation: To decimal: move decimal point two places left (\(65\% = 0.65\)).
To fraction: \(65\% = \frac{65}{100}\). Both numbers divide by 5.
\(65 \div 5 = 13\) and \(100 \div 5 = 20\). Fraction is \(\frac{13}{20}\).
2. Convert 45% to a simplified fraction and a decimal.
Explanation: Decimal: \(45\% = 0.45\).
Fraction: \(\frac{45}{100}\). Divide top and bottom by 5 to simplify: \(\frac{9}{20}\).
3. Convert 8% to a simplified fraction and a decimal.
Explanation: Decimal: \(8\% = 0.08\) (remember to move it two places).
Fraction: \(\frac{8}{100}\). Divide by 4 top and bottom: \(\frac{2}{25}\).

8. Circumference and Radius of a Circle

1. Find the circumference of a circle with a radius of 5 cm (Use \(\pi \approx 3.14\)).
Explanation: Formula is \(C = 2\pi r\).
\(C = 2 \times 3.14 \times 5\). Since \(2 \times 5 = 10\), we have \(10 \times 3.14 = 31.4 \text{ cm}\).
2. If the circumference of a circle is 18.84 m, what is its radius? (Use \(\pi \approx 3.14\)).
Explanation: Use \(C = 2\pi r\). Plug in: \(18.84 = 2 \times 3.14 \times r\).
\(18.84 = 6.28 \times r\). Divide both sides by 6.28.
\(r = 3 \text{ m}\).
3. Find the circumference of a circle with a radius of 10 in (Use \(\pi \approx 3.14\)).
Explanation: Formula is \(C = 2\pi r\).
\(C = 2 \times 3.14 \times 10\).
\(C = 6.28 \times 10 = 62.8 \text{ in}\).
