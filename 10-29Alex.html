1.(28)
EXPLAIN During the first six months of the year, the Montgomery’s monthly electric bills were $128.45, $131.50, $112.30, $96.25, $81.70, and $71.70. How can the Montgomerys find their average monthly electric bill for the first six months of the year?
Answer
\(\text{Add the six bills and divide by }6\text{ — the average is }\$103.65\)
Solution
To find an average (mean), add all the values, then divide by how many values there are. First add the six bills: \(128.45 + 131.50 + 112.30 + 96.25 + 81.70 + 71.70 = \$621.90\). Then divide by the 6 months: \(621.90 \div 6 = \$103.65\). So their average monthly electric bill for the first six months was $103.65.
2.(23, 30)
There were \(2\frac{1}{2}\) gallons of milk in the refrigerator before breakfast. There were \(1\frac{3}{4}\) gallons after dinner. How many gallons of milk were consumed during the day?
Answer
\(\frac{3}{4}\text{ gallon}\)
Solution
Subtract the milk left after dinner from the milk there was before breakfast: \(2\frac{1}{2} - 1\frac{3}{4}\). Use the common denominator 4: \(2\frac{2}{4} - 1\frac{3}{4}\). Since \(\frac{2}{4} < \frac{3}{4}\), borrow 1 from the 2: \(1\frac{6}{4} - 1\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{4}\) gallon.
3.(28)
A one-year subscription to a monthly magazine costs $15.60. The regular newsstand price is $1.75 per issue. How much is saved per issue by paying the subscription price?
Answer
\(\$0.45\text{ per issue}\)
Solution
A monthly magazine has 12 issues per year, so the subscription costs \(15.60 \div 12 = \$1.30\) per issue. Savings per issue: \(1.75 - 1.30 = \$0.45\).
4.(28)
In the 1896 Summer Olympics, Alfred Hajos of Hungary swam 100 m in about 1 minute 22 seconds. One hundred years later, Li Jinyi of China swam the same distance about 27 seconds faster than Alfred Hajos. About how many seconds did it take Li Jinyi to swim 100 m?
Answer
\(\text{About }55\text{ seconds}\)
Solution
1 minute 22 seconds = 82 seconds. Faster means less time: \(82 - 27 = 55\) seconds.
5.(19)
The perimeter of the square equals the perimeter of the regular pentagon. Each side of the pentagon is 16 cm long. How long is each side of the square?
Answer
\(20\text{ cm}\)
Solution
The regular pentagon's perimeter is \(5 \times 16 = 80\) cm. The square has the same perimeter, and a square has 4 equal sides: \(80 \div 4 = 20\) cm per side.
6.(22)
JUSTIFY Only \(\frac{1}{11}\) of the 110 elements known to modern scientists were discovered in ancient times.
a. How many elements were discovered in ancient times?
b. How many elements were not discovered in ancient times?
Answer
\(\text{a. }10 \qquad \text{b. }100\)
Solution
a. \(\frac{1}{11}\) of 110 is \(110 \div 11 = 10\) elements. b. The rest were not discovered in ancient times: \(110 - 10 = 100\) elements. (Justification: 10 is exactly one eleventh of 110, since \(10 \times 11 = 110\).)

12.(33)
Miss Gaviria bought 10.38 gallons of gasoline at $2.28\(\frac{9}{10}\) per gallon.
a. JUSTIFY About how much did she pay for the gasoline? Justify your answer by showing what numbers you rounded off and why.
b. EXPLAIN Is it possible to pay \(\frac{9}{10}\) of one cent? Why do you think gas stations give their prices this way?
Answer
\(\text{a. About }\$23 \qquad \text{b. No — see the solution}\)
Solution
a. Round to numbers that are easy to multiply: 10.38 gallons rounds to 10 gallons, and $2.28\(\frac{9}{10}\) (which is $2.289) rounds to $2.30. Then \(10 \times 2.30 = \$23.00\), so she paid about $23. (The exact cost is \(10.38 \times 2.289 \approx \$23.76\), so the estimate is close.) b. No. The smallest coin is the penny, worth 1 cent, so no one can hand over \(\frac{9}{10}\) of a cent; instead the pump adds up the total and rounds it to the nearest cent. Gas stations price in tenths of a cent because tiny price differences add up over thousands of gallons, and a price like $2.28\(\frac{9}{10}\) looks a little cheaper than $2.29.

27.(9, 15)
Which property of multiplication is used to rename \(\frac{5}{6}\) to \(\frac{10}{12}\)?
\(\frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{2}{2} = \frac{10}{12}\)
Answer
\(\text{Identity Property of Multiplication}\)
Solution
The fraction was multiplied by \(\frac{2}{2}\), which equals 1. Multiplying by 1 renames the number without changing its value — that is the Identity Property of Multiplication.

30.(17)
Use a protractor to draw a triangle that has two 45° angles.
Answer
\(\text{Sample: a right isosceles triangle (}45^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ\text{)}\)
Solution
The angles of a triangle add to 180°, so if two angles are 45° each, the third must be \(180 - 45 - 45 = 90^\circ\). So the triangle is a right triangle with the two legs equal (a right isosceles triangle), as drawn. Any triangle with two 45° angles must have this shape (it may be any size).
RWC.
Kayla goes to the grocery store. While shopping she estimates the cost of each item by rounding to the nearest whole dollar. The items she put in her basket were ground beef ($2.99), cereal ($3.09), milk ($3.89), fruit ($1.23), and peanut butter ($1.29).
a. Based on her method of rounding, how much does she estimate she will spend on groceries?
b. How does her estimation compare to what she will actually pay at the register?
c. How could Kayla change her method to be sure she does not put more in her basket than she can afford?
Answer
\(\text{a. }\$12 \qquad \text{b. She will actually pay }\$12.49 \qquad \text{c. Always round up}\)
Solution
a. Round each price to the nearest whole dollar: beef $2.99 → $3, cereal $3.09 → $3, milk $3.89 → $4, fruit $1.23 → $1, peanut butter $1.29 → $1. Estimate: \(3 + 3 + 4 + 1 + 1 = \$12\). b. The actual total is \(2.99 + 3.09 + 3.89 + 1.23 + 1.29 = \$12.49\), so her estimate is $0.49 less than what she will actually pay — she will pay more than she estimated. c. She should always round each price UP to the next whole dollar instead of to the nearest dollar. Then her estimate will always be at least the actual cost, so if the estimate fits her budget, the real total will too.
