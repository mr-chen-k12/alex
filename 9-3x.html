Practice j
Jack's ruler is divided into eighths of an inch. Assuming the ruler is used correctly, what is the greatest possible measurement error that can be made with Jack's ruler? Express your answer as a fraction of an inch.
Final Answer
\(\frac{1}{16}\text{ inch}\)

Explanation: The greatest possible error in a measurement using a precision ruler is half of the smallest unit mark on the scale. Half of \(\frac{1}{8}\) inch is:

$$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{16}\text{ inch}$$

Problem 3
What is the quotient when the product of 20 and 20 is divided by the sum of 10 and 10?
Final Answer
\(20\)

Step 1: Find the product of 20 and 20: \(20 \times 20 = 400\).

Step 2: Find the sum of 10 and 10: \(10 + 10 = 20\).

Step 3: Divide the product by the sum: \(\frac{400}{20} = 20\).

Problem 4 ANALYZE
List the single-digit divisors of 1680.
Final Answer
\(1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\)

Check single digits (1 to 9):

• 1: Always divides any integer.

• 2: Ends in 0 (even) \(\rightarrow\) Yes.

• 3: Sum of digits \(1+6+8+0 = 15\) (divisible by 3) \(\rightarrow\) Yes.

• 4: Last two digits 80 divisible by 4 \(\rightarrow\) Yes.

• 5: Ends in 0 \(\rightarrow\) Yes.

• 6: Divisible by 2 and 3 \(\rightarrow\) Yes.

• 7: \(1680 \div 7 = 240\) \(\rightarrow\) Yes.

• 8: \(1680 \div 8 = 210\) \(\rightarrow\) Yes.

• 9: Sum of digits 15 is not divisible by 9 \(\rightarrow\) No.

Problem 17 JUSTIFY
Give a reason for each of the first two steps taken to solve the equation \(9 + (n + 8) = 20\): $$\begin{align*} 9 + (n + 8) &= 20 && \text{Given equation} \\ 9 + (8 + n) &= 20 && \text{a. }\underline{\quad\quad\quad\quad} \\ (9 + 8) + n &= 20 && \text{b. }\underline{\quad\quad\quad\quad} \end{align*}$$
Final Answer

a. Commutative property of addition

b. Associative property of addition

• In step a, the order of terms inside parentheses changed from \((n+8)\) to \((8+n)\) (Commutative Property).

• In step b, the grouping changed from \(9 + (8+n)\) to \((9+8) + n\) (Associative Property).

Problem 28
Refer to the figure of quadrilateral \(ABCD\) (trapezoid with right angles at \(C\) and \(D\)). Name the acute, obtuse, and right angles.
Final Answer

Acute angle: \(\angle B\) (or \(\angle ABC\))

Obtuse angle: \(\angle A\) (or \(\angle DAB\))

Right angles: \(\angle C\) and \(\angle D\)

• Angle \(B\) is smaller than \(90^\circ\) (acute).

• Angle \(A\) is larger than \(90^\circ\) (obtuse).

• Angles \(C\) and \(D\) are marked with square symbols indicating \(90^\circ\) (right angles).

Problem 29

Final Answer

a. \(\overline{CB}\) (or \(\overline{BC}\))

b. \(\overline{DC}\) (or \(\overline{CD}\))

a. Segment \(\overline{CB}\) is horizontal and run parallel to \(\overline{DA}\).

b. Segment \(\overline{DC}\) meets \(\overline{DA}\) at a \(90^\circ\) right angle, making it perpendicular.

Problem 30 EXPLAIN
Referring to the line with points \(Q, R, S\), what is the difference in meaning between the notations \(\overline{QR}\) and \(QR\)?
Final Answer
\(\overline{QR}\) refers to the geometric segment, while \(QR\) refers to the length/distance of that segment.

• \(\overline{QR}\) (with a bar above) represents the physical geometric object (segment QR).

• \(QR\) (without a bar) represents a numerical quantity indicating the distance or length between point \(Q\) and point \(R\).
