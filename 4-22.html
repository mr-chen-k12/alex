Lesson Practice

Problem a
Draw a rectangle and a parallelogram that is not a rectangle. Draw all the lines of symmetry for each shape, if any exist.
Answer & Visual:
Explanation:

A rectangle has exactly 2 lines of symmetry (one horizontal and one vertical cutting through the midpoints of the opposite sides). A standard parallelogram (that is not a rectangle or rhombus) has 0 lines of symmetry because folding it over any line will not result in matching halves.

Problem b
All but one of these letters can be drawn to have a line of symmetry. Which of these letters does not have a line of symmetry?

B   D   E   G   H   M
Answer:

G

Explanation:

H and M have a vertical line of symmetry. B, D, E, and H have a horizontal line of symmetry. The letter G cannot be folded over any line to match perfectly.

Problem c
Which three of these letters have rotational symmetry? (Hint: Rotating your paper upside down might help.)

H   L   O   R   V   X
Answer:

H, O, and X

Explanation:

If you rotate the letters H, O, and X by 180 degrees (upside down), they look identical to their original orientation. The others (L, R, V) will look upside down and not map onto themselves.

Written Practice

Problem 1
When the greatest four-digit number is divided by the greatest single-digit number, what is the quotient?
Answer:

1111

Explanation:

The greatest four-digit number is 9999. The greatest single-digit number is 9.
$$9999 \div 9 = 1111$$

Problem 2
The ratio of cats to dogs at an animal shelter is 3 to 2. If there are 18 cats at the shelter, how many dogs are there?
Answer:

12 dogs

Explanation:

Set up a proportion: $$\frac{\text{Cats}}{\text{Dogs}} = \frac{3}{2} = \frac{18}{D}$$ Find the scale factor: $3 \times 6 = 18$, so $2 \times 6 = 12$.

Problem 3
A rectangular prism has a length of 4 inches, a width of 5 inches, and a height of 10 inches. What is the volume of the prism?
Answer:

200 cubic inches

Explanation:

The volume of a rectangular prism is length times width times height.
$$V = 4 \times 5 \times 10 = 20 \times 10 = 200 \text{ in}^3$$

Problem 4
A full turn is 360 degrees. How many degrees is $\frac{1}{4}$ of a turn?
Answer:

90 degrees

Explanation:

Multiply the full turn by the fraction: $$360^\circ \times \frac{1}{4} = 90^\circ$$

Problem 5
Does a regular hexagon have rotational and line symmetry? Use words to explain how you know.
Answer:

Yes, it has both.

Explanation:

Line Symmetry: A regular hexagon has 6 lines of symmetry (three connecting opposite vertices, and three connecting the midpoints of opposite sides).
Rotational Symmetry: Because all 6 sides and angles are equal, rotating the hexagon by 60 degrees ($360 \div 6$) around its center perfectly maps it onto its original position.

Problem 6
Sketch a rectangle similar to a 4 cm by 7 cm rectangle. Make the scale factor from the original rectangle to your sketch 3. What is the perimeter of the rectangle you sketched?
Answer:

66 cm

Explanation:

The original rectangle has dimensions 4 cm and 7 cm. With a scale factor of 3, the new side lengths are $4 \times 3 = 12 \text{ cm}$ and $7 \times 3 = 21 \text{ cm}$.
The perimeter of the new rectangle is $12 + 21 + 12 + 21 = 66 \text{ cm}$.

Problem 7
What is the area of a right triangle with legs of 6 cm and 8 cm, and a hypotenuse of 10 cm?
Answer:

24 cm²

Explanation:

The area of a right triangle is calculated using the two legs as the base and height (the hypotenuse is not needed for area).
$$Area = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = \frac{1}{2} \times 48 = 24 \text{ cm}^2$$

Problem 8
Draw a ratio box for this problem. Then solve the problem using a proportion.

A dealership has 80 cars on the lot. If 40% of the cars are sedans and the rest are SUVs, how many SUVs are on the lot?
Answer:

48 SUVs

Explanation:

If 40% are sedans, then 60% are SUVs (100% - 40%). Set up a proportion comparing the SUV percentage to the total: $$\frac{\text{Percent}}{\text{Total}} \Rightarrow \frac{60}{100} = \frac{x}{80}$$ Cross-multiplying gives: $100x = 4800$, so $x = 48$.
There are 48 SUVs.

Problem 9
Convert the fraction $3 \frac{1}{2}$ to a decimal (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. 3.5    b. 350%

Explanation:

$\frac{1}{2}$ is equivalent to 0.5, so $3 \frac{1}{2} = 3.5$. To find the percent, multiply the decimal by 100 to get 350%.

Problem 10
Convert the decimal 2.8 to a mixed fraction (a) and a percent (b).
Answer:

a. $2 \frac{4}{5}$    b. 280%

Explanation:

2.8 is "two and eight-tenths" ($2 \frac{8}{10}$), which simplifies to $2 \frac{4}{5}$ by dividing numerator and denominator by 2. Multiply 2.8 by 100 to find the percent: 280%.

Problem 11
Convert 45% to a fraction (a) and a decimal (b).
Answer:

a. $\frac{9}{20}$    b. 0.45

Explanation:

45% as a decimal is 0.45. As a fraction, it is $\frac{45}{100}$, which reduces by dividing numerator and denominator by 5 to get $\frac{9}{20}$.

Problem 12
$$5 \frac{1}{3} + 2 \frac{1}{6} + 1 \frac{1}{2}$$
Answer:

9

Explanation:

Find a common denominator for the fractions, which is 6: $$5 \frac{2}{6} + 2 \frac{1}{6} + 1 \frac{3}{6}$$ Add the whole numbers: $5 + 2 + 1 = 8$.
Add the fractions: $\frac{2}{6} + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{6}{6}$.
Since $\frac{6}{6} = 1$, add it to the whole number:
Total: $8 + 1 = 9$.

Problem 13
$$\left(4 \frac{1}{2} \div 3\right) \div 1 \frac{1}{2}$$
Answer:

1

Explanation:

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions: $$\left(\frac{9}{2} \div \frac{3}{1}\right) \div \frac{3}{2}$$ Follow PEMDAS, evaluate parentheses by multiplying by the reciprocal: $$\left(\frac{9}{2} \times \frac{1}{3}\right) \div \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{6} \div \frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{2} \div \frac{3}{2}$$ Any number divided by itself is 1.

Explanation:

First, add the numbers in parentheses: $3.2 + 6.8 = 10.0$.
Subtract from 25: $25.0 - 10.0 = 15$.

Problem 17
Round the quotient to the nearest hundredth: $$0.145 \div 5$$
Answer:

0.03

Explanation:

Divide: $$0.145 \div 5 = 0.029$$ The hundredths place is 2. The next digit is 9, which is 5 or greater, so round the 2 up to 3. The rounded answer is 0.03.

Problem 18
Write the prime factorization of 600 using exponents.
Answer:

$2^3 \times 3 \times 5^2$

Explanation:

Break down 600: $600 = 6 \times 100$.
$6 = 2 \times 3$.
$100 = 10 \times 10 = (2 \times 5) \times (2 \times 5) = 2^2 \times 5^2$.
Combine them in numerical order: $(2 \times 2^2) \times 3 \times 5^2 = 2^3 \times 3 \times 5^2$.

Problem 19
"All equilateral triangles are similar." True or false?
Answer:

True

Explanation:

All equilateral triangles have three equal angles of 60 degrees. Because their corresponding angles are always identical and their sides are uniformly proportional, they are universally similar shapes.

Problem 20
$$4^3 - 4^2 \div 4 - 4 \times 3$$
Answer:

48

Explanation:

Follow the order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS):
Exponents first: $64 - 16 \div 4 - 4 \times 3$
Multiplication and Division (left to right): $64 - 4 - 12$
Addition and Subtraction (left to right): $60 - 12 = 48$.

Problem 21
What is the perimeter of this polygon? (An L-shaped polygon with top horizontal edge = 15 cm, left vertical edge = 12 cm, right vertical edge = 5 cm, and bottom-inner horizontal edge = 9 cm).
Answer:

54 cm

Explanation:

To find the perimeter, add all the outer boundaries.
Top edge = 15 cm
Left edge = 12 cm
Right edge = 5 cm
Bottom-inner horizontal edge = 9 cm
The missing bottom horizontal segment is $15 \text{ cm} - 9 \text{ cm} = 6 \text{ cm}$.
The missing inner vertical "step-down" segment is $12 \text{ cm} - 5 \text{ cm} = 7 \text{ cm}$.
$$Perimeter = 15 + 12 + 6 + 7 + 9 + 5 = 54 \text{ cm}$$

Problem 22
What is the area of the polygon from problem 21?
Answer:

117 cm²

Explanation:

Split the polygon into two rectangles.
Rectangle 1 (left side column): Width = 6 cm, Height = 12 cm. Area = $6 \times 12 = 72 \text{ cm}^2$.
Rectangle 2 (right side step): Width = 9 cm, Height = 5 cm. Area = $9 \times 5 = 45 \text{ cm}^2$.
Total Area = $72 + 45 = 117 \text{ cm}^2$.

Problem 23
Figures A and B are congruent triangles. Figure A is pointing to the left, while Figure B is pointing to the right and has been shifted higher up. Name the transformations that would position Figure A on Figure B.
Answer:

Reflection and Translation

Explanation:

Because the figure has had its directional orientation flipped backwards, a reflection over a vertical axis is required. To then slide it perfectly into the new location of Figure B higher up, a translation is needed.

Problem 24
The perimeter of a rectangle is 50 cm. If the width of the rectangle is 10 cm, what is the length?
Answer:

15 cm

Explanation:

The perimeter formula for a rectangle is $P = 2l + 2w$.
$$50 = 2l + 2(10)$$ $$50 = 2l + 20$$ $$30 = 2l$$ $$l = 15 \text{ cm}$$

Problem 25
A circular track has a diameter of 300 m. Find the circumference of the track to the nearest ten meters.
Answer:

940 m

Explanation:

Circumference formula is $C = \pi \times d$. Using 3.14 for $\pi$:
$$C \approx 3.14 \times 300 = 942 \text{ m}$$
The question asks to round to the nearest ten meters. 942 rounded to the nearest ten is 940 m.

Problem 26
Use a ruler to draw line segment $\overline{AB}$ $5 \frac{1}{2}$ inches long. Then draw a dot at the midpoint of $AB$, and label the point $M$. How long is $\overline{AM}$?
Answer:

$2 \frac{3}{4}$ inches

Explanation:

The midpoint divides the segment exactly in half. Divide $5 \frac{1}{2}$ by 2: $$5 \frac{1}{2} = \frac{11}{2}$$ $$\frac{11}{2} \div 2 = \frac{11}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{11}{4} = 2 \frac{3}{4}$$

Problem 27
Use a compass to draw a circle on a coordinate plane. Make the center of the circle the origin, and make the radius 7 units. At which two points does the circle cross the y-axis?
Answer:

(0, 7) and (0, -7)

Explanation:

Since the circle is centered at the origin (0,0) and has a radius of 7, it will extend 7 units straight up and 7 units straight down along the y-axis.

Problem 28
What is the area of the circle in problem 27? (Use 3.14 for $\pi$.)
Answer:

153.86 square units

Explanation:

The area formula for a circle is $A = \pi \times r^2$.
Radius $r = 7$, so $r^2 = 49$.
$$A = 3.14 \times 49 = 153.86$$

Problem 30
If Sarah flips a standard coin four times, what is the probability that it lands on heads all four times?
Answer:

$\frac{1}{16}$

Explanation:

The probability of landing on heads on a single flip is $\frac{1}{2}$. Since the four flips are independent events, multiply the probabilities together:
$$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{16}$$
