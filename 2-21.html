1. Figure ABCD is a rectangle. Segment AB is 6 cm long, Segment BC is 8 cm long, and Segment AC is 10 cm long. What is the area of triangle ABC?

Explanation: To find the area of triangle ABC, we use the formula for the area of a right triangle: \( \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} \).

The base (BC) is 8 cm and the height (AB) is 6 cm. The diagonal AC (10 cm) is not needed to find the area.

\( \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 6 = \frac{1}{2} \times 48 = 24 \)

(Note: The student originally wrote 6 cm², which was a calculation error from \( 8 \times 6 \div 2 \).)

Answer: 24 cm²
2. One third of the dozen months have exactly 30 days. How many of the months do not have exactly 30 days?

Explanation: A dozen is equal to 12. We first find how many months have exactly 30 days by calculating one third of 12:

\( \frac{1}{3} \times 12 = 4 \text{ months} \)

To find the months that do not have exactly 30 days, we subtract that number from the total:

\( 12 - 4 = 8 \text{ months} \)

Answer: 8 months
3. True or false: Some triangles are quadrilaterals.

Explanation: A triangle, by definition, is a polygon with exactly 3 sides. A quadrilateral is a polygon with exactly 4 sides. Therefore, a shape cannot be both at the same time.

Answer: False
4. Compare using >, <, or = : \( \frac{4}{5} \bigcirc 0.7 \)

Explanation: First, convert the fraction to a decimal to easily compare the two values.

\( \frac{4}{5} = \frac{8}{10} = 0.8 \)

Since \( 0.8 \) is greater than \( 0.7 \), we use the greater-than sign.

Answer: >
5. \( 4 \div 0.4 \)

Explanation: When dividing by a decimal, it's easiest to multiply both the dividend and the divisor by 10 to clear the decimal point.

\( 4 \div 0.4 \rightarrow 40 \div 4 \)

\( 40 \div 4 = 10 \)

Answer: 10
6. \( 4.5 \div 10^2 \)

Explanation: First, evaluate the exponent: \( 10^2 = 100 \).

The problem is now \( 4.5 \div 100 \).

Dividing by 100 moves the decimal point two places to the left.

\( 4.5 \rightarrow 0.45 \rightarrow 0.045 \)

Answer: 0.045
7. \( 2.8 \div \sqrt{64} \)

Explanation: First, solve the square root: \( \sqrt{64} = 8 \).

The problem is now \( 2.8 \div 8 \).

Using long division, 8 goes into 28 three times with a remainder of 4. Bring down a zero to make 40. 8 goes into 40 exactly 5 times. Keep the decimal in place.

\( 2.8 \div 8 = 0.35 \)

Answer: 0.35
8. \( 3 \div 2 \frac{2}{3} \)

Explanation: First, convert the mixed number to an improper fraction:

\( 2 \frac{2}{3} = \frac{(2 \times 3) + 2}{3} = \frac{8}{3} \)

Now, dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal:

\( \frac{3}{1} \div \frac{8}{3} = \frac{3}{1} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{9}{8} \)

Convert back to a mixed number: \( \frac{9}{8} = 1 \frac{1}{8} \).

Answer: \( 1 \frac{1}{8} \)
9. \( 2 \frac{1}{2} + 4 \frac{3}{4} \)

Explanation: Find a common denominator for the fractions. The lowest common multiple of 2 and 4 is 4.

\( 2 \frac{1}{2} = 2 \frac{2}{4} \)

Add the whole numbers and the fractions together:

\( 2 \frac{2}{4} + 4 \frac{3}{4} = 6 \frac{5}{4} \)

The fraction \( \frac{5}{4} \) is an improper fraction, which is \( 1 \frac{1}{4} \). Add this back to the whole number:

\( 6 + 1 \frac{1}{4} = 7 \frac{1}{4} \)

Answer: \( 7 \frac{1}{4} \)
10. \( 4 \frac{1}{4} - 3 \frac{3}{4} \)

Explanation: Because \( \frac{1}{4} \) is smaller than \( \frac{3}{4} \), we need to borrow 1 from the whole number 4.

\( 4 \frac{1}{4} = 3 + \frac{4}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = 3 \frac{5}{4} \)

Now we can subtract:

\( 3 \frac{5}{4} - 3 \frac{3}{4} \)

Subtract the whole numbers: \( 3 - 3 = 0 \)

Subtract the fractions: \( \frac{5}{4} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{2}{4} \)

Simplify the fraction:

\( \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \)

Answer: \( \frac{1}{2} \)
