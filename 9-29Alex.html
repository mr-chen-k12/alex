Lesson Practice

a.

What is the shape of a stop sign?

Answer: An octagon.

Solution: A stop sign has 8 sides and 8 angles. An 8-sided polygon is called an octagon.

b.

What do we usually call a regular quadrilateral?

Answer: A square.

Solution: "Regular" means all sides and all angles are equal. A quadrilateral with 4 equal sides and 4 right angles is a square.

c.

What kind of angle is each angle of a regular triangle?

Answer: A \(60^\circ\) angle, which is an acute angle.

Solution: The angles of a triangle total \(180^\circ\). A regular (equilateral) triangle has 3 equal angles, so each angle is \(\frac{180^\circ}{3} = 60^\circ\). Since \(60^\circ < 90^\circ\), each angle is acute.

d. EXPLAIN

Are all squares similar? How do you know?

Answer: Yes.

Solution: Similar figures have the same shape: corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides proportional. Every square has four \(90^\circ\) angles, and all of its sides are equal to each other, so any two squares have the same shape — they are always similar.

e.

Are all squares congruent?

Answer: No.

Solution: Congruent figures must have the same size and shape. Squares can have different side lengths (for example, a \(2\text{ in.}\) square and a \(5\text{ in.}\) square), so not all squares are congruent.

f.

Sketch a pentagon, a hexagon, and a heptagon.

Answer: Draw three closed figures made of straight sides:

Solution: A pentagon has 5 sides, a hexagon has 6 sides, and a heptagon has 7 sides. Each sketch should be a closed figure whose sides are straight line segments.

g. ANALYZE

Referring to example 4, which angle in \(\triangle ABC\) corresponds to \(\angle Y\) in \(\triangle XYZ\)?

Answer: \(\angle B\).

Solution: In Example 4, \(\triangle ABC\) corresponds to \(\triangle XYZ\) with \(\angle A\) corresponding to \(\angle X\) (and \(\overline{BC}\) corresponding to \(\overline{YZ}\)). Matching the vertices in order, \(\angle B\) corresponds to \(\angle Y\).

h.

Referring to example 3, are the angles in figure II larger in measure, smaller in measure, or equal in measure to the corresponding angles in figure I?

Answer: Equal in measure.

Solution: Figures I and II are similar figures, and corresponding angles of similar figures are equal in measure (only the side lengths change).

i. MODEL

Draw a triangle that contains a right angle. Label the vertices \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) so that the right angle is at vertex \(C\).

Answer: A right triangle with the square corner at vertex \(C\).

Solution: Draw a triangle with one \(90^\circ\) angle (mark it with the small square symbol). Label the vertex with the right angle \(C\) and the other two vertices \(A\) and \(B\).

j.

Which of these figures is not a polygon? Write the reason for your choice. (A: hexagon, B: open figure, C: star)

Answer: Figure B.

Solution: A polygon must be a closed figure formed by line segments. Figure B is not closed (its sides do not connect to enclose a region), so it is not a polygon.

Written Practice — Problems 1–11

1. (13)

The Collins family drove the 2825-mile, coast-to-coast drive from New York, New York to Los Angeles, California in 6 days. They drove about the same distance each day. What was the average number of miles they traveled each day?

Answer: About \(471\) miles per day (exactly \(470\frac{5}{6}\) miles).

Solution: Divide the total distance by the number of days: \(\frac{2825}{6} = 470\frac{5}{6} \approx 470.83\). Rounded to the nearest mile, that is about 471 miles per day.

a. This function table shows the number of gallons (output) in a given number of quarts (input). Copy and extend the table to include 5, 6, 7, and 8 quarts.

b. GENERALIZE What is the rule for this function table? Justify your answer.

Answer:

a. Extended table:

QuartsGallons

2\(\frac{1}{2}\)
3\(\frac{3}{4}\)
4\(1\)
5\(1\frac{1}{4}\)
6\(1\frac{1}{2}\)
7\(1\frac{3}{4}\)

b. The rule is: gallons = quarts \(\div\) 4 (divide the input by 4).

Solution: There are 4 quarts in 1 gallon, so each quart is \(\frac{1}{4}\) gallon. Multiplying each number of quarts by \(\frac{1}{4}\) (equivalently, dividing by 4) gives the gallons: \(5 \div 4 = 1\frac{1}{4}\), \(6 \div 4 = 1\frac{1}{2}\), \(7 \div 4 = 1\frac{3}{4}\), \(8 \div 4 = 2\).

3. (14)

Albert ran 3977 meters of the 5000-meter race but walked the rest of the way. How many meters of the race did Albert walk?

Answer: \(1023\) meters.

Solution: Subtract the meters run from the total: \(5000 - 3977 = 1023\).

4. (5, 12)

One billion is how much greater than ten million? Use words to write the answer.

Answer: Nine hundred ninety million.

Solution: \(1{,}000{,}000{,}000 - 10{,}000{,}000 = 990{,}000{,}000\), which is written "nine hundred ninety million."

5. (4, 10)

a. Arrange these numbers in order from least to greatest: \(\frac{5}{3}, -1, \frac{3}{4}, 0, 1\)

b. Which of these numbers are not positive?

Answer: a. \(-1, \; 0, \; \frac{3}{4}, \; 1, \; \frac{5}{3}\)    b. \(-1\) and \(0\).

Solution: a. Note that \(\frac{5}{3} = 1\frac{2}{3} \approx 1.67\), which is greater than 1. Ordering by value: \(-1 < 0 < \frac{3}{4} < 1 < \frac{5}{3}\). b. Positive numbers are greater than 0, so \(-1\) (negative) and \(0\) (neither positive nor negative) are not positive.

6. (7)

In rectangle \(ABCD\), which side is parallel to side \(BC\)?

Answer: Side \(AD\).

Solution: In rectangle \(ABCD\), the vertices go in order around the shape. Side \(BC\) and side \(AD\) are opposite sides of the rectangle, and opposite sides of a rectangle are parallel.

7. (4)

Refer to this number line to answer a and b (number line from \(-4\) to \(4\)):

a. What integer is two units to the left of the origin?

b. What integer is seven units to the right of \(-3\)?

Answer: a. \(-2\)    b. \(4\).

Solution: a. The origin is 0; two units to the left is \(0 - 2 = -2\). b. Seven units to the right of \(-3\) is \(-3 + 7 = 4\).

*8. (15)

Write each number as a reduced fraction or mixed number:

a. \(2\%\)    b. \(\frac{12}{20}\)    c. \(6\frac{15}{20}\)

Answer: a. \(\frac{1}{50}\)    b. \(\frac{3}{5}\)    c. \(6\frac{3}{4}\).

Solution: a. \(2\% = \frac{2}{100} = \frac{1}{50}\). b. \(\frac{12}{20} = \frac{12 \div 4}{20 \div 4} = \frac{3}{5}\). c. \(6\frac{15}{20} = 6\frac{15 \div 5}{20 \div 5} = 6\frac{3}{4}\).

*9. (15)

For each fraction, find an equivalent fraction that has a denominator of 30:

a. \(\frac{4}{5}\)    b. \(\frac{2}{3}\)    c. \(\frac{1}{6}\)

Answer: a. \(\frac{24}{30}\)    b. \(\frac{20}{30}\)    c. \(\frac{5}{30}\).

Solution: Multiply numerator and denominator to reach 30: a. \(\frac{4 \times 6}{5 \times 6} = \frac{24}{30}\). b. \(\frac{2 \times 10}{3 \times 10} = \frac{20}{30}\). c. \(\frac{1 \times 5}{6 \times 5} = \frac{5}{30}\).

10. (18)

Name each of these figures. Then tell which is not a polygon. Give the reason for your choice. (a. 8-sided figure, b. 5-sided figure, c. circle)

Answer: a. Octagon, b. pentagon, c. circle. The circle is not a polygon.

Solution: Figure a has 8 sides (octagon) and figure b has 5 sides (pentagon). A polygon must be made of straight line segments — a circle is a curved figure with no straight sides, so it is not a polygon.

*11. (7, 18)

a. MODEL Draw a triangle that has one obtuse angle.

b. What kind of angles are the other two angles of the triangle?

Answer: a. A triangle with one angle greater than \(90^\circ\). b. Acute angles.

Solution: a. Draw a triangle with one angle measuring more than \(90^\circ\) (an obtuse angle). b. The angles of a triangle total \(180^\circ\); if one angle already exceeds \(90^\circ\), the other two must each be less than \(90^\circ\), so they are both acute.

Written Practice — Problems 12–27

*12. (14, 15)

a. PREDICT What is the probability of spinning a 3? (The spinner has 8 equal sectors: three 1's, three 2's, and two 3's.)

b. What is the probability of not spinning a 3?

Answer: a. \(\frac{1}{4}\)    b. \(\frac{3}{4}\).

Solution: The spinner has 8 equal sectors, and 2 of them show a 3. a. \(P(3) = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}\). b. \(P(\text{not } 3) = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}\) (equivalently, \(\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}\)).

*13. (2, 9, 15)

Which properties are illustrated by these equations?

a. \(\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{3} = \frac{3}{6}\)    b. \(\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = 1\)

Answer: a. Identity property of multiplication. b. Inverse property of multiplication.

Solution: a. Since \(\frac{3}{3} = 1\), this is \(\frac{1}{2} \times 1 = \frac{1}{2}\); multiplying by 1 leaves the value unchanged (identity property). b. \(\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\frac{3}{2}\) are reciprocals, and a number times its reciprocal equals 1 (inverse property).

14. (9)

Find the unknown number: \(x - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{5}{8}\)

Answer: \(x = 1\).

Solution: Add \(\frac{3}{8}\) to both sides: \(x = \frac{5}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{8}{8} = 1\). Check: \(1 - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{5}{8}\) ✓

*15. (9, 15)

Find the unknown number: \(y + \frac{3}{10} = \frac{7}{10}\)

Answer: \(y = \frac{2}{5}\).

Solution: Subtract \(\frac{3}{10}\) from both sides: \(y = \frac{7}{10} - \frac{3}{10} = \frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}\). Check: \(\frac{2}{5} + \frac{3}{10} = \frac{4}{10} + \frac{3}{10} = \frac{7}{10}\) ✓

*16. (9, 15)

Find the unknown number: \(\frac{5}{6} - m = \frac{1}{6}\)

Answer: \(m = \frac{2}{3}\).

Solution: \(m = \frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}\). Check: \(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{6} - \frac{4}{6} = \frac{1}{6}\) ✓

17. (9)

Find the unknown number: \(\frac{3}{4}x = 1\)

Answer: \(x = 1\frac{1}{3}\) (or \(\frac{4}{3}\)).

Solution: Multiply both sides by the reciprocal \(\frac{4}{3}\): \(x = 1 \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{4}{3} = 1\frac{1}{3}\). Check: \(\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = 1\) ✓

*18. (15)

Simplify: \(5\frac{7}{10} - \frac{3}{10}\)

Answer: \(5\frac{2}{5}\).

Solution: Subtract the fractions: \(\frac{7}{10} - \frac{3}{10} = \frac{4}{10}\). So \(5\frac{7}{10} - \frac{3}{10} = 5\frac{4}{10} = 5\frac{2}{5}\) (reduced).

*19. (15)

Simplify: \(\frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{4}\)

Answer: \(\frac{3}{4}\).

Solution: Multiply numerators and denominators: \(\frac{3 \cdot 2}{2 \cdot 4} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}\). (The 2's cancel: \(\frac{3}{\cancel{2}} \cdot \frac{\cancel{2}}{4} = \frac{3}{4}\).)

23. (2, 9)

Use and identify the commutative, associative, inverse, and identity properties of multiplication to simplify the following expression. (Hint: See problem 20 in Lesson 15.)

\[\frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{4}{9} \cdot \frac{8}{5}\]

Answer: \(\frac{4}{9}\).

Solution: Use the commutative property to reorder and the associative property to regroup: \(\left(\frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{8}{5}\right) \cdot \frac{4}{9}\). By the inverse property, \(\frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{8}{5} = 1\). By the identity property, \(1 \cdot \frac{4}{9} = \frac{4}{9}\).

*24. (16) ANALYZE

What percent of a pound is 8 ounces?

Answer: \(50\%\).

Solution: There are 16 ounces in a pound, so 8 ounces is \(\frac{8}{16} = \frac{1}{2}\) of a pound, and \(\frac{1}{2} = 50\%\).

*25. (17)

a. How many degrees is \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a circle or \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a full turn?

b. How many degrees is \(\frac{1}{6}\) of a circle or \(\frac{1}{6}\) of a full turn?

Answer: a. \(90^\circ\)    b. \(60^\circ\).

Solution: A full circle (full turn) is \(360^\circ\). a. \(\frac{1}{4} \cdot 360^\circ = 90^\circ\). b. \(\frac{1}{6} \cdot 360^\circ = 60^\circ\).

*26. (17)

a. Use a protractor to draw a \(135^\circ\) angle.

b. A \(135^\circ\) angle is how many degrees less than a straight angle?

Answer: a. An obtuse angle measuring \(135^\circ\). b. \(45^\circ\) less.

Solution: a. Draw an angle measuring \(135^\circ\) with a protractor (it is obtuse, since \(135^\circ > 90^\circ\)). b. A straight angle is \(180^\circ\): \(180^\circ - 135^\circ = 45^\circ\).

*27. (18)

Refer to the triangles below to answer a–c (\(\triangle ABC\), \(\triangle DEF\), \(\triangle QRS\), and \(\triangle XYZ\)).

a. Which triangle appears to be congruent to \(\triangle ABC\)?

b. Which triangle is not similar to \(\triangle ABC\)?

c. Which angle in \(\triangle DEF\) corresponds to \(\angle R\) in \(\triangle SQR\)?

Answer: a. \(\triangle DEF\)    b. \(\triangle XYZ\)    c. \(\angle E\).

Solution: a. \(\triangle DEF\) has the same size and shape as \(\triangle ABC\). b. \(\triangle QRS\) is a smaller version of the same shape (similar), but \(\triangle XYZ\) is a different, long skinny shape — it is not similar to \(\triangle ABC\). c. \(\triangle SQR\) is similar to \(\triangle ABC\) with \(S \leftrightarrow A\), \(Q \leftrightarrow C\), \(R \leftrightarrow B\); since \(\triangle DEF\) is congruent to \(\triangle ABC\) with \(D \leftrightarrow A\), \(E \leftrightarrow B\), \(F \leftrightarrow C\), the angle at \(R\) corresponds to the angle at \(E\), i.e. \(\angle E\).
