Lesson Practice

Problems a - e

What transformation(s) could be used to position triangle I on triangle II? Explain all answers.

a.

Answer: Reflection

Explanation: Triangle I is flipped over the horizontal line segment it shares with Triangle II. This "flip" is a reflection across that horizontal axis.

b.

Answer: Translation

Explanation: Triangle I is simply slid to the right to position it over Triangle II. Its orientation and size do not change. A "slide" is called a translation.

c.

Answer: Rotation

Explanation: Triangle I is turned $90^\circ$ clockwise around the vertex with the right angle. This turning motion is a rotation.

d.

Answer: Reflection (and Translation)

Explanation: Triangle I is flipped over a vertical axis to create a mirror image, which is a reflection. Since there is a gap between them, it would be reflected across a line exactly halfway between them (or reflected over its vertical leg and then translated to the right).

e.

Answer: Rotation

Explanation: Triangle I is turned $180^\circ$ around the central shared vertex to exactly match the position of Triangle II.

Answer:

$\Delta ABC$: $A(-2, 4), B(-1, 1), C(-4, 2)$

$\Delta A'B'C'$: $A'(2, 4), B'(1, 1), C'(4, 2)$

Explanation: Read the coordinates directly from the given grid. The origin $(0,0)$ is where the thick axes cross. To reflect a point across the $y$-axis, the $x$-coordinate changes its sign while the $y$-coordinate remains exactly the same $(x, y) \rightarrow (-x, y)$.

Problem 1

What is the sum of the first five positive even numbers?

Answer: $30$

Explanation: The first five positive even numbers are $2, 4, 6, 8, \text{ and } 10$.
Sum = $2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 = 30$.

Problem 2

The team's win-loss ratio is 4 to 3. If the team has won 12 games, how many games has the team lost?

Answer: $9$ games lost

Explanation: Set up a proportion using the ratio of wins to losses: $\frac{4}{3}$.
Let $x$ be the number of lost games.
$$\frac{4}{3} = \frac{12}{x}$$ Multiply both sides by $3x$ (or cross-multiply):
$$4x = 36$$ $$x = 9$$ The team lost 9 games.

Problem 3

Five students were absent today. The teacher reported that 80% of the students were present. Find the number of students who were present and justify your answer.

Answer: $20$ students present

Explanation: If $80\%$ of the students are present, then $100\% - 80\% = 20\%$ of the students are absent.
We know the absent students equal $5$. Therefore, $20\%$ of the total class = $5$ students.
Since $20\% = \frac{1}{5}$, the total number of students in the class is $5 \times 5 = 25$.
To find the number of present students, subtract the absent students from the total: $25 - 5 = 20$.

Problem 4

Kaliska joined the band and got a new drum. Its diameter is 12 inches. What is the area of the top of the drum? Round your answer to the nearest square inch. (Use 3.14 for $\pi$.)

Answer: $113$ square inches

Explanation: First, find the radius. The radius is half of the diameter: $r = 12 / 2 = 6$ inches.
The formula for the area of a circle is $A = \pi r^2$.
$$A \approx 3.14 \times (6)^2$$ $$A \approx 3.14 \times 36$$ $$A \approx 113.04$$ Rounding to the nearest square inch, we get $113$ square inches.

Problem 5

Three eighths of the 48 band members played woodwinds. How many woodwind players were in the band?

Answer: $18$ woodwind players

Explanation: Multiply the total number of band members by the fraction that plays woodwinds.
$$\frac{3}{8} \times 48 = \frac{3 \times 48}{8}$$ Divide $48$ by $8$ first to make it easier: $48 / 8 = 6$.
$$3 \times 6 = 18$$

Problem 6

What is the least common multiple (LCM) of 6, 8, and 12?

Answer: $24$

Explanation: Find the prime factorization of each number:
$6 = 2 \times 3$
$8 = 2^3$
$12 = 2^2 \times 3$
The LCM must include the highest power of each prime factor present: $2^3 \times 3 = 8 \times 3 = 24$.

Problem 7

Triangles I and II are congruent. Describe the transformations that would position triangle I on triangle II.

Answer: Translation (right and up)

Explanation: Triangle I maintains its exact orientation and size. It has simply slid to the right and then slid up. Sliding a figure without rotating or flipping it is called a translation.

Problem 8

Complete this proportion: $\frac{0.7}{20} = \frac{n}{100}$

Answer: $n = 3.5$

Explanation: You can solve this by seeing the relationship between the denominators. $20 \times 5 = 100$. Therefore, multiply the numerator by $5$ as well:
$$n = 0.7 \times 5 = 3.5$$ Alternatively, cross-multiply:
$$20n = 0.7 \times 100$$ $$20n = 70$$ $$n = 70 / 20 = 3.5$$

Problem 12

$4 \frac{3}{4} + (2 \frac{1}{4} + \frac{7}{8})$

Answer: $7 \frac{7}{8}$

Explanation: First, find a common denominator (which is 8) for all fractions to make addition easier.
$$4 \frac{6}{8} + (2 \frac{2}{8} + \frac{7}{8})$$
Work inside the parentheses first:
$$2 \frac{2}{8} + \frac{7}{8} = 2 \frac{9}{8} = 3 \frac{1}{8}$$
Now add the outside number:
$$4 \frac{6}{8} + 3 \frac{1}{8} = 7 \frac{7}{8}$$

Problem 13

$1 \frac{1}{5} (2 \div 1 \frac{2}{3})$

Answer: $1 \frac{11}{25}$ or $\frac{36}{25}$

Explanation: First, convert mixed numbers to improper fractions:
$\frac{6}{5} (\frac{2}{1} \div \frac{5}{3})$
Do the division inside the parentheses by multiplying by the reciprocal:
$\frac{2}{1} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{6}{5}$
Now multiply the outside fraction by the result:
$$\frac{6}{5} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{36}{25}$$
Convert back to a mixed number: $\frac{36}{25} = 1 \frac{11}{25}$.

Problem 18

Arrange these numbers in order from least to greatest: $0.3, 0.31, 0.305$

Explanation: To compare them easily, give them all the same number of decimal places by adding zeros to the end:
$0.300$
$0.310$
$0.305$
Now compare the numbers like whole numbers: $300 < 305 < 310$. Therefore, the order is $0.3, 0.305, 0.31$.

Problem 19

If each edge of a cube is 10 centimeters long, then its volume is how many cubic centimeters?

Answer: $1000 \text{ cm}^3$

Explanation: The formula for the volume of a cube is $V = s^3$, where $s$ is the side length.
$$V = 10 \times 10 \times 10 = 1000$$
The volume is $1000$ cubic centimeters.

Problem 20

$2^5 - 5^2 + \sqrt{25} \times 2$

Answer: $17$

Explanation: Follow the order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS). Resolve exponents and roots first:
$2^5 = 32$
$5^2 = 25$
$\sqrt{25} = 5$
Substitute back into the equation:
$$32 - 25 + 5 \times 2$$
Next, perform multiplication:
$$5 \times 2 = 10$$
Now add and subtract from left to right:
$$32 - 25 + 10$$
$$7 + 10 = 17$$

Problem 21

Solve and check: $8a - 4 = 60$

Answer: $a = 8$

Explanation:
First, add $4$ to both sides of the equation to isolate the term with the variable:
$$8a = 64$$
Next, divide both sides by $8$:
$$a = 8$$
Check: Substitute $8$ back into the original equation for $a$:
$$8(8) - 4 = 60$$
$$64 - 4 = 60$$
$$60 = 60 \text{ (True)}$$

Problem 22

Acute angle $a$ is one third of a right angle. What is the measure of angle $a$?

Answer: $30^\circ$

Explanation: A right angle is exactly $90^\circ$. If angle $a$ is one third of a right angle, multiply $90$ by $\frac{1}{3}$ (or divide by 3).
$$\frac{90^\circ}{3} = 30^\circ$$

Problem 23

What is the perimeter of this polygon?

Answer: $100 \text{ mm}$

Explanation: The perimeter is the total distance around the outside of the shape. We need the lengths of all 6 sides. We are given 4 of them:
- Top horizontal: $30$
- Far Right vertical: $15$
- Inner horizontal: $10$
- Bottom horizontal: $20$
- Left vertical: $20$
Wait, the implicit vertical inner line must be calculated. Since the total left vertical side is $20$, and the far right vertical side is $15$, the inner vertical "step" is $20 - 15 = 5$.
Perimeter = $30 + 15 + 10 + 5 + 20 + 20 = 100 \text{ mm}$.

Problem 24

What is the area of this polygon?

Answer: $550 \text{ mm}^2$

Explanation: You can find the area by splitting the polygon into two simpler rectangles. Split it vertically:
1. Left rectangle: Width is $20$ (from the bottom), height is $20$ (from the left). Area = $20 \times 20 = 400 \text{ mm}^2$.
2. Right rectangle: The total width at the top is $30$, and the left piece is $20$, so the width of the right piece is $30 - 20 = 10$ (which matches the diagram label). The height is given as $15$. Area = $10 \times 15 = 150 \text{ mm}^2$.
Total Area = $400 + 150 = 550 \text{ mm}^2$.
(Alternatively, subtract the missing corner from a $30 \times 20$ large rectangle: $600 - (10 \times 5) = 550$).

Problem 25

A pint of water weighs about one pound. About how much does a two-gallon bucket of water weigh? (Disregard the weight of the bucket.)

Answer: About $16 \text{ pounds}$

Explanation: First, figure out how many pints are in $2$ gallons.
- There are $2$ pints in $1$ quart.
- There are $4$ quarts in $1$ gallon.
- So, there are $8$ pints in $1$ gallon ($2 \times 4 = 8$).
For $2$ gallons, there are $16$ pints ($8 \times 2 = 16$).
If $1$ pint weighs $1$ pound, then $16$ pints weigh $16$ pounds.

Problem 26

The parallel sides of this trapezoid are $10 \text{ mm}$ apart. The trapezoid is divided into two triangles. What is the area of the trapezoid?

Answer: $160 \text{ mm}^2$

Explanation: You can use the formula for the area of a trapezoid: $A = \frac{1}{2}h(b_1 + b_2)$.
Here, the height $h = 10 \text{ mm}$, and the bases $b_1 = 20 \text{ mm}$ and $b_2 = 12 \text{ mm}$.
$$A = \frac{1}{2}(10)(20 + 12)$$ $$A = 5 \times 32 = 160 \text{ mm}^2$$
(Alternatively, find the area of the two triangles formed by the dashed line: $\frac{1}{2}(20)(10) + \frac{1}{2}(12)(10) = 100 + 60 = 160$).

Problem 27

The cubic container shown can contain one liter of water. One liter is how many milliliters?

Answer: $1000 \text{ milliliters}$

Explanation: First, find the volume of the cube in cubic centimeters (cm³):
Volume = $10 \text{ cm} \times 10 \text{ cm} \times 10 \text{ cm} = 1000 \text{ cm}^3$.
One cubic centimeter ($1 \text{ cm}^3$) is exactly equal to one milliliter ($1 \text{ mL}$).
Since the cube holds $1000 \text{ cm}^3$, and is stated to hold $1$ liter, $1$ liter must equal $1000 \text{ mL}$. (The prefix "milli-" also means one-thousandth, so $1000 \text{ mL} = 1 \text{ L}$).

Problem 28

A bag contains 6 red marbles and 4 blue marbles. If Delia draws one marble from the bag and then draws another marble without replacing the first, what is the probability that both marbles will be red?

Answer: $\frac{1}{3}$

Explanation: This is a dependent probability problem.
1. Total marbles at start = $6 \text{ red} + 4 \text{ blue} = 10 \text{ marbles}$.
Probability of drawing a red marble first ($P(R_1)$) = $\frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5}$.
2. Since she doesn't replace it, there are now only $9$ marbles left, and $5$ of them are red.
Probability of drawing a red marble second ($P(R_2)$) = $\frac{5}{9}$.
3. Multiply the probabilities for both events to occur:
$$\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{9} = \frac{15}{45}$$
Simplify the fraction: Divide top and bottom by $15$ to get $\frac{1}{3}$.

Problem 30

On a coordinate plane draw triangle $RST$ with these vertices: $R(-1, 4), S(-3, 1), T(-1, 1)$. Then draw its reflection across the $y$-axis. Name the reflection $\Delta R'S'T'$. What are the coordinates of the vertices of $\Delta R'S'T'$?

Answer: $R'(1, 4), S'(3, 1), T'(1, 1)$

Explanation: To reflect a shape across the $y$-axis, you multiply the $x$-coordinate by $-1$ (change its sign) and keep the $y$-coordinate exactly the same.
$R(-1, 4) \rightarrow R'(1, 4)$
$S(-3, 1) \rightarrow S'(3, 1)$
$T(-1, 1) \rightarrow T'(1, 1)$
