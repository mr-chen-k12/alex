1 (11)

Octavio was writing a report on New Hampshire. He found that, in 2002, the population of Hanover, NH was 11,123. The population of Hollis, NH was 7416. The population of Newmarket, NH was 8449. What was the total population of these three places?

Answer: 26,988

Solution: \(11{,}123 + 7{,}416 + 8{,}449 = 26{,}988\).

2 (13, 16)

Rebecca and her mother built a shelf that was six feet long. How many inches long is this shelf?

Answer: 72 inches

Solution: One foot equals 12 inches, so \(6 \times 12 = 72\) inches.

3 (27)

Generalize If the cost of one dozen eggs was \$1.80, what was the cost per egg? Write an equivalent division problem that is easy to calculate mentally. Then find the quotient.

Answer: \$0.15 per egg

Solution: Cost per egg \(= 1.80 \div 12\). An equivalent problem: halve both numbers to get \(0.90 \div 6\), or divide cents: \(180\text{¢} \div 12 = 15\text{¢}\). Either way the quotient is \$0.15\.

4 (5, 20)

Which of the following equals one billion?

  1. \(10^3\)
  2. \(10^6\)
  3. \(10^9\)
  4. \(10^{12}\)

Answer: C — \(10^9\)

Solution: One billion is 1,000,000,000, which has 9 zeros: \(10^9\).

5 (22)

Read this statement and answer the questions that follow.

Three eighths of the 712 students bought their lunch.

a. How many students bought their lunch?

b. How many students did not buy their lunch?

Answer: a. 267 students   b. 445 students

Solution: a. \(\frac{3}{8} \times 712 = \frac{2136}{8} = 267\). b. \(712 - 267 = 445\).

6 (19, 20)

The width of this rectangle is 6 inches and its perimeter is 30 inches.

a. What is the length of the rectangle?

b. What is the area of the rectangle?

Answer: a. 9 inches   b. 54 square inches

Solution: a. \(P = 2l + 2w\), so \(30 = 2l + 12\), \(2l = 18\), \(l = 9\) in. b. \(A = 9 \times 6 = 54\) sq in.

7 (27)

Use prime factorization to find the least common multiple of 25 and 45.

Answer: 225

Solution: \(25 = 5^2\) and \(45 = 3^2 \cdot 5\). The LCM uses the highest power of each prime: \(3^2 \cdot 5^2 = 9 \cdot 25 = 225\).

8 (4)

What number is halfway between 3000 and 4000?

Answer: 3500

Solution: \(\frac{3000 + 4000}{2} = \frac{7000}{2} = 3500\).

9 (15, 24)

a. Write 24% as a reduced fraction.

b. Use prime factorization to reduce \(\frac{36}{180}\).

Answer: a. \(\frac{6}{25}\)   b. \(\frac{1}{5}\)

Solution: a. \(24\% = \frac{24}{100} = \frac{6}{25}\). b. \(36 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2\), \(180 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5\); cancel \(2^2 \cdot 3^2\) to get \(\frac{1}{5}\).

10 (16)

It was a very hot day. The temperature was 102°F in the shade.

a. The temperature was how many degrees above the freezing point of water?

b. The temperature was how many degrees below the boiling point of water?

c. Discuss What additional information did we need to know to answer a and b?

Answer: a. 70°F above   b. 110°F below   c. The freezing point (32°F) and boiling point (212°F) of water

Solution: a. \(102 - 32 = 70\). b. \(212 - 102 = 110\). c. We needed the reference points: water freezes at 32°F and boils at 212°F.

11 (2, 15)

For each fraction, write an equivalent fraction that has a denominator of 36.

a. \(\frac{5}{12}\)    b. \(\frac{1}{6}\)    c. \(\frac{7}{9}\)

d. Analyze What property do we use when we find equivalent fractions?

Answer: a. \(\frac{15}{36}\)   b. \(\frac{6}{36}\)   c. \(\frac{28}{36}\)   d. The Identity Property of Multiplication

Solution: a. \(\frac{5}{12} \times \frac{3}{3} = \frac{15}{36}\). b. \(\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{6}{6} = \frac{6}{36}\). c. \(\frac{7}{9} \times \frac{4}{4} = \frac{28}{36}\). d. Multiplying by a form of 1 (such as \(\frac{3}{3}\)) does not change a number's value.

12 (21)

a. Generalize Write the prime factorization of 576 using exponents.

b. Find \(\sqrt{576}\).

Answer: a. \(2^6 \cdot 3^2\)   b. 24

Solution: a. \(576 = 64 \times 9 = 2^6 \cdot 3^2\). b. \(\sqrt{576} = \sqrt{2^6 \cdot 3^2} = 2^3 \cdot 3 = 8 \cdot 3 = 24\).

13 (26)

Write \(5\frac{5}{6}\) and \(6\frac{6}{7}\) as improper fractions and find their product.

Answer: Improper fractions: \(\frac{35}{6}\) and \(\frac{48}{7}\); product: 40

Solution: \(5\frac{5}{6} = \frac{35}{6}\), \(6\frac{6}{7} = \frac{48}{7}\). Product: \(\frac{35}{6} \cdot \frac{48}{7} = \frac{1680}{42} = 40\). (Cancel first: \(\frac{5 \cdot 8}{1 \cdot 1} = 40\).)

14 (7)

In the figure below, quadrilaterals ABCF and FCDE are squares. Refer to the figure to answer problems 14–16.

a. What kind of angle is \(\angle ACD\)?

b. Name two segments parallel to \(\overline{FC}\).

Answer: a. Obtuse angle   b. \(\overline{ED}\) and \(\overline{AB}\)

Solution: a. \(\overline{CA}\) is a diagonal of square ABCF, so it makes a 45° angle with the base; \(\angle ACD = 180° - 45° = 135°\), which is obtuse. b. \(\overline{FC}\) is vertical, and the other vertical sides \(\overline{ED}\) and \(\overline{AB}\) are parallel to it.

15 (8)

a. What fraction of square CDEF is shaded?

b. What fraction of square ABCF is shaded?

c. What fraction of rectangle ABDE is shaded?

Answer: a. \(\frac{1}{2}\)   b. \(\frac{1}{2}\)   c. \(\frac{1}{2}\)

Solution: a. \(\overline{EC}\) is a diagonal of square CDEF, so triangle EFC is half the square. b. \(\overline{AC}\) is a diagonal of square ABCF, so triangle AFC is half the square. c. The shaded triangle ECA has base \(EA\) (two square-sides) and height one square-side, so its area equals one whole square — half of the two-square rectangle.

16 (19, 20)

If AB is 3 ft,

a. what is the perimeter of rectangle ABDE?

b. what is the area of rectangle ABDE?

Answer: a. 18 ft   b. 18 sq ft

Solution: Each square has side 3 ft, so rectangle ABDE is 6 ft by 3 ft. a. \(P = 2(6 + 3) = 18\) ft. b. \(A = 6 \times 3 = 18\) sq ft.

17 (3)

Solve: \(10y = 360^\circ\)

Answer: \(y = 36^\circ\)

Solution: \(y = 360^\circ \div 10 = 36^\circ\).

18 (3, 20)

Solve: \(p + 2^4 = 12^2\)

Answer: \(p = 128\)

Solution: \(2^4 = 16\) and \(12^2 = 144\), so \(p + 16 = 144\) and \(p = 128\).

19 (23)

Solve: \(5\frac{1}{8} - n = 1\frac{3}{8}\)

Answer: \(n = 3\frac{3}{4}\)

Solution: \(n = 5\frac{1}{8} - 1\frac{3}{8} = 4\frac{9}{8} - 1\frac{3}{8} = 3\frac{6}{8} = 3\frac{3}{4}\).

24 (9, 15) Simplify

Simplify: \(5\frac{1}{4} \div 3\)

Answer: \(1\frac{3}{4}\)

Solution: \(\frac{21}{4} \div 3 = \frac{21}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{21}{12} = \frac{7}{4} = 1\frac{3}{4}\).

25 (24) Simplify

Simplify: \(\frac{5}{4} \cdot \frac{9}{8} \cdot \frac{4}{15}\)

Answer: \(\frac{3}{8}\)

Solution: Cancel the 4s: \(\frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{9}{15} = \frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{3}{5}\); cancel the 5s: \(\frac{3}{8}\).

26 (9, 15) Simplify

Simplify: \(\frac{8}{9} - \left(\frac{7}{9} - \frac{5}{9}\right)\)

Answer: \(\frac{2}{3}\)

Solution: Work inside the parentheses first: \(\frac{7}{9} - \frac{5}{9} = \frac{2}{9}\). Then \(\frac{8}{9} - \frac{2}{9} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}\).

27 (Inv. 2)

If the diameter of a circle is half of a yard, then its radius is how many inches?

Answer: 9 inches

Solution: Half a yard \(= 18\) inches, so the diameter is 18 in. The radius is half the diameter: \(18 \div 2 = 9\) in.

28 (27)

Generalize Divide \$12.00 by 16 or find the quotient of an equivalent division problem.

Answer: \$0.75

Solution: An easier equivalent problem: halve both numbers to get \(6.00 \div 8 = 0.75\) (halve again: \(3.00 \div 4 = 0.75\)). So \(12.00 \div 16 = 0.75\), or \$0.75\.

29 (19, 20)

A 3-by-3-in. paper square is cut from a 5-by-5-in. paper square as shown.

a. What is the perimeter of the resulting polygon?

b. How many square inches of the 5-by-5-in. square remain?

Answer: a. 20 inches   b. 16 square inches

Solution: a. Tracing the L-shape: \(5 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 5 = 20\) in. (Cutting a square from the corner removes 3 in. from each of two sides but adds two 3-in. cut edges, so the perimeter is unchanged from the original 20 in.) b. \(25 - 9 = 16\) sq in. remain.

30 (Inv. 2)

Classify Refer to this circle with center at point M to answer a–e:

a. Which chord is a diameter?

b. Which chord is not a diameter?

c. What angle is an acute central angle?

d. Which angles are inscribed angles?

e. Which two sides of triangle AMB are equal in length?

Answer: a. \(\overline{CB}\)   b. \(\overline{AB}\)   c. \(\angle AMB\)   d. \(\angle MAB\) (\(\angle CAB\)) and \(\angle ABM\) (\(\angle CBA\))   e. \(\overline{AM}\) and \(\overline{MB}\)

Solution: a. \(\overline{CB}\) passes through the center \(M\). b. \(\overline{AB}\) does not pass through the center. c. \(\angle AMB\) has its vertex at the center and measures less than 90°. d. Inscribed angles have vertices on the circle: \(\angle MAB\) at \(A\) and \(\angle ABM\) at \(B\). e. \(\overline{AM}\) and \(\overline{MB}\) are both radii of the circle, so they are equal.
