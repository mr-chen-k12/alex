1.(28)
* The 5 starters on the basketball team were tall. Their heights were 76 inches, 77 inches, 77 inches, 78 inches, and 82 inches. What was the average height of the 5 starters?
Answer
\(78\text{ inches}\)
Solution
Add the heights: \(76+77+77+78+82 = 390\). Divide by 5: \(390 \div 5 = 78\) inches.
2.(28)
* Marie bought 6 pounds of carrots for \$0.87 per pound and paid for them with a \$10 bill. How much did she get back in change?
Answer
\(\$4.78\)
Solution
Cost: \(6 \times \$0.87 = \$5.22\). Change: \(\$10.00 - \$5.22 = \$4.78\).
3.(29)
* Verify While helping her father build a stone fence, Tanisha lifted 17 rocks averaging 8 pounds each. She calculated that she had lifted over 2000 pounds in all. Her father thought Tanisha’s calculation was unreasonable. Do you agree or disagree with Tanisha’s father? Why?
Answer
\(\text{Agree with her father}\)
Solution
Total weight: \(17 \times 8 = 136\) pounds — nowhere near 2000. To lift 2000 pounds at 8 pounds per rock, she would need about 250 rocks. Her father is right; the calculation is unreasonable.
4.(14, 24)
One hundred forty of the two hundred sixty students in the auditorium were not seventh graders. What fraction of the students in the auditorium were seventh graders?
Answer
\(\dfrac{6}{13}\)
Solution
Seventh graders: \(260 - 140 = 120\). Fraction: \(\frac{120}{260} = \frac{12}{26} = \frac{6}{13}\).
5.(22)
In the following statement, change the percent to a reduced fraction. Then answer the questions.
The Daltons completed 30% of their 2140-mile trip the first day.
a. How many miles did they travel the first day?
b. How many miles of their trip do they still have to travel?
Answer
\(30\% = \dfrac{3}{10};\quad \text{a. }642\text{ miles};\quad \text{b. }1498\text{ miles}\)
Solution
\(30\% = \frac{30}{100} = \frac{3}{10}\). a. \(\frac{3}{10} \times 2140 = 642\) miles. b. \(2140 - 642 = 1498\) miles still to travel.
6.(16, 19)
If the perimeter of a square is 5 feet, how many inches long is each side of the square?
Answer
\(15\text{ inches}\)
Solution
Each side: \(5 \div 4 = 1\frac{1}{4}\) feet. Convert: \(1\frac{1}{4} \times 12 = 15\) inches.
7.(30)
* Generalize Use prime factorization to subtract these fractions: \(\dfrac{1}{18} - \dfrac{1}{30}\)
Answer
\(\dfrac{1}{45}\)
Solution
\(18 = 2 \cdot 3^2\) and \(30 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5\), so the LCM is \(2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 90\). \(\frac{1}{18} - \frac{1}{30} = \frac{5}{90} - \frac{3}{90} = \frac{2}{90} = \frac{1}{45}\).
8.(29)
* Mt. Whitney in California is 14,494 ft high.
a. What is Mt. Whitney’s height to the nearest thousand feet?
b. What is Mt. Whitney’s height to the nearest hundred feet?
Answer
\(\text{a. }14{,}000\text{ ft}\qquad\text{b. }14{,}500\text{ ft}\)
Solution
a. The hundreds digit is 4, so round down: 14,000 ft. b. The tens digit is 9, so round up: 14,500 ft.
9.(29)
* Estimate Martin used a calculator to divide 28,910 by 49. The answer displayed was 59. Did Martin enter the problem correctly? (Use estimation to determine whether the displayed answer is reasonable.)
Answer
\(\text{No — the answer should be about }600\)
Solution
Estimate: \(28{,}910 \div 49 \approx 30{,}000 \div 50 = 600\). The displayed 59 is only about a tenth of that, so it is not reasonable — Martin entered the problem incorrectly. (The exact quotient is \(590\), since \(49 \times 590 = 28{,}910\).)
10.(15, 24)
a. Write 32% as a reduced fraction.
b. Use prime factorization to reduce \(\dfrac{48}{72}\).
Answer
\(\text{a. }\dfrac{8}{25}\qquad\text{b. }\dfrac{2}{3}\)
Solution
a. \(32\% = \frac{32}{100} = \frac{8}{25}\). b. \(48 = 2^4 \cdot 3\) and \(72 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2\). Cancel the common factors \(2^3 \cdot 3\): \(\frac{48}{72} = \frac{2}{3}\).
11.(30)
Write these fractions so that they have common denominators. Then compare the fractions. \(\dfrac{5}{6} \bigcirc \dfrac{7}{8}\)
Answer
\(\dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{20}{24} < \dfrac{21}{24} = \dfrac{7}{8}\)
Solution
LCM of 6 and 8 is 24. \(\frac{5}{6} = \frac{20}{24}\) and \(\frac{7}{8} = \frac{21}{24}\). Since \(20 < 21\), \(\frac{5}{6} < \frac{7}{8}\).

In the figure, a 3-by-3-in. square is joined to a 4-by-4-in. square.
a. What is the area of the smaller square?
b. What is the area of the larger square?
c. What is the total area of the figure?

13.(19)
* a. What is the perimeter of the hexagon that is formed by joining the two squares?
b. The perimeter of the hexagon is how many inches less than the combined perimeter of the two squares?
c. Justify Explain your answer to b.
Answer
\(\text{a. }22\text{ in.}\qquad\text{b. }6\text{ in. less}\)
Solution
a. Trace the outline: \(3 + 3 + 1 + 4 + 4 + 7 = 22\) inches. b. Combined perimeters: \((4 \times 3) + (4 \times 4) = 12 + 16 = 28\) in. \(28 - 22 = 6\) inches less. c. The 3-inch side where the squares join is counted in both squares’ perimeters (\(2 \times 3 = 6\) in.), but it lies inside the joined figure, so it is not part of the hexagon’s perimeter.
14.(21)
a. Write the prime factorization of 5184 using exponents.
b. Use the answer to a to find \(\sqrt{5184}\).
Answer
\(\text{a. }2^6 \cdot 3^4\qquad\text{b. }72\)
Solution
a. \(5184 = 64 \times 81 = 2^6 \cdot 3^4\). b. Halve the exponents: \(\sqrt{5184} = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 = 8 \cdot 9 = 72\). Check: \(72^2 = 5184\).
15.(28)
* What is the mean of 5, 7, 9, 11, 12, 13, 24, 25, 26, and 28?
Answer
\(16\)
Solution
Sum: \(5+7+9+11+12+13+24+25+26+28 = 160\). There are 10 numbers, so the mean is \(160 \div 10 = 16\).
16.(28)
List the single-digit divisors of 5670.
Answer
\(1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7,\ 9\)
Solution
\(5670 = 2 \cdot 3^4 \cdot 5 \cdot 7\). Test digits 1–9: 5670 is divisible by 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, and 9 (but not 4 or 8, since \(5670 \div 4\) and \(5670 \div 8\) are not whole numbers).
17.(3, 20)
Solve: \(6w = 6^3\)
Answer
\(w = 36\)
Solution
\(6^3 = 216\), so \(6w = 216\). Divide both sides by 6: \(w = 36\).
18.(3)
Solve: \(90^\circ + 30^\circ + a = 180^\circ\)
Answer
\(a = 60^\circ\)
Solution
\(90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ\), so \(a = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ\).
19.(3, 13)
Formulate Write an equal groups word problem for this equation and solve the problem. \(36x = \$45.00\)
Answer
\(x = \$1.25\)
Solution
Sample problem: Thirty-six identical concert tickets cost \$45.00 in all. What is the cost of one ticket? Divide: \(x = 45.00 \div 36 = 1.25\). One ticket costs \$1.25. (Check: \(36 \times 1.25 = 45.00\).) Answers may vary.
