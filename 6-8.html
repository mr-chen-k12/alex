Problem 1
When the product of 4 and 4 is divided by the sum of 4 and 4, what is the quotient?
Answer: 2
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Find the product of 4 and 4:
    $$4 \times 4 = 16$$
  • Find the sum of 4 and 4:
    $$4 + 4 = 8$$
  • Divide the product by the sum to get the final quotient:
    $$16 \div 8 = 2$$
Problem 2
SUMMARIZE If you know the subtrahend and the difference, how can you find the minuend? Write a complete sentence to answer the question.
Answer: You can find the minuend by adding the subtrahend and the difference together.
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Recall the foundational structural arrangement of a subtraction operation:
    $$\text{Minuend} - \text{Subtrahend} = \text{Difference}$$
  • Isolate the unknown value by performing the inverse operation (addition):
    $$\text{Minuend} = \text{Subtrahend} + \text{Difference}$$
Problem 3
ANALYZE Which property of addition is stated by this equation?
$$\quad (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$$
Answer: Associative Property of Addition
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Notice that the sequence of elements stays identical on both sides, while the grouping parentheses shift positions.
  • The algebraic property confirming that changing grouping arrangements does not modify the structural sum is the Associative Property of Addition.

Problem 5
GENERALIZE Use the numbers 3 and 4 to illustrate the Commutative Property of Multiplication. Use a center dot to indicate multiplication.

Answer: \(3 \cdot 4 = 4 \cdot 3\)
Step-by-Step Explanation:

Problem 6
JUSTIFY List the properties used in the first and second steps to simplify the expression \(5 + (x + 7)\).
Step:
\(5 + (x + 7)\) — Given expression
\(5 + (7 + x)\) — a. _________
\((5 + 7) + x\) — b. _________
\(12 + x\) — \(5 + 7 = 12\)
Answer: a. Commutative Property of Addition | b. Associative Property of Addition
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Step a: The positions of \(x\) and \(7\) inside the grouping container are flipped. This is justified by the Commutative Property of Addition.
  • Step b: The items remain in place, but the grouping brackets migrate to wrap around the first two values. This is justified by the Associative Property of Addition.

Real-World Connection
Jessica and her friend went to the local high school football game. The price of admission was \$4.50. They each bought a bottle of water for \$1.25 and a pickle. Together they spent a total of \$13.50.

a. Find the cost of each pickle.
b. Jessica had \$10 to spend at the game. How much should she have left after buying her ticket, the water, and a pickle?

Answer: a. \$1.00 per pickle  |  b. \$3.25 remaining
Step-by-Step Explanation:
  • Part a: Find the total subtotal for two tickets and two bottles of water:
    $$\text{Tickets} = 2 \times \$4.50 = \$9.00$$
    $$\text{Water} = 2 \times \$1.25 = \$2.50$$
    $$\text{Combined Subtotal} = \$9.00 + \$2.50 = \$11.50$$
  • Subtract this subtotal from the grand total to find the cost of both pickles:
    $$\$13.50 - \$11.50 = \$2.00 \text{ (for 2 pickles)} \implies \$2.00 \div 2 = \$1.00 \text{ each}$$
  • Part b: Sum the costs of Jessica's individual purchases:
    $$\text{Individual Total} = \$4.50 + \$1.25 + \$1.00 = \$6.75$$
  • Subtract her total spending from her initial \$10 bill to find her remaining change:
    $$\$10.00 - \$6.75 = \$3.25$$
