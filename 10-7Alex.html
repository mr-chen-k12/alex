Written Practice

1

In Room 7 there are 28 students. In Room 9 there are 30 students. In Room 11 there are 23 students. How many students are in all three rooms?

Answer: 81 students

Solution: Add the three rooms: \(28 + 30 + 23 = 58 + 23 = 81\).

2

If the total number of students in problem 1 were equally divided among three rooms, how many students would be in each room?

Answer: 27 students

Solution: Divide the total from problem 1 equally: \(81 \div 3 = 27\).

3

The largest state is Alaska. It has an area of about 663,000 square miles. The smallest state, Rhode Island, has an area of about 1,500 square miles. About how many more square miles is Alaska than Rhode Island?

Answer: About 661,500 square miles

Solution: Subtract: \(663{,}000 - 1{,}500 = 661{,}500\). Since the given areas are already rounded ("about"), the answer is about 661,500 square miles.

4

a. Write the formula for the perimeter of a square.

b. A landscape planner designed a square garden that is 24 feet long per side. How many feet of border are needed to surround the garden?

Answer: a. \(P = 4s\)   b. 96 feet

Solution: a. A square has 4 equal sides, so perimeter \(= 4 \times \text{side}\), or \(P = 4s\).
b. \(P = 4 \times 24 = 96\) feet of border.

5 Model

Diagram this statement. Then answer the questions that follow.
Five ninths of the 36 spectators were happy with the outcome.

a. How many spectators were happy with the outcome?

b. How many spectators were not happy with the outcome?

Answer: a. 20 spectators   b. 16 spectators

Solution: a. \(\frac{5}{9} \times 36 = \frac{5 \times 36}{9} = \frac{180}{9} = 20\) happy spectators.
b. \(36 - 20 = 16\) spectators were not happy (equivalently, \(\frac{4}{9} \times 36 = 16\)).

6

In the following statement, change the percent to a reduced fraction. Then diagram the statement and answer the questions.
Twenty-five percent of three dozen plants are blooming.

a. What fraction of the total number of plants are not blooming?

b. How many plants are not blooming?

Answer: \(25\% = \frac{1}{4}\)   a. \(\frac{3}{4}\)   b. 27 plants

Solution: \(25\% = \frac{25}{100} = \frac{1}{4}\). Three dozen = \(3 \times 12 = 36\) plants.
a. Not blooming \(= 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}\) of the plants.
b. \(\frac{3}{4} \times 36 = 27\) plants are not blooming (9 are blooming).

7

a. What fraction of the rectangle is shaded?

b. What percent of the rectangle is not shaded?

Answer: a. \(\frac{2}{5}\)   b. 60%

Solution: a. 4 of the 10 equal parts are shaded: \(\frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}\).
b. \(10 - 4 = 6\) parts are not shaded: \(\frac{6}{10} = \frac{60}{100} = 60\%\).

8

a. How many \(\frac{1}{4}\)s are in 1?

b. Explain Explain how you can use the answer to part a to find the number of \(\frac{1}{4}\)s in 3.

Answer: a. 4   b. Multiply by 3: \(3 \times 4 = 12\)

Solution: a. \(1 \div \frac{1}{4} = 4\) — it takes four quarters to make one whole.
b. Since each whole contains four \(\frac{1}{4}\)s, three wholes contain \(3 \times 4 = 12\) quarters: \(3 \div \frac{1}{4} = 12\).

9

a. Multiply: \(6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 0\)

b. Analyze What property is illustrated by the multiplication in part a?

Answer: a. 0   b. Zero property of multiplication

Solution: a. Because one factor is 0, the entire product is 0.
b. The zero property of multiplication: any number multiplied by 0 equals 0.

10 Analyze

Simplify and compare: \(\frac{3}{3} - \left(\frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{1}\right) \bigcirc \left(\frac{3}{3} - \frac{1}{3}\right) \cdot \frac{3}{1}\)

Answer: \(\frac{3}{3} - \left(\frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{1}\right) < \left(\frac{3}{3} - \frac{1}{3}\right) \cdot \frac{3}{1}\)   (that is, \(0 < 2\))

Solution: Left side: \(\frac{3}{3} - \left(\frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{1}\right) = 1 - \frac{3}{3} = 1 - 1 = 0\).
Right side: \(\left(\frac{3}{3} - \frac{1}{3}\right) \cdot \frac{3}{1} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 3 = 2\).
Since \(0 < 2\), the correct symbol is \(<\).

11

Draw a rectangle \(ABCD\) so that \(AB\) is 2 in. and \(BC\) is 1 in.

a. What is the perimeter of rectangle \(ABCD\)?

b. What is the area of the rectangle?

c. What is the sum of the measures of all four angles of the rectangle?

Answer: a. 6 in.   b. 2 sq in.   c. 360°

Solution: a. \(P = 2(2 + 1) = 6\) inches.
b. \(A = 2 \times 1 = 2\) square inches.
c. Each angle of a rectangle is 90°, so \(4 \times 90° = 360°\).

12 Generalize

Write the prime factorization of each number:

a. 32    b. 900    c. \(\sqrt{900}\)

Answer: a. \(2^5\)   b. \(2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^2\)   c. \(2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5\)

Solution: a. \(32 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 2^5\).
b. \(900 = 9 \cdot 100 = (3 \cdot 3) \cdot (2 \cdot 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 5) = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^2\).
c. \(\sqrt{900} = 30 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5\).

13

For each fraction, write an equivalent fraction that has a denominator of 60.

a. \(\frac{5}{6}\)    b. \(\frac{3}{5}\)    c. \(\frac{7}{12}\)

Answer: a. \(\frac{50}{60}\)   b. \(\frac{36}{60}\)   c. \(\frac{35}{60}\)

Solution: a. \(\frac{5}{6} = \frac{5 \times 10}{6 \times 10} = \frac{50}{60}\).
b. \(\frac{3}{5} = \frac{3 \times 12}{5 \times 12} = \frac{36}{60}\).
c. \(\frac{7}{12} = \frac{7 \times 5}{12 \times 5} = \frac{35}{60}\).

14

Add the three fractions with denominators of 60 from problem 13; and write their sum as a mixed number.

Answer: \(2\frac{1}{60}\)

Solution: \(\frac{50}{60} + \frac{36}{60} + \frac{35}{60} = \frac{121}{60}\). Since \(121 = 2 \times 60 + 1\), \(\frac{121}{60} = 2\frac{1}{60}\).

15

a. Arrange these numbers in order from least to greatest:

\[0,\ -\frac{2}{3},\ 1,\ \frac{3}{2},\ -2\]

b. Which of these numbers are positive?

Answer: a. \(-2,\ -\frac{2}{3},\ 0,\ 1,\ \frac{3}{2}\)   b. \(1\) and \(\frac{3}{2}\)

Solution: a. On the number line, negatives are left of 0: \(-2 < -\frac{2}{3} < 0 < 1 < \frac{3}{2}\) (note \(\frac{3}{2} = 1\frac{1}{2} > 1\)).
b. Positive numbers are greater than 0: \(1\) and \(\frac{3}{2}\).

16 Predict

If one card is drawn from a regular deck of cards, what is the probability the card will be a heart?

Answer: \(\frac{1}{4}\)

Solution: A regular deck has 52 cards with 13 hearts. \(P(\text{heart}) = \frac{13}{52} = \frac{1}{4}\).

20

\(10^2 \cdot 10^5 = 10^n\)

Answer: \(n = 7\)

Solution: When multiplying powers of 10, add the exponents: \(10^2 \cdot 10^5 = 10^{2+5} = 10^7\), so \(n = 7\).

Answer: \(2\frac{1}{2}\)

Solution: \(\frac{5}{6} + \frac{5}{6} + \frac{5}{6} = \frac{15}{6} = \frac{5}{2} = 2\frac{1}{2}\).

22

\(\frac{15}{2} \cdot \frac{10}{3}\)

Answer: 25

Solution: \(\frac{15}{2} \cdot \frac{10}{3} = \frac{15 \times 10}{2 \times 3} = \frac{150}{6} = 25\). (Cancel first: \(\frac{15}{3} = 5\) and \(\frac{10}{2} = 5\), so \(5 \times 5 = 25\).)

23

\(\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2\)

Answer: \(\frac{25}{36}\)

Solution: Square the numerator and the denominator: \(\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2 = \frac{5^2}{6^2} = \frac{25}{36}\).

24

\(\sqrt{30^2}\)

Answer: 30

Solution: The square root undoes the square: \(\sqrt{30^2} = 30\).

25 Justify

Give reasons for the steps used to simplify the following expression by using the commutative, associative, inverse, and identity properties of multiplication.

\[\frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{3}\]

Answer: \(\frac{2}{3}\)

Solution: Simplify with reasons for each step:

  1. \(\frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{3}\) — commutative property (reorder the factors).
  2. \(= \left(\frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{3}\right) \cdot \frac{2}{3}\) — associative property (regroup the factors).
  3. \(= 1 \cdot \frac{2}{3}\) — inverse property (\(\frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{3} = 1\)).
  4. \(= \frac{2}{3}\) — identity property (\(1 \times\) any number is that number).
26

Write \(1\frac{1}{2}\) and \(1\frac{2}{3}\) as improper fractions. Then multiply the improper fractions, and write the product as a mixed number.

Answer: \(2\frac{1}{2}\)

Solution: \(1\frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}\) and \(1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{3}\). Multiply: \(\frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{3} = \frac{15}{6} = \frac{5}{2} = 2\frac{1}{2}\).

27

A package that weighs 1 lb 5 oz weighs how many ounces?

Answer: 21 ounces

Solution: 1 pound = 16 ounces, so \(16 + 5 = 21\) ounces.

28

Use a protractor to draw a 45° angle.

Answer: A 45° angle (as drawn above)

Solution: Place the protractor's center on the vertex and its 0° mark along one ray. Mark a dot at 45°, remove the protractor, and draw the second ray from the vertex through the dot. The angle between the rays measures 45° — half of a right angle.

29 Justify

Find the next number in this sequence and explain how you found your answer.

\[\ldots,\ 100,\ 10,\ 1,\ \frac{1}{10},\ \ldots\]

Answer: \(\frac{1}{100}\)

Solution: Each term is \(\frac{1}{10}\) of the one before it: \(100 \div 10 = 10\), \(10 \div 10 = 1\), \(1 \div 10 = \frac{1}{10}\). Continuing the pattern, \(\frac{1}{10} \div 10 = \frac{1}{100}\).

30

Write an odd negative integer greater than \(-3\).

Answer: \(-1\)

Solution: Integers greater than \(-3\) are \(-2, -1, 0, 1, \ldots\). Of these, the negative ones are \(-2\) and \(-1\); \(-1\) is odd (and \(-2\) is even). So the answer is \(-1\).

Real-World Connection

Real-World Connection

This weekend workers will cut the grass on the high school football field and repaint the white outline around the field.

a. The field is 360 feet long and 160 feet wide. Find the perimeter and area of the field.

b. One quart of paint is enough to paint a 200 ft stripe. How many quarts of paint should be purchased to paint a stripe around the entire field? Show your work.

c. If it takes a large mower 25 seconds to mow 800 ft2, how long will it take to mow the whole field? Show your work. (Assume the paths are cut with no overlap.)

Answer: a. \(P = 1040\) ft, \(A = 57{,}600\) ft²   b. 6 quarts   c. 30 minutes

Solution:
a. \(P = 2(360 + 160) = 2(520) = 1040\) ft. \(A = 360 \times 160 = 57{,}600\) ft².
b. \(\frac{1040}{200} = 5.2\) quarts needed. Since paint can't be bought in fractions of a quart here and 5 quarts would not be enough, purchase 6 quarts.
c. \(\frac{57{,}600}{800} = 72\) mower passes; \(72 \times 25 = 1800\) seconds \(= \frac{1800}{60} = 30\) minutes.

Motivating Mindset

Motivating Mindset

What is a math skill you would like to understand better?

Answer: Any honest answer — there is no single correct response.

Solution: This question asks you to reflect. A good answer names a specific skill and says why, for example: "I want to understand dividing fractions better because I mix up when to flip the second fraction," or "I want to get better at word problems so I know which operation to use." Pick the skill that feels hardest to you right now.
