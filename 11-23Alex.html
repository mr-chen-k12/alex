g. CONNECT Use the distance formula to find how far Chris could ride in \(6\) hours at an average rate of \(14\) mph.

The distance formula is \(d = rt\), where \(r\) is the rate and \(t\) is the time.

Substitute \(r = 14\) mph and \(t = 6\) hours:

\[ d = rt = 14 \times 6 = 84 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{84\text{ miles}}\)

Written Practice

1. Brand X costs \(\$2.40\) for \(16\) ounces. Find the unit price.

The unit price is the total price divided by the number of ounces:

\[ \frac{\$2.40}{16} = \$0.15 \text{ per ounce} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\$0.15\text{ per ounce}}\)

2. Germany’s Autobahn is the world’s second largest superhighway system. A car traveled \(702\) kilometers along the Autobahn in \(6\) hours. How many kilometers per hour did the car average?

Average speed \(=\) distance \(\div\) time:

\[ \frac{702\text{ km}}{6\text{ h}} = 117 \text{ km per hour} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{117\text{ kilometers per hour}}\)

3. REPRESENT A penny, a nickel, and a dime are flipped once. Make a tree diagram to find the possible outcomes. Then write the sample space for the experiment, recording each outcome in penny, nickel, dime order. (For example, one outcome is HTH meaning heads for the penny, tails for the nickel, and heads for the dime.)

Each coin has \(2\) possible results, so the number of outcomes is

\[ 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8 \]

Reading each path from left to right gives the sample space:

Answer: \(\boxed{\{\text{HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT, THH, THT, TTH, TTT}\}}\)

4. At four different stores the price of \(1\) gallon of milk was \(\$2.86\), \(\$2.83\), \(\$2.98\), and \(\$3.09\). Find the average price per gallon rounded to the nearest cent.

First add the four prices:

\[ 2.86 + 2.83 + 2.98 + 3.09 = 11.76 \]

Then divide by \(4\) stores:

\[ \frac{11.76}{4} = 2.94 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\$2.94\text{ per gallon}}\)

5. Two and three hundredths is how much less than three and two tenths? Write the answer in words.

Write both numbers as decimals: two and three hundredths is \(2.03\), and three and two tenths is \(3.2\).

\[ 3.2 - 2.03 = 1.17 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{one and seventeen hundredths}}\)

6. A math book is \(1\frac{1}{2}\) inches thick. How many math books will fit on a shelf that is \(2\) feet long?

Change the shelf length to inches: \(2\) feet \(= 24\) inches.

Divide the shelf length by the thickness of one book:

\[ 24 \div 1\frac{1}{2} = 24 \div \frac{3}{2} = 24 \times \frac{2}{3} = 16 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{16\text{ books}}\)

7. Read this statement. Then answer the questions that follow.

Three eighths of the \(48\) roses were red.

a. How many roses were red?

b. What was the ratio of red to not red roses?

c. What fraction of the roses were not red?

a. Find \(\frac{3}{8}\) of \(48\):

\[ \frac{3}{8} \times 48 = 18 \text{ red roses} \]

b. Roses that were not red: \(48 - 18 = 30\). The ratio of red to not red is

\[ 18 : 30 = 3 : 5 \]

c. The fraction not red is

\[ \frac{30}{48} = \frac{5}{8} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 18 \qquad \text{b. } 3:5 \qquad \text{c. } \frac{5}{8}}\)

8. Replace each circle with the proper comparison symbol:

a. \(3.0303 \bigcirc 3.303\)

b. \(0.\overline{6} \bigcirc 0.600\)

a. Compare place by place. The ones digits are equal, but in the tenths place \(0 < 3\), so \(3.0303\) is the smaller number.

b. \(0.\overline{6} = 0.666\ldots\), which is greater than \(0.600\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 3.0303 < 3.303 \qquad \text{b. } 0.\overline{6} > 0.600}\)

9. From goal line to goal line, a football field is \(100\) yards long. How many feet long is a football field?

There are \(3\) feet in \(1\) yard, so multiply:

\[ 100 \times 3 = 300 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{300\text{ feet}}\)

10. a. Write \(0.080\) as a fraction.

b. Write \(37\frac{1}{2}\%\) as a decimal.

c. Write \(\frac{1}{11}\) as a decimal with a bar over the repetend.

a. \(0.080\) is eighty thousandths. Reduce by dividing the numerator and denominator by \(40\):

\[ 0.080 = \frac{80}{1000} = \frac{2}{25} \]

b. Move the decimal point two places to the left to change a percent to a decimal: \(37\frac{1}{2}\% = 37.5\% = 0.375\).

c. Divide \(1 \div 11\). The digits \(09\) repeat:

\[ \frac{1}{11} = 0.090909\ldots = 0.\overline{09} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } \frac{2}{25} \qquad \text{b. } 0.375 \qquad \text{c. } 0.\overline{09}}\)

11. ANALYZE Archie earns \(\$9.50\) per hour helping a house painter. How much does Archie earn in an \(8\)-hour workday? Tell why your answer is reasonable.

Multiply the hourly pay by the number of hours:

\[ \$9.50 \times 8 = \$76.00 \]

The answer is reasonable because \(\$9.50\) is about \(\$10\), and \(\$10 \times 8 = \$80\), which is close to \(\$76\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\$76.00}\)

12. The coordinates of three vertices of a triangle are \((4, 0)\), \((5, 3)\), and \((0, 0)\). What is the area of the triangle?

Plot the points. The side from \((0,0)\) to \((4,0)\) lies on the \(x\)-axis, so use it as the base. Its length is \(4\) units.

The height is the vertical distance from \((5,3)\) to the \(x\)-axis, which is its \(y\)-coordinate, \(3\).

\[ A = \frac{1}{2}bh = \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3 = 6 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{6\text{ square units}}\)

13. ANALYZE The numbers \(1\) through \(52\) are written on individual cards and placed face down. If you draw one of the cards, what is the probability that it will have a number less than \(13\) on it? Explain how you arrived at your answer.

Count the favorable outcomes: the numbers less than \(13\) are \(1, 2, 3, \ldots, 12\), which is \(12\) numbers.

There are \(52\) cards in all, so the probability is \(\frac{12}{52}\). Reduce by dividing the numerator and denominator by \(4\):

\[ \frac{12}{52} = \frac{3}{13} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\frac{3}{13}}\)

14. What is the average of the first five prime numbers?

The first five prime numbers are \(2, 3, 5, 7,\) and \(11\). Add them:

\[ 2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 11 = 28 \]

Divide by \(5\):

\[ \frac{28}{5} = 5.6 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{5.6}\)

15. ANALYZE Show two ways to evaluate \(x(y + z)\) for \(x = 0.3\), \(y = 0.4\), and \(z = 0.5\).

Way 1 — add first, then multiply:

\[ x(y+z) = 0.3(0.4 + 0.5) = 0.3(0.9) = 0.27 \]

Way 2 — distribute, then add:

\[ xy + xz = (0.3)(0.4) + (0.3)(0.5) = 0.12 + 0.15 = 0.27 \]

Both ways give the same result.

Answer: \(\boxed{0.27}\)

16. ANALYZE In this figure all angles are right angles. Dimensions are in inches.

a. What is the perimeter of the figure?

b. What is the area of the figure?

First find the width of the top bar. The stem is \(3\) inches wide, with \(4\) inches of the bar on each side:

\[ 4 + 3 + 4 = 11 \text{ inches} \]

a. Add all eight sides of the outline:

\[ P = 11 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 3 + 5 + 4 + 3 = 38 \text{ inches} \]

b. Split the figure into the top bar and the stem:

\[ A = (11 \times 3) + (3 \times 5) = 33 + 15 = 48 \text{ square inches} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 38\text{ inches} \qquad \text{b. } 48\text{ square inches}}\)

17. The circle with center at point \(O\) has been divided into three sectors as shown. Find the measure of each of these central angles.

a. \(\angle AOB\)   b. \(\angle BOC\)   c. \(\angle AOC\)

A full circle measures \(360^\circ\). Multiply each fraction of the circle by \(360^\circ\).

a. \(\angle AOB\) is formed by the diameter \(BA\), a straight line, and it matches the \(\frac{1}{2}\) sector:

\[ \frac{1}{2} \times 360^\circ = 180^\circ \]

b. \(\angle BOC\) matches the \(\frac{1}{3}\) sector:

\[ \frac{1}{3} \times 360^\circ = 120^\circ \]

c. \(\angle AOC\) matches the \(\frac{1}{6}\) sector:

\[ \frac{1}{6} \times 360^\circ = 60^\circ \]

Check: \(180^\circ + 120^\circ + 60^\circ = 360^\circ\). ✓

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 180^\circ \qquad \text{b. } 120^\circ \qquad \text{c. } 60^\circ}\)

Cross-multiply, then divide:

20. \(4.7 - w = 1.2\)

To find the missing subtrahend, subtract \(1.2\) from \(4.7\):

\[ w = 4.7 - 1.2 = 3.5 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{w = 3.5}\)

21. \(10x = 10^2\)

First evaluate the power: \(10^2 = 100\). Then divide both sides by \(10\):

\[ 10x = 100 \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad x = \frac{100}{10} = 10 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{x = 10}\)

Estimate each answer to the nearest whole number. Then perform the calculation.

22. \(1\frac{11}{18} + 2\frac{11}{24}\)

Estimate: \(1\frac{11}{18} \approx 2\) and \(2\frac{11}{24} \approx 2\), so the sum is about \(2 + 2 = 4\).

Exact: Use the common denominator \(72\):

\[ 1\frac{11}{18} + 2\frac{11}{24} = 1\frac{44}{72} + 2\frac{33}{72} = 3\frac{77}{72} = 4\frac{5}{72} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{Estimate: } 4 \qquad \text{Exact: } 4\frac{5}{72}}\)

23. \(5\frac{5}{6} - \left(3 - 1\frac{1}{3}\right)\)

Estimate: \(5\frac{5}{6} \approx 6\) and \(3 - 1\frac{1}{3} \approx 2\), so the difference is about \(6 - 2 = 4\).

Exact: Work inside the parentheses first:

\[ 3 - 1\frac{1}{3} = 1\frac{2}{3} \] \[ 5\frac{5}{6} - 1\frac{2}{3} = 5\frac{5}{6} - 1\frac{4}{6} = 4\frac{1}{6} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{Estimate: } 4 \qquad \text{Exact: } 4\frac{1}{6}}\)

Simplify:

24. \(\dfrac{2}{3} \times 4 \times 1\frac{1}{8}\)

Write \(1\frac{1}{8}\) as \(\frac{9}{8}\), then multiply and reduce:

\[ \frac{2}{3} \times 4 \times \frac{9}{8} = \frac{2 \times 4 \times 9}{3 \times 8} = \frac{72}{24} = 3 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{3}\)

25. \(6\frac{2}{3} \div 4\)

Write \(6\frac{2}{3}\) as \(\frac{20}{3}\), then multiply by the reciprocal of \(4\):

\[ \frac{20}{3} \div 4 = \frac{20}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{20}{12} = \frac{5}{3} = 1\frac{2}{3} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{1\frac{2}{3}}\)

26. \(3.45 + 6 + (5.2 - 0.57)\)

Work inside the parentheses first:

\[ 5.2 - 0.57 = 4.63 \]

Then add:

\[ 3.45 + 6 + 4.63 = 14.08 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{14.08}\)

27. \(2.4 \div 0.016\)

Shift the decimal point three places in both numbers to make the divisor a whole number:

\[ 2.4 \div 0.016 = 2400 \div 16 = 150 \]

Answer: \(\boxed{150}\)

28. ESTIMATE Describe how to estimate the area of this book cover.

Round each side of the cover to the nearest whole inch:

\[ 4\frac{3}{8} \text{ in.} \approx 4 \text{ in.} \qquad 6\frac{13}{16} \text{ in.} \approx 7 \text{ in.} \]

Then multiply the rounded length and width to estimate the area:

\[ 4 \times 7 = 28 \text{ square inches} \]

(The exact area is about \(29.8\) square inches, so the estimate is close.)

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{about } 28\text{ square inches}}\)

29. Name the angle or side in \(\triangle ABC\) that corresponds to the following angle and side from \(\triangle CDA\):

a. \(\angle ACD\)   b. \(\overline{DC}\)

The congruence statement \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA\) gives the correspondence \(A \leftrightarrow C\), \(B \leftrightarrow D\), and \(C \leftrightarrow A\).

a. \(\angle ACD\) has its vertex at \(C\) and sides toward \(A\) and \(D\). Matching each letter (\(A \to C\), \(C \to A\), \(D \to B\)) gives \(\angle CAB\).

b. Side \(\overline{DC}\) joins \(D\) and \(C\). Matching the letters (\(D \to B\), \(C \to A\)) gives \(\overline{BA}\), that is, \(\overline{AB}\).

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } \angle CAB \qquad \text{b. } \overline{AB}}\)

30. The measure of \(\angle ACB\) is \(45^\circ\), and the measure of \(\angle ADC\) is \(60^\circ\). Find the measure of

a. \(\angle B\)   b. \(\angle CAB\)   c. \(\angle CAD\)

Corresponding angles of congruent triangles have equal measures.

a. \(\angle B\) corresponds to \(\angle ADC\), so \(\angle B = 60^\circ\).

c (first, because it helps with b). \(\angle CAD\) corresponds to \(\angle ACB\), so \(\angle CAD = 45^\circ\).

b. In \(\triangle CDA\), the three angles add to \(180^\circ\):

\[ \angle ACD = 180^\circ - 45^\circ - 60^\circ = 75^\circ \]

\(\angle CAB\) corresponds to \(\angle ACD\), so \(\angle CAB = 75^\circ\).

Check: \(45^\circ + 60^\circ + 75^\circ = 180^\circ\). ✓

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{a. } 60^\circ \qquad \text{b. } 75^\circ \qquad \text{c. } 45^\circ}\)

Real-World Connection

Johanna, Dawn, Simone and Kelly run on a relay team. In a relay race, each runner takes a turn by running a leg that is an evenly divided portion of the total distance. Johanna, Dawn, Simone, and Kelly completed a \(5\)-mile relay in \(39\) minutes.

a. If each member of the team ran at approximately the same speed, how long did it take each member to complete her leg? Write the answer as a mixed number and as a decimal of minutes and as minutes and seconds.

There are \(4\) runners, so divide the total time by \(4\):

\[ 39 \div 4 = 9\frac{3}{4} \text{ minutes} = 9.75 \text{ minutes} \]

Change the fractional part to seconds: \(\frac{3}{4}\) of a minute \(= \frac{3}{4} \times 60 = 45\) seconds. So each leg took \(9\) minutes \(45\) seconds.

Answer: \(\boxed{9\frac{3}{4}\text{ min} = 9.75\text{ min} = 9\text{ min } 45\text{ sec}}\)

b. How far did each team member run? Write the answer as a mixed number and as a decimal.

Divide the total distance equally among the \(4\) runners:

\[ 5 \div 4 = 1\frac{1}{4} \text{ miles} = 1.25 \text{ miles} \]

Answer: \(\boxed{1\frac{1}{4}\text{ miles} = 1.25\text{ miles}}\)
